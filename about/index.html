About

A research site for following Blaschka objects through records.

The project treats glass models as objects with documentary lives: made, ordered, priced, packed, shipped, accessioned, damaged, conserved, rediscovered and sometimes dispersed again.

Project
2026

What this project does

The Blaschka Object Network reconstructs object histories by joining workshop evidence to later institutional and market records. The unit of analysis can be a single model, a numbered order, a shipping case, a museum group, or a documented chain of custody.

The public site is selective. It presents cases where the evidence is strong enough to make the chain useful while keeping unresolved joins visible.

Evidence policy

Three things remain separate: source-supported connections, comparisons that help route research, and unresolved gaps. Shared catalogue numbers, nearby register numbers, similar model types or institutional succession do not by themselves prove that two records describe the same physical object.

Where a claim depends on OCR, an incomplete register, a later recollection or a secondary account, that evidential condition should remain visible rather than being silently normalised into certainty.

People and roles

The project also tracks people as actors inside object chains: makers, workshop participants, patrons, commissioners, dealers, carriers, curators, conservators and rediscovery actors. These are documentary roles rather than honorific labels. When the evidence supports only a qualified role such as “likely recipient” or “curator during the acquisition,” the qualification remains visible.

Researcher

The project is researched, written and maintained by Haohao Zhang. It is an independent research prototype rather than an official site of a museum, archive, university or the Blaschka family.

Corrections and source leads

Accession numbers, labels, archival references, movement records, conservation evidence and documented corrections are welcome. Where possible, include the repository, collection identifier, catalogue number or stable source link that supports the correction.

The site does not currently operate a public comment system. Correspondence is private and goes directly through project email; a moderated web form may be added later if it can be kept low-data and spam-resistant.
