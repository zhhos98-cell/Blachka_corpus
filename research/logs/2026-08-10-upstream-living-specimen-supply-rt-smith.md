# R. T. Smith and upstream living-specimen supply — 10 August 2026

## Discovery

A cross-project exact-name search produces a strong bridge between the Blaschka workshop archive and the nineteenth-century British microscopy corpus.

The Blaschka/Rakow OCR contains repeated correspondence with **R. T. Smith, Weymouth**, whom Leopold Blaschka recalls as an aquarium dealer. One draft requests a consignment of British sea anemones worth about 15 Reichsmarks, gives preferred species/varieties and asks Smith to propose a forwarding method. Another draft headed `Mr. Smith Waymouth 17. Mai` reports that the anemones arrived alive and recovered immediately in a workshop tank despite very warm weather and several days in transit from Weymouth to Dresden. A further draft asks for another Devon supplier because Caryophyllia Smithii could not be obtained in Weymouth and recalls that Smith had forwarded small quantities of marine animals.

J. P. Short's published archival citations independently locate Leopold-to-Smith drafts dated 25 December 1882, 1 March 1886? and late March 1886? in LRBC MS 0013 Box 23. The late-March sequence reports weakness after transit and four specimens that failed to revive, possibly because of night frost on the railway. Rudolf Blaschka's later account to Goodale describes the household seawater tanks as stocked partly from the Imperial Austrian Zoological Station at Trieste and partly from England and northern coasts.

## Microscopy corpus crosswalk

The user's Hardwicke's Science-Gossip corpus contains **five exact R. T. Smith records across 1873–1876**:

- 1873, D118 / R43088: `R. T. Smith, Weymouth` offers larvae in exchange for good microscopic slides.
- 1874, D125 / R44448: `R. T. Smith, 25, St. Alban's-street, Weymouth` offers well-mounted microscopic slides of marine algae, forty varieties, plus small unmounted starfish for good slides.
- 1874, D125 / R44496: same name/address offers microscopic slides of marine algae and starfish plus small unmounted starfish.
- 1874, D125 / R44688: same name/address offers named, well-mounted marine-algae slides for mounted sections of the human body.
- 1876, D120 / R43515: same name/address offers Bulla hydatis and other material for palate mounting, plus palates and jaws of Gyrodus, in exchange for good slides.

The repeated address and marine-material specialization make this much stronger than a name-only match. It shows Smith already embedded in exchange circuits connecting marine collecting and microscopy before the surviving Blaschka orders.

## Collector-list ambiguity

William R. Hughes, *On the Principles and Management of the Marine Aquarium* (paper read 1874, published 1875), contains a list of marine-animal collectors said to be taken principally from W. A. Lloyd's handbook. Project OCR D105 gives `E. J. Smith, 25, St. Alban's Row, Weymouth`. This is potentially the same address but the initials differ. It must remain unresolved until the printed page and preferably Lloyd's source are image-checked. Possible explanations include OCR error, another Smith at the same address, or a distinct collector.

## Research implication

This changes the circulation model. Blaschka model-making did not begin with glass and end with shipment. A prior logistics chain brought fragile living organisms into Dresden: capture → selection → price/exchange → packing → weather/season → transit → survival/revival in aquarium → observation → glass production. The completed glass model then entered a second outward chain.

This **reverse circulation** is especially useful because Smith connects the Blaschka project to the microscopy project at the level of one actor, one address and one class of marine material. It also makes weather and transport failure epistemically relevant before model production: if a living organism arrived weak, changed after transit or died, the workshop's observational opportunity changed with it.
