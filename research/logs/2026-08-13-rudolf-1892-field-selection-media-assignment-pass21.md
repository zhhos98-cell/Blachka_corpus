# Rudolf 1892 field selection + media assignment pass 21 — 13 August 2026

## Purpose

Passes 19 and 20 established an asymmetric Tempe herbarium packet. Several field references survive at Harvard, while only Hoffmannseggia currently has a secure later glass descendant. The immediate question is whether that selectivity arose only after the material returned to Hosterwitz.

The Tempe letters show that selection had already begun in Arizona.

Rudolf did not describe an indiscriminate collecting operation. He repeatedly converted abundant vegetation into a smaller working set, and on 22 April he states the filtering rule unusually clearly: many desert plants were too small to interest the party, so they ignored most of them and chose the **showiest** and most **characteristic** forms, producing twenty-five species that were **useful for them**. At the same time he assigned different objects to different preservation routes: drawings, temporary freshness holders, live uprooted cacti intended for shipment, possible alcohol preservation, and deliberate noncollection.

The strongest formulation after this pass is:

> **Selective conversion began in the field. Before a plant could enter the workshop's later model pipeline, it first had to be selected as a useful reference and assigned a medium in which its relevant features could remain available.**

This gives the project two separate selection gates:

1. **field selection and media assignment**;
2. **later workshop reactivation and model production**.

No public map changes are made.

---

## 1. Tempe was itself a yield decision

In the 18 April letter (`BLA-D00773 / 017:127`), Rudolf calls Tempe his first western destination and says that he and Ganong expect to remain there for several days because the vegetation offers a great deal of good study material.

The stop therefore already has a production logic. Time spent in place is partly determined by anticipated reference yield.

This matters because the itinerary should not be read only as a sequence of railway stops. Some places become long enough to matter because they support a particular density of study material. Mobility and working time are coupled.

---

## 2. Drawing turns abundance into a counted working set

On 20 April (`BLA-D00779 / 017:133`), Rudolf says that he did a great deal of drawing the previous day and through that work now has **ten interesting species**. He describes the desert vegetation as extraordinarily rich and unfamiliar, and mentions an excursion with Ganong by carriage. He also says that he has already drawn two additional smaller cactus species that day.

The wording is useful because the count follows from work rather than from simple botanical presence. The desert contains an enormous number of plants; drawing produces a smaller set that Rudolf can count as `interesting species`.

The ten should not be treated automatically as an earlier subtotal of the twenty-five species mentioned two days later. The categories differ: `interesting` in the 20 April drawing report, `useful`, `showy` and `characteristic` in the 22 April selection statement. The chronology is suggestive, but the source does not prove a single cumulative ledger.

---

## 3. The 22 April letter gives an explicit selection rule

The 22 April Tempe letter (`BLA-D00785 / 017:139`) is now one of the most important methodological documents in the 1892 corpus.

Rudolf describes a group excursion to mountains and desert a few miles outside Tempe. The party hires a wagon and leaves early in the morning. They carry holders specifically to keep plants fresh.

The key passage comes later. Rudolf says that a large proportion of the desert plants are very small, so **most were ignored**. They picked out the **most showy** and **most characteristic** plants, and the number of species useful to them came to **twenty-five**. He adds that this was more than he had expected to find.

This is direct evidence that `field collection` was already a filtering operation. The relevant contrast is not simply collected / unavailable. It is:

`abundant vegetation → evaluated vegetation → retained useful subset`.

The criteria are partly representational. A plant could be worth retaining because it was visually striking or characteristic of a vegetation type. The field packet was therefore shaped by what the Blaschka project considered worth carrying forward as a reference.

---

## 4. Selection and preservation medium were decided together

The same letter makes the reference medium contingent on the material object.

### Temporary freshness

The excursion party uses holders to keep plants fresh. This is a short-duration preservation technology between field encounter and later study or drawing.

### Drawing

Rudolf draws flowers of about four cactus species. He also finds a striking Delphinium in a small patch of desert and draws it after returning in the afternoon.

Drawing allows visible form to remain available without requiring the whole plant to remain alive or continuously movable.

### Live transport

For the cactus species, Rudolf digs up young examples and takes them with him. He explicitly says that these cacti do not need to be preserved in alcohol; he intends instead to send them from California to his parents in Hosterwitz.

That sentence is unusually valuable. It reveals a choice among preservation regimes rather than a generic act of collecting:

`live plant` versus `alcohol-preserved plant`.

The decision is tied to the organism's capacity to survive transport.

### Deliberate rejection

The `Jumping Cactus` is not collected because its hooked spines make handling dangerous. Elsewhere in the same passage, most very small desert plants are simply ignored.

So absence from the return packet can itself be an active historical outcome.

## 5. Herbarium sheets add another route, but should not be forced into the letter chronology

Pass 19 identified a minimum digital Tempe packet in Harvard University Herbaria, including sheets dated 18, 20, 21 and 22 April 1892. That material establishes another reference form: the pressed herbarium specimen.

But the letter chronology prevents a simplistic mapping.

On Friday 22 April Rudolf refers to excursions `today and Tuesday`, which identifies at least two group excursions on **22 April and 19 April**. The currently indexed HUH sheets, however, include dates of 18, 20, 21 and 22 April.

Therefore:

`specimen date ≠ automatically the date of one of Rudolf's named group excursions`.

Several explanations remain possible. Ganong may have collected independently; Rudolf may have collected outside the two group excursions; a sheet date may register a different moment in collection/preparation/label practice; there may be additional excursions that the letter does not foreground. At present the evidence does not choose among these.

This creates a useful source-critical distinction between several clocks:

- **mobility clock** — when the party travelled or made excursions;
- **drawing/work clock** — when a plant was studied or rendered;
- **specimen-label clock** — the date preserved on herbarium metadata;
- **dispatch clock** — when a retained reference was actually sent onward.

They should be joined only where a source explicitly joins them.

## 6. A two-stage selective-conversion model

The Tempe evidence now supports a more exact production chain:

`encounter → evaluate → select/reject → assign medium → stabilize/dispatch → later workshop reactivation → model or no model`.

The first gate is in the field. Rudolf and his companions decide which plants are useful enough to retain and which material form will preserve the relevant information.

The second gate occurs later. Passes 19 and 20 show that only some surviving field references can currently be linked to later models. Hoffmannseggia becomes model 433; several other Tempe herbarium sheets remain without a secure model descendant after synonym expansion.

This means that `selective conversion` should no longer be used only for workshop choice. There are two selections separated by transport and time.

A plant could fail to enter the model pipeline because it was never retained in the field. Another could be retained as a specimen or drawing and never reactivated. Another could move through several media before later becoming glass.

---

## 7. Transport implication: portability was produced before packing

The earlier transport argument focused strongly on cases, supports, forwarding, customs and receiving. Tempe pushes the same mechanism upstream.

A plant did not become transportable only when somebody packed it. It became transportable when a decision was made about **what part of the encounter had to survive** and in which material form.

For different references, that could mean:

- preserve freshness for hours;
- draw floral form;
- press a specimen;
- uproot and ship a living plant;
- preserve in alcohol;
- leave the plant behind.

Transport therefore begins with **media assignment**. Packing is one later phase in a longer history of making a reference movable.

This gives the current paper a more coherent symmetry. On the field side, plants are decomposed into reference forms. On the finished-model side, glass objects are rebuilt into transport assemblages. Both processes change what counts as the movable object.

---

## 8. Relation to existing literature and the 2007 Corning account

Rossi-Wilcox and Whitehouse already note that Rudolf and Ganong took excursions around Tempe and that Rudolf reported twenty-five studies there. That retrospective account is useful confirmation of the episode, but the direct letters let us recover distinctions smoothed out by narrative summary: `interesting species`, `useful species`, freshness holders, live cactus shipment, alcohol as an alternative, selective rejection, and the temporal separation of drawing, specimen dates and dispatch.

The contribution is therefore not the discovery that Rudolf sketched plants in Arizona. It is the reconstruction of **how a field encounter was converted into a transportable reference packet**.

---

## 9. Current strongest formulation

After pass 21, the field side of the article can be stated more tightly:

> **Rudolf's journey did not simply collect plants for later copying. It converted botanical encounters into differentiated reference forms. Selection was explicit: most small plants were ignored, showy and characteristic forms were retained, and material properties helped determine whether a reference was drawn, kept fresh, uprooted alive, preserved, pressed or left behind. Only after that first conversion could the split workshop decide which returned references to reactivate as glass.**

This makes the later asymmetry of the Tempe herbarium packet historically intelligible. The archive should contain more references than models because the reference corpus served more functions and passed through an earlier selection logic of its own.

## 10. Map decision

No extra Tempe route geometry.

The eventual evidence/detail layer should instead represent a **media switch** at Tempe:

`encounter → select / reject → drawing | fresh holder | herbarium | live shipment | other preservation → dispatch / retention`.

This is more faithful than drawing separate lines for every plant or specimen.
