# Bibliography expansion: final multilingual / global sweep, pass 36

Date: 2026-08-09

## Purpose

This is the closing discovery pass for the public Blaschka bibliography. The search was deliberately widened beyond the established British, Irish, German, Austrian, French and North American core. Priority went to (1) publications in languages underrepresented in the existing bibliography and (2) publications that make Blaschka objects legible within collections, teaching cultures, conservation projects or public-science contexts outside the usual Euro-American nodes.

The existing scope guard remains unchanged: a publication enters the public bibliography only when Leopold or Rudolf Blaschka, their workshop, their glass models, a Blaschka collection, conservation/material study, scientific use, distribution or substantial reception is a meaningful part of the publication. Generic glass history, generic museum-model history and pages that merely mention the Blaschkas remain excluded.

## Repository deduplication before promotion

The repository already contains substantial non-English coverage. Exact-title checks confirmed that the following should **not** be re-added:

- 佐々木 類 [Rui Sasaki], “ブラシュカ父子のガラスの花 : ハーバード自然史博物館ウェア・コレクション” (2017), already in pass 8.
- 西 弘嗣 [Hiroshi Nishi], “ガラス模型と博物館展示” (2014), already in pass 8.
- Gianna Innocenti and other Italian collection literature already in pass 8 and later passes.
- María Isabel Herráez Martín, *Los modelos Blaschka*, already represented in the existing bibliography.
- Matthew D. Shaw et al., “Ideas made glass: Blaschka glass models at Canterbury Museum” (2017), already represented in pass 16.
- French Strasbourg and Central-European German/Czech material added in passes 26–34.

Pass 35 itself had already been created with B. J. Gill, H. R. Grenfell and W. M. Blom’s 2019 *Records of the Auckland Museum* article. It is retained unchanged and now loads pass 36.

## Records promoted in pass 36

### Chinese / East Asian reception

- 郭英剑 [Guo Yingjian], “走进哈佛自然历史博物馆,” *中国科学报* [*China Science Daily*], 4 July 2013, p. 8. ScienceNet preserves the newspaper text. The article contains a substantial section on the Ware Collection, its pedagogical purpose, the Ware patronage and Blaschka glassworking. This is useful as Chinese-language scientific/museum reception rather than as a substitute for specialist Blaschka scholarship.

### Italian / digital documentation

- Fondazione Scienza e Tecnica, “I modelli Blaschka per la prima volta in 3D,” 15 March 2017. Direct institutional report on the E-RIHS-linked experimental 3D documentation campaign in Florence. It identifies the FST collection as 118 models and explains object-specific documentation of form, glass and morphology. This is materially useful for the project’s documentation / digital-object strand.

### Spanish / Mexico

- Teresa I. Fortoul van der Goes, “Cuando imitar se hace por amor,” *Revista de la Facultad de Medicina (México)* 62.3 (May–June 2019): 57–58, DOI 10.22201/fm.24484865e.2019.62.3.11. Direct Spanish-language Blaschka reception in a Mexican medical-education journal. Its value is reception and the use of the Glass Flowers as an analogy for teaching through increasingly lifelike simulation; it should not be treated as an authority for fine-grained Blaschka chronology.
- Antonio Lazcano Araujo, “El retablo de las maravillas,” *Revista de la Universidad de México*, dossier “Animales” (May 2020). High-value regional item. Lazcano embeds the Blaschkas within the history of Mexican natural-history display and explicitly links Alfonso L. Herrera and his successors to surviving models of *Pelagia noctiluca* and *Argonauta argo* from the former Museo Nacional de Historia Natural / El Chopo, now associated with the Museo de Historia Natural y Cultura Ambiental.
- Patricia López Suárez, “Difunde cortometraje colecciones del Instituto de Biología,” *Gaceta UNAM*, 13 April 2023. Useful present-day scientific-heritage evidence because it explicitly identifies the Instituto de Biología / Pabellón Nacional de la Biodiversidad “Animales de Cristal Blaschka” among the institution’s scientific collections.

### Arabic-language reception

- Natasha Daly, “روائع بحر زجاجية” [“Glass Sea Marvels”], photographs by Guido Mocafico, *National Geographic AlArabiya*, 1 June 2021. Direct Arabic-language illustrated feature on Blaschka marine models as research/teaching objects and their later museum afterlives. This is a regional-language edition of the Mocafico/Daly story and is retained as documented Arabic reception, not counted as an independent research finding about object provenance.

## Searches run but not promoted

### Portuguese / Brazil and Portugal

Portuguese searches recovered the September 2021 *National Geographic Portugal* version of the Natasha Daly / Guido Mocafico feature, “Um zoo (marinho) de vidro,” pp. 88–94. Its issue metadata and text are recoverable through web indexing, but the easily discoverable full-issue copies are third-party mirrors. Because the same underlying feature is now represented through an official Arabic National Geographic page, the Portuguese edition was held rather than adding a duplicate with a weaker public link. Brazil-specific searches did not recover a direct specialist Blaschka publication or a Brazilian collection case strong enough for promotion.

### Japan / Korea / Chinese-language web

Japanese searching through NDL-style queries recovered the Sasaki 2017 article, but repository deduplication showed it was already present. Korean searches did not recover a publication with sufficient directness and bibliographic stability. Chinese searching did recover the 2013 *China Science Daily* article promoted above; later travel and lifestyle pages were excluded.

### South Asia

English and local-route searches around India / Calcutta recovered the already-known historical statement that the Indian Museum in Calcutta appeared among institutions purchasing Blaschka invertebrate models. No current India-based publication was found that could securely establish a surviving collection, later institutional history or direct modern Blaschka study. The historical purchase lead remains a provenance/census research problem rather than a reason to pad the public bibliography.

### Africa

Searches combining Blaschka with South African, African museum and French-language African terms did not recover a reliable direct local publication or collection study. Results were overwhelmingly global/Harvard/European pages. No African entry was promoted merely to create continental symmetry.

### Arabic

Arabic searching produced both newspaper retellings and the official *National Geographic AlArabiya* feature. The latter was selected because it is a stable, direct, illustrated publication and makes the language/region expansion without relying on a derivative news rewrite.

### South America beyond Mexico

Spanish searches for Argentina, Chile and wider Latin America produced generic glass exhibitions and unrelated uses of “modelos de vidrio,” but no publication meeting the Blaschka-specific scope guard. Mexico remains the strongest Latin-American publication cluster recovered in this pass.

## Why the final pass stops here

The diminishing-return pattern is now clear. The English-language core, German/Central-European scholarship, French museum literature, Italian collection literature, Dutch material, Japanese items and Australasian collection scholarship are already substantially represented. The remaining search results increasingly consist of translations, derivative popular pieces, generic glass/model contexts or historical institution names without a modern publication trail.

Pass 36 therefore closes discovery rather than pretending that multilingual completeness is achievable. Future additions should be event-driven: a newly identified collection, a newly digitised local publication, a bibliographic lead emerging from an archive file, or a publication supplied by a curator. Broad undirected searching is unlikely to improve the bibliography enough to justify further passes.
