# Rudolf 1892 deferred-supply / phenology-status pass 31 — 13 August 2026

## Result

The current access-repair category has become too broad. `later supply`, `substitution` and `failed observation` describe different historical states. Three cases now let us separate them: **Crinum/Orcutt, Sequoia sempervirens, and Darlingtonia californica**.

The main result is simple:

> **Phenological failure did not necessarily end a reference chain. The network could repair temporary inaccessibility by moving the observation in time, delegating later supply, or changing the site at which the desired biological state became observable.**

But those repairs must be tracked by fulfillment status. A promise of later flowers is not equivalent to a bulb actually received in Cambridge.

No public map change.

---

## 1. Crinum: deferred supply actually reaches Cambridge

The June Cambridge letter `BLA-D00927 / 017:282` is much stronger than a generic plan for future collecting. While repairing the existing Harvard models, Rudolf reports that he has just received **a large Crinum bulb from Mr. Orcutt in San Diego**, sent after an earlier packet of seeds from the same man had already arrived.

Rudolf immediately explains why this matters. He plans to arrange for a larger quantity of seeds and bulbs from plants **whose flowering time they could not wait for while in California**. In this way, he says, they can gather much more material.

The chain is therefore directly documented to Cambridge:

`flowering unavailable within travel window → local supplier relation → seed packet sent/received → Crinum bulb sent/received later → proposal for standing deferred-supply arrangement`.

This is a genuine extension of the field season after bodily departure from California.

The open part begins only after Cambridge. We still need to know whether this material was forwarded to Hosterwitz, survived, flowered there, produced a drawing or entered a glass model genealogy.

So Crinum's current status is **fulfilled deferred supply to relay**, not `model source`.

---

## 2. Sequoia: a deferred supply plan remains only a plan

The San Francisco letter of 18 May, `BLA-D00856 / 017:210`, provides a different state. Rudolf and Ganong had reached the sequoia forests, but the flowering season of most central Californian conifers had nearly ended. Their hope of seeing *Sequoia sempervirens* in bloom failed.

Rudolf then says that through local botanical acquaintances they should be able to have flowers sent later. Mr. Lemmon, the conifer specialist, was absent, and the cones Rudolf himself found appeared empty.

This is an access-repair design:

`correct locality reached → target phenological state absent → local network expected to provide later flowering material`.

At present we have **no closed dispatch or receipt event** for those later Sequoia flowers. Therefore this must remain `planned deferred supply`.

This distinction prevents a common narrative inflation: the existence of a supply network made later receipt possible, but the source we currently have records expectation, not fulfillment.

---

## 3. Darlingtonia repairs access by changing site, not by waiting

Darlingtonia belongs beside Crinum and Sequoia because all three begin with inaccessibility, but its solution is structurally different.

On 18 May Rudolf treats the Shasta-area habitat as a four-day, $70 decision problem and Ganong proposes telegraphing Goodale. The subsequent Colorado follow-up records that Goodale's reply told them **not to go to Shasta**. Days later, at the St Louis botanical garden, the desired plant is flowering. Rudolf makes a colour sketch and places a specimen in alcohol.

The repair chain is:

`desired target → habitat route costly → remote consultation → route rejected → cultivated institution supplies flowering state → visual + preserved references captured`.

This is **institutional substitution**, not deferred delivery. Instead of moving the plant later to the traveller, the journey later reaches another institution where the desired biological state is already available.

The distinction matters because the transport intervention is different. Crinum changes **time and carrier**. Darlingtonia changes **site of observation**. Sequoia currently changes **anticipated time** but has no verified delivery yet.

## 4. A status ontology for deferred references

Future searches should stop collapsing all asynchronous references under `later supply`. The following states are independently meaningful:

`desired/needed → temporarily inaccessible → repair strategy selected → promised/requested → dispatched → received at relay → forwarded onward → survived/cultivated → entered target biological state → drawn/preserved → reactivated in model production`.

A chain can stop at any point.

Examples:

- **Sequoia** currently reaches `promised/expected`.
- **Crinum** reaches `received at relay`.
- **Darlingtonia** bypasses delivery and reaches `drawn/preserved` through site substitution.

The May 4 San Diego live cactus/bulb packet belongs to yet another branch because its material was deliberately sent alive toward Cambridge/Hosterwitz so that future biological states might remain available.

---

## 5. Transporting future observability

This gives a sharper interpretation of living reference material. A seed or bulb is not simply an inferior miniature of the adult plant. In this workflow it can be a device for moving **future observability**.

The relevant target may be absent at the moment of travel because:

- flowering has passed;
- flowering has not yet begun;
- the route cannot wait;
- direct habitat access is too expensive;
- a specialist collector is unavailable.

Living carriage or deferred supply moves the possibility of observing the plant later under another schedule. That is materially different from a drawing, which freezes an already completed observation, or an herbarium sheet, which stabilizes selected structure.

The media therefore solve different temporal problems.

| Access problem | Repair | Current closure |
|---|---|---|

| Sequoia flowering season already passed | local botanists expected to send flowers later | plan only; dispatch/receipt open |

This is better than a generalized claim that `networks overcame distance`. The archive shows which dimension of inaccessibility was repaired and how far the repair actually succeeded.
