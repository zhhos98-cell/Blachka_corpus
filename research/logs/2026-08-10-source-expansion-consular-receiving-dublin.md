# Source expansion: consular paperwork, receiving skill, and Dublin transport failure — 10 August 2026

## Consular infrastructure

The Harvard/Ware correspondence shows that consular documentation was a route-making technology rather than an incidental certificate.

- 1906: Rudolf records an insured Harvard shipment accompanied by a consular invoice valued at approximately three years of work.
- 1922: during postwar planning he explicitly notes that the American consulate had returned to Bremen, while export licensing and taxation remained unsettled.
- 1929: Ehrhorn, Emden & Mayer proposes a cheaper direct Bremen–Boston service. Rudolf rejects the route because changing an already prepared itinerary would require new consular paperwork and delay.
- 1932: the Rakow finding aid lists a surviving `Consular Certificate for Shipment to Harvard University` in Series 04 Business Files.

NARA Record Group 84 is now indexed as the U.S. government-side archive universe. It preserves Foreign Service post records and can include merchandise certifications, shipping/cargo records, fees and local business correspondence. Exact Bremen post series remain to be identified through the RG 84 finding aids; no Blaschka certificate is claimed on the U.S. side yet.

## Receiving skill at Harvard

Harvard HOLLIS materially strengthens the receiving-side archive.

The collection-level finding aid for `ecb00006` states that it contains a 1931 diary by Louis Bierweiler documenting his trip to Hosterwitz as curator of the museum to visit Rudolf Blaschka. HOLLIS also identifies:

- `Louis C. Bierweiler, 1935–1964`, Box Biographical 1, Folder 26, 2 folders.
- a color transparency of Bierweiler at work with the glass models, between 1949 and 1957.
- the 1954 stereoscopic-slide programme in which G. Blake Johnson, assisted by Bierweiler, photographed models for 386 species in their exhibition cases, producing 638 unique glass stereoscopic slides.

These records matter because Rudolf's 1932 correspondence independently praises Bierweiler's skill and carefulness in unpacking a difficult shipment. Bierweiler therefore connects workshop visit, customs/clearance and unpacking, repair/handling, and later collection documentation. Receiving-side knowledge was embodied in trained museum staff and could circulate back to the workshop.

The companion `Harvard Botanical Museum records, ecb00011`, is retained as broader institutional context for museum administration, research, teaching and exhibition; it is not substituted for direct shipment correspondence.

## Dublin failure chain

A re-read of the workshop business OCR produced a stronger 1887 Irish logistics cluster.

A Damon letter reports a very large case for Alfred Cort Haddon at the Royal College of Science, Dublin, insured for 500 Marks with a 495-Mark invoice. The case-prefix is OCR-read `L.D. 262` and the Hamburg forwarding name is OCR-read `M.Miller`; both fields require manuscript-image verification before normalization. The transaction carried approximately 200 Marks of Damon credit after the invoice.

The same letter reports a preceding shipment to W. J. Sollas at Trinity College Dublin. According to the workshop, a mistake in the shipper's note caused the consignment to be landed at Bristol. It arrived about two weeks late and models nos. 239 and 356 were broken, probably as a result of the transport sequence. Fresh examples were placed in Haddon's subsequent consignment.

This is an unusually complete failure-and-recovery mechanism:

`shipper's note → wrong port → delay → breakage → replacement → consolidation into another customer's shipment`.

It also expands the Möller problem. Exact `M. Otto W. Moller` / `M. O. W. Moller` is secure in the Marlborough and Liverpool letters later in 1887. The earlier Haddon record's `M.Miller` may be the same forwarder, but this remains an image-level question and has not been silently upgraded.

Receiving-side archive targets are now explicit:

- UCD Archives, `IE UCDA RCSI`, Records of the Royal College of Science for Ireland, 1867–1929, containing accounts, correspondence, order books and equipment/supply records.
- Trinity College Dublin Manuscripts & Archives, College and departmental records, for the Sollas order/damage/replacement side; an exact Sollas series has not yet been isolated.
- University of St Andrews `ms13973–MS13981`, Haddon letters to D'Arcy Thompson, 1885–1900, as same-period museum/specimen-exchange context only, not Blaschka evidence.

## Result

The source architecture now captures three additional mechanisms that would disappear in a museum-centred provenance narrative:

1. paperwork can constrain route choice before an object moves;
2. skilled receiving labour can be a condition of successful transport;
3. a failed shipment can be repaired through the logistics of a later, different institutional order.

The next retrieval phase should therefore code documents by action rather than repository alone: certification, forwarding instruction, shipper's note, misrouting, insurance, unpacking, breakage, replacement, remounting and feedback.
