# Bibliography expansion: Central European / workshop-primary route, passes 26–28

Date: 2026-08-09

## Scope guard

The public bibliography remains strictly Blaschka-focused. Central-European glass-history journals, Czech and German library catalogues, KVK-style union discovery, MDZ/BSB, SLUB, museum publication lists and family-history resources are discovery infrastructure only. General Bohemian glass history, generic lampworking, model-making history and unrelated Blaschka-family material are excluded unless the publication itself substantially treats Leopold or Rudolf Blaschka, their workshop, their catalogues, their models, or a directly relevant collection / conservation / material-analysis case.

## Pass 26

Added five direct records after repository deduplication and external bibliographic checking:

- Vladimír Dufek, *Genealogie hodkovického sklářského rodu Blaschků* (Praha, 2011), 19 pp. Direct genealogy of the glassmaking Blaschka family.
- Vladimír Dufek, *Skleněný svět Blaschků*, 2nd expanded ed. (Praha, 2012), 60 pp., ISBN 978-80-260-2882-6.
- Vladimír Dufek, *Blaschkas Glaswelt*, 1st ed. (Prag, 2012), 52 pp., ISBN 978-80-254-9558-2. Harvard-derived MARC metadata records bibliographical references on pp. 50–52.
- Zuzana Cílová and Vladimír Dufek, “Průzkum chemického složení zbytků skla z bývalé dílny Leopolda Blaschky a jeho syna Rudolfa,” *Keramický zpravodaj* 28.2 (2012): 10–14. This is direct chemical analysis of glass remnants recovered from the former Blaschka workshop and is therefore inside scope despite being technical literature.
- Sabine Hackethal, “Maritime Creatures Made of Glass,” in *Research Travel Exploration: The Lifeworlds of the Leibniz Association Archives* (2014), 38–39. Direct Museum für Naturkunde / archive-oriented Blaschka publication.

## Pass 27

Added four further records, concentrating on workshop-authored / commercial primary publications and direct later scholarship:

- Rudolf Blaschka, “Die Nacktschnecken des Meeres,” *Sitzungs-Berichte der naturwissenschaftlichen Gesellschaft Isis zu Dresden*, Jahrgang 1880 (published Dresden 1881), 23–26. The title and pagination are independently recoverable through modern nudibranch bibliography and later Blaschka scholarship; the digitised Isis volume is available via BHL and BSB/MDZ.
- Leopold Blaschka, *Katalog über Blaschka’s Modelle von wirbellosen Thieren dargestellt von Leopold Blaschka in Hosterwitz bei Dresden*. Druck Gustav Winter, Stolpen, 1885. Tübingen reproduces the catalogue and gives copy-level provenance: National Museums and Galleries of Wales; scan supplied by Sternwarte Kremsmünster.
- Chris Meechan, “A Glass Menagerie: The Works of Leopold and Rudolph Blaschka,” *The Conchologists’ Newsletter* 138 (1996): 690–697. This is distinct from Meechan’s already-listed 1995 *Glass Cone* article; the Conchological Society confirms the newsletter run and external malacological bibliography supplies the issue/pages.
- J. P. Short, “Arc of the Anemone: Modeling Nature from the Wunderkammer to the Warenwelt,” in *Before Photography: German Visual Culture in the Nineteenth Century* (De Gruyter, 2021), 181–204. Direct chapter centred substantially on Blaschka marine models and nineteenth-century commodity/model culture; DOI 10.1515/9783110696448-010.

## Pass 28: Vienna / Central European collection scholarship

Added five direct records after exact-title repository checks:

- Claudia Feigl, “Glasmodell einer Blumenkohlqualle,” University of Vienna, *Objekt des Monats* (January 2016). Direct treatment of Blaschka scientific-model production, teaching use and the Vienna collection.
- Claudia Feigl, “Glasmodell eines Röhrenpolypen,” University of Vienna, *Objekt des Monats* (January 2017). Especially useful because it reconstructs the Vienna/Trieste/Blaschka exchange relationship, including live-animal shipments and the 1884–1887 order sequence.
- Maximilian Petrasko, “Glasmodell einer Seenelke,” University of Vienna, *Objekt des Monats* (August 2018). Direct object study incorporating Ward-catalogue identification, material-analysis literature, micro-CT work and the model's changing teaching/research status.
- Ines Kaffka, “Glasbläserkunst für die Wissenschaft: Lupenreine Täuschungen,” *Der Spiegel* (18 July 2019). Direct Blaschka feature centred on Guido Mocafico's photographic documentation of models in European collections.
- Claudia Feigl, “Die Glasmodelle mariner Wirbelloser von Leopold und Rudolf Blaschka als Ergebnis eines Netzwerks europäischer Wissenschaftler des ausgehenden 19. Jahrhunderts,” in Johannes Seidl and Ingrid Kästner, eds., *Tauschen und Schenken: Wissenschaftliche Sammlungen als Resultat europäischer Zusammenarbeit* (Düren: Shaker, 2020), 57–72. This is a substantial direct Blaschka chapter; the host volume's imprint gives ISBN 978-3-8440-6490-2 and ISSN 1868-6125.

## Tübingen sourcebook audit

The 2006 Stadtmuseum Tübingen catalogue remains the strongest recovered German sourcebook. Its printed `Quellen- und Literaturverzeichnis` (pp. 145–148) was checked against the repository. Most direct Blaschka items there are now already represented: the early Leopold catalogues, Rudolf’s *Hydroidquallen*, Hofmeister 1982, Hollowday 1989, Whitehouse 1990, McNally/Buschini 1993, Scholtz 1997, Reiling 1998/2000/2002/2003, Pantano/Rossi-Wilcox/Lange 1998, Warmus 2001, Meechan/Reiling 2002, Rossi-Wilcox/Reiling/Bisaga 2003, Reiling/Spunarová 2005, and the 2006 catalogue/chapter set. General zoology, model-making, Haeckel and museum-context works remain excluded.

The Tübingen bibliography also identifies *Neueste Nachrichten des Glasmuseums Weißwasser*, no. 6, 4 August 2004, as a source used specifically in the Blaschka biographical discussion. The original article title/author have not yet been recovered, so the issue remains a hold rather than a public bibliography record.

## Czech / Bohemian discovery cluster — hold pending article-level verification

GLASS.CZ’s historical index for *Sklář a keramik* confirms a potentially important 2007 cluster:

- M. Gelnar and V. Dufek, “Významné hodkovické sklářské rodiny 19. století,” no. 3/2007.
- P. Nový, “Firma Josef Blaschka&Söhne z Hodkovic nad Mohelkou, její předchůdci a dědicové,” no. 4–5/2007.
- R. Hais, “Firma J. Blaschka & Söhne z Hodkovic n. M., její předchůdci a dědicové II, Receptář na skleněnou kompozici,” no. 6/2007.

These are not promoted yet. Exact pagination has not been recovered, and the present search evidence does not establish how substantially each article treats Leopold/Rudolf themselves rather than the wider Hodkovice family/firm lineage. They remain discovery candidates under the strict Blaschka scope guard.

A Czech library-index result also confirms Vladimír Dufek’s 2008 title *Život a dílo českých uměleckých sklářů Leopolda Blaschky a jeho syna Rudolfa*. A Czech-German Future Fund record independently describes a Czech-German monograph project on the Blaschka glassmaking family with Dufek as applicant and Blaschka-Haus e.V. as partner. Full publication metadata (imprint/pagination/ISBN if any) remains unresolved, so this title is also held for collation rather than promoted with guessed details.

## Early Reichenbach notices — hold

The project source index contains two early discovery records:

- Ludwig Reichenbach, “Marine-Aquarien mit Actinien oder Strahlblumenpolypen in naturgetreuen Modellen,” *Leopoldina*, 4. Heft (1863).
- Ludwig Reichenbach, “Glasmodelle lebender Schnecken,” *Allgemeine naturhistorische Zeitung* (1864): 231.

Independent Reichenbach bibliography confirms these titles, and later scholarship securely establishes Reichenbach’s 1863 presentation/promotion of Leopold Blaschka’s early marine models. However, the exact original issue text has not yet been inspected to prove that these two bibliographic notices themselves are explicitly about Blaschka. They therefore remain outside the public bibliography for now. No artificial extension of the public chronology from 1870 back to 1863 is made without original-text confirmation.

## Other holds / dedupe results

The 1996 Glass Art Society Boston conference literature contains a paper titled “The Blaschka Glass Flowers at Harvard,” pp. 40–44, in the 26th conference / *Critical Mass* proceedings. Later scholarly citation confirms title, venue and pages, but the author has not yet been securely recovered. Do not assign an author by inference.

A fresh citation-chain audit against María Isabel Herráez Martín's 2014 *Los modelos Blaschka* recovered no major unrepresented core literature: Fulton/Rossi-Wilcox 2008, Brierley 2009, Pirotte 2009, Pinto's Harvard thesis, Daston 2004, the 2008 *Historical Biology* papers, Whitehouse 2007 and the standard conservation literature are already represented in the repository. This confirms that the remaining yield is increasingly in local-language, grey, museum and regional publication layers rather than the obvious English-language core.

Two direct institutional web publications are still held only because their publication dates are not exposed reliably: Natural History Museum London's “Colour in the collections: Blaschka glass models” (Katie Pavid; demonstrably online by January 2021), and National Museums Scotland's “The glass artists who mastered museum models.” Both are substantial enough for scope if a reliable publication date is recovered.
