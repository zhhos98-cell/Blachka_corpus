# Jamaica 106/116 count discrepancy and counting ontology — pass 47 — 13 August 2026

## Purpose

The source register has long carried a discrepancy between Rudolf's Jamaica letters and the Jamaican botanical administration's annual report. The direct 27 March 1892 letter is now closed in the local OCR master, while the official report's publication context can be fixed more precisely from the surviving digitized volume. This pass keeps the discrepancy open instead of smoothing it into a single expedition yield.

## Result

Two high-quality primary sources disagree by exactly ten species:

### Rudolf's own terminal Jamaica count

`BLA-D00721 / 017:75` (German OCR) and its English translation `BLA-D00725 / 017:79`, Hope Garden, **27 March 1892**. Rudolf writes immediately before leaving Jamaica that he has **finished his work with 106 species**. The letter then describes his successful Spanishtown/Rio Cobre excursion, a final visit to Gordon Town that morning, and departure early the next morning via Castleton/Annotto Bay to the Boston steamship.

The wording is terminal, not a forecast: `I have finished my work with 106 species`.

### Jamaican institutional report

Project source `JAM-001`, `Foreign Students in the Gardens`, p. 18 of the official *Report of the Director of Public Gardens and Plantations, Jamaica, for the Year ended 31st March, 1892*, states that Harvard sent two representatives and credits Blaschka with accurate coloured drawings of **116 species**, while Robert Cameron made dried collections of the same and other plants for their herbarium. The report also records Goodale's later appreciation for the facilities and success of the work.

The digitized container is now independently fixed: the report was printed at the Jamaica Government Printing Office in **1893** and survives inside the *Bulletin of the Botanical Department, Jamaica*, Internet Archive identifier `bulletinofbotani3950fawc`, volume metadata `no.39-50 1893`. Its contents explicitly list section 13, `Foreign Students in the Gardens`.

Therefore:

`Rudolf 27 Mar 1892 = 106 species`

versus

`Jamaican official retrospective report, printed 1893 = 116 species drawings`.

The discrepancy is real at the current evidence level.

---

## 1. A simple “ten more species after the letter” explanation is currently weak

The chronology makes a casual temporal reconciliation difficult. Rudolf's 27 March letter says the Jamaica work is finished and that he will depart early the next morning. He mentions a final Gordon Town visit that morning, but he does not present 106 as a running count awaiting several more working days.

Possible but unproven scenarios remain:

- additional drawings were completed after the letter but before embarkation;
- the official report counted a category that Rudolf excluded from `finished work`;
- one source contains a numerical error or later transcription/OCR error;
- the report's `116 species` refers to drawings/records assembled institutionally rather than Rudolf's own internally defined completed-study count;
- duplicated, partial or supplementary studies were normalized differently by the two counters.

None is yet strong enough to choose.

Do **not** write `Rudolf completed 116 species in Jamaica` as if the archive is internally unanimous.

---

## 2. The two numbers belong to different actors and documentary purposes

The discrepancy is analytically useful because the counts are produced in different administrative settings.

### 106

- counter/speaker: Rudolf;
- date: 27 March, before departure;
- medium: private letter to parents;
- operation: his own statement of completed work;
- likely unit: `species` for which he considered his work finished.

### 116

- counter: Jamaican botanical administration;
- date of events: March 1892; publication printed 1893;
- medium: annual government botanical report;
- operation: institutional account of visiting foreign students and outputs;
- unit: species represented by accurate coloured drawings attributed to Blaschka.

The difference may therefore be a **counting-ontology problem**, even if one number eventually proves mistaken.

This reinforces pass 27/28: expedition numbers must retain actor, date, medium and operation.

---

## 3. Cameron's parallel output makes the institutional count especially important

`JAM-001` does more than provide 116. It says Cameron assembled dried specimens of the same and other plants for the herbarium. The Jamaican work therefore generated at least two linked but non-identical output series:

`Blaschka coloured drawings`

and

`Cameron dried/herbarium collections`.

The report's institutional perspective may have been built from the combined working record of the visit rather than from Rudolf's private self-count. This is a hypothesis to test, not a reconciliation.

A complete Cameron specimen list could supply an independent denominator or at least reveal whether a ten-item block exists outside Rudolf's 106 count.

---

## 4. Publication context is now clearer

The earlier source register left `exact issue/year` open. One part can now be closed:

- annual-report period: year ended **31 March 1892**;
- printing/publication imprint: **Jamaica Government Printing Office, Kingston, 1893**;
- digitized container: *Bulletin of the Botanical Department, Jamaica*, IA `bulletinofbotani3950fawc`, volume metadata `no.39-50 1893`;
- report contents explicitly include `13. Foreign Students in the Gardens`.

What remains open is the exact Bulletin issue/container logic if a formal issue number is required for citation. The annual report itself should be cited by its own title/imprint/page rather than forcing it into a Bulletin issue number before the bound volume architecture is inspected.

---

## 5. Consequence for the Jamaica yield sequence

Keep at least the following quantities distinct:

- early/forecast counts in Rudolf's Jamaica letters;
- Rudolf's **106** terminal self-count on 27 March;
- Jamaican institutional **116** coloured-drawing species count;
- Cameron's dried collection, described as the same and other plants but not yet enumerated specimen by specimen;
- later Harvard/Corning drawing inventories, which are preservation/catalogue counts rather than automatically expedition species counts;
- later model output derived selectively from Jamaica material.

There is no defensible single number called `the number of species Rudolf collected/studied in Jamaica` without specifying the operation.

---

## 6. Source-critical guards

1. `106` is direct in both German OCR and English translation of the 27 March letter and should be treated as Rudolf's own terminal count.
2. `116` is retained from the project's primary-source transcription/register of the Jamaican official report; publication use should still inspect the p.18 page image and quote its exact wording.
3. The difference of ten is **not** evidence that ten species were completed on 28 March.
4. Do not treat `drawn`, `studied`, `collected`, `dried`, and `modelled` as interchangeable verbs or denominators.
5. The report was printed in 1893 but reports the year ending 31 March 1892; do not misdate the field event to 1893.
6. Cameron's `same and other plants` prevents assuming a one-to-one 116-sheet herbarium counterpart.
7. Do not use later Harvard's `more than 300 specimens` figure to resolve this discrepancy; that aggregate combines 1892 and 1895 material.

## Working conclusion

The Jamaica corpus contains two terminal-looking counts generated by different knowledge systems. **Rudolf counted 106 completed species as he prepared to leave; the colonial botanical administration later counted 116 species in his coloured drawings. The ten-species gap is evidence about counting before it is evidence about productivity.**
