# Southwick seasonal dispatch and transatlantic packing — pass 40 — 13 August 2026

## Result

Pass 38 established a living California bulb supplied by Gillett & Horsford to Hosterwitz in 1890 and flowering there by July 1891. A catalogue-side pass now adds two pieces of commercial context, while preserving their different dates.

First, the title/advertising text surfaced for the **1890 Southwick Nurseries wholesale catalogue of North American perennials, new Tigridias and California bulbs** states that orders were filled when plants were ripe, **mostly from September until December**, and urged customers to order early. This is directly relevant to the workshop's statement that the Fritillaria bulb had been sent `last year`: it gives a plausible commercial dispatch season for living/bulb material from Southwick in 1890.

Second, a later **1895 Gillett & Horsford catalogue** explicitly describes packing and transmission as part of the firm's selling competence. It recommends express where practical because more care could be taken in packing, while saying small orders could be sent safely by mail and that the firm's packing method could carry them **even to Europe** in good condition. This is five years later than the Fritillaria transaction and is retained as longitudinal calibration, not direct evidence for the exact 1890 package.

The combined implication is modest but useful:

> **Southwick was not merely a botanical source. It was a commercial apparatus for timing, packing and dispatching living plant material, and by the mid-1890s it advertised European survivability as part of that apparatus.**

This gives the pre-1892 supply ecology a more operational form.

## 1. 1890 catalogue: biological ripeness structured commercial dispatch

The bibliographic record for the Southwick Nurseries 1890 wholesale catalogue is secure in BHL/NAL. Its title foregrounds `North American perennials`, `new Tigridias`, and `California bulbs`.

A reproduced excerpt of the catalogue gives the order rule: plants were dispatched when ripe, mostly during the September–December period, and orders were expected early.

For the Blaschka chain this matters because the supplier's commercial calendar was itself phenological. A bulb did not simply enter transport when a customer ordered it; dispatch waited on the biological/material condition in which nursery stock could be sent.

Working sequence:

`nursery stock available`
→ `plant/bulb reaches dispatchable biological state`
→ `autumn/early-winter commercial dispatch window`
→ `transatlantic movement`
→ `Hosterwitz cultivation`
→ `flowering/observation in 1891`.

The archival workshop letter does not give the exact 1890 dispatch month, so this sequence is a calibrated mechanism rather than a dated reconstruction of the individual bulb.

## 2. Relation to Fritillaria

`BLA-D01412 / 018:283` says in July 1891 that the California *Fritillaria* bulb had been sent by Gillett & Horsford `last year` and had flowered at Hosterwitz.

The Southwick 1890 catalogue's autumn dispatch window is compatible with an 1890 shipment followed by cultivation and flowering in 1891. Compatibility is all that should be claimed until an invoice, order, packing record or dated supplier letter identifies the exact shipment.

This strengthens one point from pass 36: remote supply had its own clocks before the 1892 expedition. There was a **nursery/dispatch clock** before the Hosterwitz cultivation clock and before the workshop observation/model clock.

---

## 3. 1895 catalogue: packing for Europe becomes explicit commercial knowledge

The 1895 Gillett & Horsford catalogue contains a useful statement about dispatch method. It advises express for this class of plants when distance permits because packing can receive more care and extra material can be added. Smaller orders can be sent by mail, and the catalogue claims that the firm's mode of packing makes them likely to arrive in good condition even in Europe.

This cannot be projected backward word-for-word onto the 1890 Fritillaria transaction. It does, however, establish that within the same firm and only five years later:

- packing method was treated as proprietary/practical competence;
- shipping mode was selected according to distance/order scale;
- European delivery was explicitly within the imagined customer geography;
- survival in transit was a selling point.

The nursery therefore belongs in the project's transport history as more than an origin point.

---

## 4. Packing knowledge exists on both sides of the Blaschka system

The current project already tracks the Blaschkas' own elaborate packing knowledge for finished glass. The Southwick material adds a useful counterflow. Living references reaching Hosterwitz depended on another packing tradition optimized for biological survival.

The two flows have different protection problems:

`living plant/bulb → preserve viability / prevent desiccation or destructive handling → cultivation after arrival`

versus

`finished glass model → immobilize fragile form / manage vibration and inspection → unpack/remount after arrival`.

Both are transport technologies, but their desired post-arrival affordances differ. The living package must remain capable of growth and flowering; the glass package must remain structurally intact and safely recoverable from its temporary supports.

This should remain a comparative mechanism rather than an assertion that the same materials or packers were used.

## 6. Source-critical guards

1. The September–December dispatch language comes from a reproduced excerpt of the 1890 catalogue; page/image verification in the BHL/NAL scan remains desirable for publication.
2. The exact Fritillaria dispatch date is unknown.
3. The 1895 European-packing statement is longitudinal evidence from the same firm, not proof of the exact method used in 1890.
4. `Even to Europe` does not identify Germany, Dresden or the Blaschkas as the destination of that catalogue statement.
5. Absence of a web-indexed Fritillaria hit is not catalogue absence.
6. Do not merge nursery packing for living plants with Blaschka packing for glass; compare functions and desired future states instead.
