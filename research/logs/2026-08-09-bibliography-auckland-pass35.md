# Bibliography expansion: Auckland museum transaction study, pass 35

Date: 2026-08-09

## Scope guard

Pass 35 adds a single source because it crosses the public-bibliography threshold through substantive Blaschka content rather than title wording alone. The article is a broader study of the Cheeseman–Ward correspondence, but Blaschka glass models are an explicit keyword and the paper contains a dedicated Blaschka section, transaction correspondence and a seven-model object table. It therefore documents the Blaschka distribution network at object and transaction level.

## Added record

B. J. Gill, H. R. Grenfell, and W. M. Blom, “The Cheeseman-Ward correspondence (1878–1905) and exchanges of natural history specimens between Auckland Museum and H. A. Ward of Rochester, N.Y.,” *Records of the Auckland Museum* 54 (2019): 21–36.

The source establishes or reconstructs:

- Cheeseman's September 1882 request for a priced Blaschka catalogue and preference for exchange where possible;
- Ward's October 1882 response sending the catalogue and accepting exchange material;
- Cheeseman's April 1884 request for one or two sample models before a larger commitment;
- the 1885 delivery/payment context involving Ward-linked preparator Charles Francis Adams;
- seven surviving Auckland Blaschka models, tabulated with Blaschka/Ward catalogue numbers, individual prices and Auckland registration numbers;
- Cheeseman's later proposed £40–£50 expansion, repeatedly deferred and not shown to have been completed.

## DOI guard

The article PDF itself prints DOI `10.32912/ram.2019.54.2`, and the public bibliography uses that form. Some secondary metadata aggregators expose a conflicting `10.32912/ram.2018.54.2` rendering. The article-imprint year, journal volume and pagination are 2019, 54, 21–36. The conflicting aggregator DOI is retained as a metadata anomaly and is not propagated into the public bibliography.

## Price/count guard

The paper contains an internal price discrepancy: prose gives US$14.64 for the seven models, while the individual table prices sum to US$14.65 and the following calculation uses US$14.65. Pass 35 does not reproduce a synthetic corrected total in the citation. The underlying provenance backend preserves both textual layers.

Seven is the complete Auckland Blaschka corpus reported in the 2019 paper, not automatically an August 2026 survival count. Likewise, the article reconstructs the seven models as Ward-supplied in 1885 while noting that none of the seven has standalone acquisition documentation. The public bibliography therefore describes the article's evidential contents without converting reconstruction into seven independent invoices.

## Exclusions from the same route

Frank Collins Baker's *A Naturalist in Mexico* (1895) contains a valuable contemporary eyewitness description of a Blaschka display at the Museo Nacional de México, but the Blaschka material is a brief passage inside a broad travel/natural-history book. Under the current strict public-bibliography rule it remains a provenance/source-layer record rather than a bibliography promotion. It can be reconsidered if a separate “historical primary mentions” category is introduced later.
