# Gillett–Horsford commercial form and chronology conflict — pass 43 — 13 August 2026

## Purpose

Pass 42 produced a strong person/place bridge between F. H. Horsford and Southwick in 1890. A broader catalogue chronology now introduces a second problem: the commercial form `Gillett & Horsford` does not behave like a simple partnership name that disappears once one actor leaves. This pass separates **person identity** from **firm/legal/commercial form**.

## Result

Three kinds of evidence now coexist:

1. `Gillett & Horsford` is directly attested as a Southwick nursery name in 1890, in Harvard's 1891 archival description, and again in National Agricultural Library metadata for an 1895 catalogue.
2. National Agricultural Library metadata for 1892 and 1893 Southwick catalogues attributes the publisher/note to Edward Gillett or Southwick Nurseries without the `& Horsford` form.
3. F. H. Horsford independently has a Charlotte, Vermont nursery catalogue stream by 1893–94.

This is not a clean sequence `Gillett & Horsford → partnership ends → Edward Gillett alone`. The `Gillett & Horsford` commercial name reappears in 1895 after Edward-only Southwick metadata in 1892–93, while F. H. Horsford is simultaneously visible as a Charlotte nursery firm.

The correct status is therefore:

> **person identity increasingly strong; commercial association form unresolved.**

Do not model `Gillett & Horsford` as an exclusive legal partnership timeline until direct business records establish what the name meant in each year.

## 1. Directly attested Southwick chronology

### 1890

BHL/NAL preserves `Gillett & Horsford's 1890 descriptive catalogue of wild flowers, shrubs, trees and vines` and a Southwick wholesale catalogue of `North American perennials, new Tigridias, California bulbs, etc.` Contemporary publication notices likewise register the Southwick nursery under the Gillett/Horsford name, with a `Hosford` spelling variant in one notice.

### 1891

Harvard HOLLIS archival description is unusually explicit: `Gillett and Horsford (Edward Gillett), 1891`, containing letters from Edward Gillett `of Gillett and Horsford, plant nursery in Southwick, MA.` Pass 38 independently verifies `Southwick Nurseries (Gillett & Horsford)` on the stationery and the cable address `HORSFORD, SOUTHWICK`.

### 1892–1893

NAL/AGRIS records for Southwick catalogues shift to:

- 1892: publisher `Southwick Nurseries`; note `Edward Gillett, Southwick Nurseries`;
- 1893: publisher `Edward Gillett`; note `Edward Gillett, [Southwick Nurseries]`.

These are bibliographic description fields, not direct proof that the Horsford association had ended.

### 1895

NAL/AGRIS again records a catalogue explicitly as `Gillett & Horsford's catalogue`, publisher and corporate author `Gillett & Horsford`, with the note `grown and for sale by Gillett & Horsford.`

This reappearance is the central chronology problem.

## 2. Independent F. H. Horsford firm by 1893

BHL/NAL separately preserves `Horsford's trade list of bulbs, ferns, orchids, and bog plants for the summer and autumn of '93`, by `F.H. Horsford (Firm)`, published at **Charlotte, Vermont**, [1893], with another issue/catalogue stream continuing into 1894–95.

Pass 42 already placed `F. H. Horsford` at Southwick in April 1890. If this is Frederick Hinsdale Horsford, the chronology permits several possibilities:

- a Southwick commercial association continued while Horsford also operated independently in Charlotte;
- `Gillett & Horsford` survived as a trading/catalogue label after the underlying working arrangement changed;
- NAL metadata normalizes publishers/corporate authors inconsistently across physical catalogues;
- different seasonal/product lists used different imprints or commercial identities.

Current evidence cannot choose among these.

## 3. Why legal-partnership language is now unsafe

The project has sometimes used `partner` as shorthand. That is now too specific. None of the inspected sources yet supplies articles of partnership, dissolution notice, directory entry identifying legal partners, or a catalogue title page spelling out personal names and roles.

Use these terms instead:

- `Gillett & Horsford` = **commercial firm/name/association attested at Southwick**;
- Edward Gillett = **directly identified member/representative of that Southwick firm in Harvard's 1891 archival description**;
- F. H. Horsford = **strongly linked to Southwick in 1890 and likely the Horsford of the firm, but exact business role/form unresolved**;
- Frederick Hinsdale Horsford's Charlotte nursery = **independent firm stream directly attested by 1893**.

Avoid `exclusive partnership` or a firm dissolution date until explicit evidence appears.

## 4. Bibliographic metadata must not be turned into a corporate history

The non-monotonic publisher fields are a methodological warning. Library catalogue records answer identification/access questions; they do not necessarily preserve every historical change in trading style.

Evidence hierarchy for commercial form:

1. signed contract / partnership notice / incorporation or business registration;
2. contemporaneous title page, invoice, letterhead, advertisement or directory entry;
3. archival folder description grounded in the physical correspondence;
4. contemporary publication notice;
5. NAL/BHL publisher/corporate-author metadata;
6. later nursery/local histories.

Metadata remains excellent for locating objects. It should not override direct imprints or stationery.

## 5. Consequence for the Blaschka supply chain

The workshop source needs less corporate reconstruction than the actor-history problem does. For the 1890 bulb, the secure statement remains:

`California Fritillaria bulb → sent by Gillett & Horsford, Southwick Mass. → Hosterwitz cultivation → flowering in 1891 → comparison with literature → modelling/taxonomic uncertainty`.

Who legally owned or partnered in `Gillett & Horsford` is a second-order question unless it reveals how the bulb was sourced, named, packed or routed.

The commercial-form problem matters because the same people may have participated in overlapping networks. If F. H. Horsford moved between/among Pringle, Southwick and Charlotte operations, plant movement may have followed **inter-firm or inter-personal botanical networks** rather than a single vertically integrated nursery.

## Working conclusion

The person-level bridge has strengthened faster than the firm-level history. The safest reconstruction now treats `Gillett & Horsford` as a historically attested Southwick commercial association whose exact legal and operational form changed or remains obscured across catalogues. This keeps the Blaschka transaction secure while preventing a neat but unsupported partnership chronology.
