# Rudolf 1892 herbarium denominator / evidence-bounds pass 30 — 13 August 2026

## Result

Pass 19's Tempe packet is useful enough that it now needs a formal methodological brake. We currently have nine independently indexed Ganong–Blaschka sheets from a tightly dated April 1892 Tempe episode. Only Hoffmannseggia has a secure later glass descendant at present. That asymmetry supports **branching and selective reactivation**. It does not support a `1/9 conversion rate`.

The reason is archival rather than statistical. Harvard's Blaschka/Ware finding aid says that more than 300 herbarium specimens collected by Rudolf and Harvard-affiliated botanists during the **1892 and 1895 visits combined** remain in the Harvard University Herbaria. The finding aid does not split that aggregate by expedition. The public specimen database is also a digitized/indexed access layer into a larger and changing physical collection. Our nine Tempe sheets are therefore a minimum discovered packet, not a closed denominator.

> **Selective conversion is currently an event-level mechanism, not a measured expedition-wide rate.**

No public map change.

---

## 1. Three denominators must be kept separate

### D1 — current discovered/indexed packet

The nine Tempe sheets already recovered and checked in HUH metadata.

This denominator is good for microhistory. It lets us show repeated sampling, tight chronology, multiple taxa and divergent later histories. It does not define everything Ganong and Rudolf collected in Tempe.

### D2 — complete 1892 Ganong–Blaschka herbarium subset

This would mean every HUH sheet attributable to **W. F. Ganong & R. Blaschka in 1892**, with barcode, collection date, locality, historical name, accepted name and image.

We do not yet possess this as a bounded export or complete query result.

D2 is the first denominator from which a systematic herbarium-to-model crosswalk could legitimately be built.

### D3 — all 1892 expedition reference outputs

The expedition produced far more than herbarium sheets:

- colour and analytical drawings;
- dried specimens;
- alcohol-preserved material;
- seeds;
- bulbs;
- living cacti and other plants;
- detached fresh material supplied later;
- perhaps reference photographs and additional media.

These were produced by several people and moved through different routes. D3 is therefore heterogeneous and much harder to close. Any claim about the proportion of **field references** eventually turned into glass needs this broader denominator, not the herbarium alone.

---

## 2. What the current Tempe packet really proves

The packet still does substantial work.

First, it shows that several references were stabilized as herbarium objects in the same short episode. Second, the two *Eriogonum inflatum* sheets on adjacent days demonstrate repeated sampling, which is a stronger archival fact than a simple species checklist. Third, Hoffmannseggia gives one high-confidence metadata-level descendant chain:

`Tempe field reference → GH 01676890 → later Hosterwitz production → model 433 → January 1894 shipment`.

Several other sheets currently terminate at the herbarium in our reconstruction. Their status is `no secure model descendant yet`, not `never modelled`.

The packet therefore establishes **non-identical archival branching** even before the denominator is complete. Different references from the same collecting interval acquire different currently visible afterlives.

---

## 3. Search absence has several meanings

We now need a formal negative-evidence discipline because three distinct absences can look alike.

### No HUH search hit

This can mean no specimen, a cataloguing/digitization gap, a collector-name variation, a taxonomic-name variation, or simply that the current query failed to surface it. It cannot be translated directly into historical absence.

### No exact model-list string

This can reflect a real non-descendant, a historical synonym, spelling variation, changed determination, or a model label organized under another name. Exact-string absence is only the first pass.

### No direct sheet-to-model genealogy

Even when the taxon, date and model line up, the surviving herbarium sheet may have been one reference among several. A drawing, fresh specimen, alcohol material or later living plant could have been more important in manufacture.

This is why Hoffmannseggia remains `metadata-closed / physical-template open`.

---

## 4. The >300 finding-aid figure cannot be borrowed as an 1892 denominator

The Harvard finding aid's `more than 300 specimens` is useful evidence for the scale of surviving expedition-related herbarium material. It combines **1892 and 1895** and includes Rudolf plus affiliated botanists. Using it as a count for the first journey would erase exactly the provenance distinctions the project is trying to recover.

The right workflow is item-level:

`barcode → collector(s) → collection date → locality → historical determination → accepted determination → image/annotations → 1892/1895 assignment → model/drawing crosswalk`.

Only after that can we ask whether there is a meaningful retention or later-reactivation distribution.

---

## 5. This actually strengthens the article

A weaker paper would present a neat percentage produced from a partially visible corpus. The stronger result is to show why nineteenth-century reference systems resist a single denominator.

The same field encounter could branch into several media. Those media had different survival rates, cataloguing systems and later production functions. A historical `conversion rate` risks treating all references as interchangeable units when the archive shows they were operationally different.

The methodological claim is therefore substantive:

> **The unit of reference has to be reconstructed before its rate of conversion can be counted.**

This fits the broader argument that transport repeatedly changed what counted as the usable object.

---

## 6. Immediate retrieval path

The highest-value move is a collector-level HUH result set for `W. F. Ganong & R. Blaschka`, year 1892. If the public interface cannot reliably return/export the full set, a staff-assisted database extract would be preferable to piecemeal web indexing.

Once D2 is bounded, the next stage is historical-name normalization against:

- Harvard old model labels;
- updated model labels;
- annotated shipment lists;
- Corning/Rakow preparatory-drawing records.

Only then should exact negatives be upgraded.
