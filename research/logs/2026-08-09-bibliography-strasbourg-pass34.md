# Bibliography expansion: Strasbourg collection and conservation records, pass 34

Date: 2026-08-09

## Scope guard

This pass promotes only publications and institutional interpretation that materially document the Musée Zoologique de Strasbourg Blaschka collection. General museum publicity and generic glass/conservation literature remain excluded.

## Pass 34

Added three direct Strasbourg records:

- “Trésors de verre,” *Strasbourg Magazine*, no. 288 (2018). The municipal magazine discusses the Musée Zoologique's 58 Blaschka models, their 1890 acquisition and teaching use, restoration needs, loans, photography and planned catalogue publication.
- Samuel Cordier, “Agathe Petit : « Il s’agit de la chose la plus étonnante que j’aie vue »,” interview with Agathe Petit, Musées de Strasbourg, November 2022. This is direct conservator testimony following Petit's work on the collection and includes collection-wide condition and conservation discussion.
- Strasbourg Culture, “Les modèles Blaschka,” in *À la découverte des Chefs-d’œuvres des Musées de Strasbourg* (2025). This is the current official collection-interpretation layer and records the first presentation of all 58 models together.

## Deduplication

Two closely related Strasbourg sources were deliberately excluded because they are already represented in the public bibliography:

- Université de Strasbourg, “Portrait d’objet #5: les modèles d’invertébrés en verre des Blaschka” (2017), already present in pass 20.
- Agathe Petit, *“Le céphalopode brisé”: étude et conservation-restauration d’un modèle anatomique en verre de Sepia officinalis de Leopold et Rudolf Blaschka, 1890* (Institut national du patrimoine, 2020), already present in pass 15.

## Count guard

Strasbourg sources use different count/status formulations across time. The 2017 institutional material reported 53 remaining models in one context, while the 2018 municipal feature uses a historical/collection figure of 58 and the 2025 current interpretation states that all 58 models are presented together. These figures are not silently collapsed into a single count ontology. Model, series, surviving physical object, restored unit and displayed unit may not be identical counting units; an object-level register remains the authority needed for reconciliation.

## Evidence hierarchy

The 2018 item is a municipal publication, the 2022 interview is a museum-hosted primary professional testimony from the conservator-restorer, and the 2025 item is current official museum/cultural interpretation. They are retained because together they document collection history, conservation and present display rather than merely repeating aesthetic reception.
