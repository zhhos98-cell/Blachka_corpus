# Bibliography expansion: Sheffield Elmfield seed

Date: 2026-08-09

The University of Sheffield Elmfield Collection is now being used as a bounded discovery corpus for material and technical context around Blaschka glassworking. The collection contains 632 volumes (1662–2001) from W. E. S. Turner's personal library and the Joint Library of Glass Technology Rare Book Collection.

## Rule

The Elmfield catalogue is not treated as a Blaschka bibliography. Entries are promoted to the public bibliography only when they are useful for reconstructing one of the following:

- lampworking / glassblowing practice;
- scientific and laboratory glassworking;
- glass composition and colour;
- durability, deterioration and conservation context;
- historically proximate glass technologies relevant to reading workshop practice.

Promoted records carry `data-bib-context="material"` and an explicit `[Material-context source]` note so they are not confused with publications directly about Leopold and Rudolf Blaschka.

### Pass 18: scientific-glassworking lineage

- Eugène Péligot, *Le verre: son histoire, sa fabrication* (1877)

- H. P. Waran, *Elements of Glass-Blowing* (1923)
- Carl Woytacek, *Lehrbuch der Glasbläserei* (1924)
- M. C. Nokes, *Modern Glass Working and Laboratory Technique* (1937)
- R. H. Wright, *Manual of Laboratory Glass-Blowing* (1943)
- Julius D. Heldman, *Techniques of Glass Manipulation in Scientific Research* (1946)

This pass was cross-checked against a 1998 American Scientific Glassblowers Society proceedings bibliography, contemporary *Nature* reviews and independent library records. The point is not to posit direct Blaschka dependence on these manuals. It reconstructs the textual ecology in which lampworking and scientific glass manipulation were codified, taught, repaired and divided between laboratory workers and specialist glassblowers.

### Pass 19: colour / deterioration / properties

- Julia de Fontenelle, *Manuel complet du verrier... verres colorés, yeux artificiels, etc.* (1829)
- James Fowler, *On the Process of Decay in Glass...* (book reprint 1879; *Archaeologia* publication 1880)
- Walter Rosenhain, *Glass Manufacture* (1908)
- W. E. S. Turner, “The Nature and Constitution of Glass” (1925)
- W. E. S. Turner, “The Physical Properties of Glasses: The Relationship to Chemical Composition and Mode of Preparation” (1926)

This pass begins to connect the historical glassworking literature to the questions that recur in Blaschka conservation: colourants, specialised glass forms, annealing, chemical resistance, compositional variation and deterioration.

## Public interface change

The bibliography toolbar now has a **Layer** control:

- **All** — complete working bibliography;
- **Blaschka** — direct scholarship, historical reception, collection publications and exhibitions;
- **Material context** — the bounded technical layer described above.

CSV and CSL-JSON exports follow the active layer filter, and CSV now records the layer explicitly. This keeps the technical ecology usable without allowing it to blur into claims about direct influence on the Blaschkas.

## Cross-checks

Where possible the Sheffield catalogue record is paired with an independent open, institutional or contemporary record (Smithsonian Libraries, Project Gutenberg, Open Library, Google Books, *Nature*, RSC, Cambridge Core, national or university library catalogues). This is especially useful where Elmfield preserves a later edition but an earlier edition is historically closer to the Blaschka workshop period.

## Next extraction targets

1. Mine the nineteenth-century colour / enamel / artificial-eye literature more selectively, especially where manuals explain actual materials, fuel, joining, painting or reheating operations.
2. Mine the Society of Glass Technology journal for compatibility, annealing, lead/soda/potash glasses, colour chemistry and working properties, with priority to papers that illuminate failure or repair rather than generic industrial manufacture.
3. Cross-check the historical technical corpus against the Blaschka conservation literature: McNally/Buschini, van Giffen, Koob, Brierley, Rydlová/Kopecká, Petit, and Giani/Ferucci et al. Record only explicit technical correspondences; do not infer workshop recipes from later glass-science literature.
4. Consider a small visible `Material context` badge on entries if the context layer continues to grow; the filter already separates the layers operationally.
