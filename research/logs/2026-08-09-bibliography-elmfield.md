# Bibliography scope correction: Sheffield Elmfield seed

Date: 2026-08-09

The University of Sheffield Elmfield Collection was tested as a discovery source for glassworking literature. That experiment expanded too far beyond the intended bibliography scope.

## Corrected rule

The public bibliography is **Blaschka-specific**. A record belongs there when the publication itself substantially concerns Leopold Blaschka, Rudolf Blaschka, Blaschka glass models, a Blaschka collection, exhibition, conservation treatment, material analysis, documentation project, reception history, workshop history, distribution, or a directly related Blaschka institutional/object case.

General literature on glassblowing, lampworking, glass composition, colour, durability, scientific glass, glass manufacture, or laboratory technique does **not** enter the public bibliography merely because it provides useful historical context.

The Sheffield Elmfield catalogue may still be used as a **discovery aid**: search it for Blaschka-specific entries, citations, exhibition catalogues, collection publications, conservation literature, or bibliographic trails that lead back to Blaschka material. General glass literature remains outside this bibliography.

## Rollback

The experimental public material-context passes 16–19 were removed. The bibliography now stops at pass 15 and returns to the Blaschka-specific scope. The temporary public `Layer` filter and material-context export field were also removed.

The removed experimental records included general manuals and studies by Danger, Péligot, Shenstone, Fischer, Djakonow/Lermantoff, Bolas, Frary, Turner, Barr/Anhorn, Gillinder, Gerspach, Fowler, Rosenhain, Vigreux, Waran, Woytacek, Nokes, Wright, Heldman and others. They may remain useful to separate historical research questions, but they are not entries in the Blaschka bibliography.

## Continuing discovery protocol

1. Mine Elmfield only for **Blaschka-name / Blaschka-object / Blaschka-collection** leads.
2. Continue Corning/Rakow catalogue metadata and reference-list chaining for direct Blaschka literature.
3. Search museum and university collection bibliographies for local Blaschka publications, exhibition catalogues, conservation reports and theses.
4. Continue non-English discovery, especially German, Czech, Italian, French, Japanese and other national/local collection literature.
5. Deduplicate editions and institutional reposts unless they contain materially distinct Blaschka content.
6. Keep contextual glass-history literature in separate research notes if needed; do not promote it to the public bibliography.
