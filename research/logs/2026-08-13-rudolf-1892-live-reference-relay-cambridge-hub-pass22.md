# Rudolf 1892 live-reference relay / Cambridge packet hub pass 22 — 13 August 2026

## Purpose

Pass 21 showed that selectivity began in the field: plants were evaluated, retained or rejected, then assigned to drawings, fresh holding, dried specimens, alcohol or live carriage. The next question is what happened once those reference forms actually began to move.

The western correspondence reveals a packet system already operating before Rudolf turned back east. Different references left California on different schedules and with different intended destinations while Rudolf and Ganong continued their journey toward San Francisco and Oakland. Cambridge was neither simply the final destination nor a passive institutional address. In early May it functioned both as a **temporary cache** and as a **relay point** to Hosterwitz.

The strongest result is a small box of living cacti and bulbs assembled in San Diego. Its contents had different geographical histories: some cacti came from Jamaica, others from Arizona, while bulbs were acquired through the San Diego botanist Charles Orcutt. The box was dispatched from San Diego to Cambridge on 4 May with instructions that Goodale forward it to Hosterwitz.

This yields a useful formulation:

> **The expedition created a packet network rather than a single return cargo. Person and references could move in opposite directions, Cambridge could give different packets different dwell times and next hops, and one small box could aggregate botanical material whose provenance stretched from Jamaica to Arizona to San Diego.**

A second result concerns the purpose of live transport. Rudolf preferred living cacti to alcohol because the workshop could grow their flowers itself. The packet therefore carried a capacity for **future observation** as well as a present plant body.

No public map changes are made.

---

## 1. Tempe: the packet begins as a future route rather than an object already boxed

On 22 April at Tempe (`BLA-D00785 / 017:139`), Rudolf says that after drawing the flowers of about four cactus species he dug up young examples. He explicitly distinguishes two preservation possibilities. These cacti did not have to be conserved in alcohol. Instead, he intended to send them from California to his parents in Hosterwitz.

This is more than a collecting note. At the moment of collection Rudolf assigns the live plants a future itinerary:

`Tempe field → carry west to California → dispatch east/transatlantic → Hosterwitz cultivation`.

The route is projected before the packet exists physically. The reference medium and the intended transport chain are chosen together.

---

## 2. San Diego, 30 April: Arizona cacti acquire new companions

The continuation of Rudolf's 30 April San Diego letter (`BLA-D00803 / 017:157`, with the dated header at `BLA-D00804 / 017:158`) confirms that the Arizona plants remained with him during the westward journey.

He says that the cacti he personally collected for the workshop in Arizona have been brought to San Diego. He now intends to obtain important bulbs through Mr. Orcutt and send the whole group together to his parents.

The packet has therefore changed since Tempe. It is no longer simply a set of Arizona cacti. San Diego acts as an **assembly point** where personally collected material and locally procured material are combined.

This is also another example of scientific mobility using a local specialist rather than direct field collection alone. Orcutt's contribution allows Rudolf to supplement what his own short stay could produce.

---

## 3. 4 May: actual dispatch to Cambridge

The 4 May San Diego letter (`BLA-D00814 / 017:168`) closes the first physical leg.

Rudolf says that a **small box of cacti and some flowering bulbs was sent that day with other things to Cambridge**. He will ask Goodale to send it onward to his parents.

The route therefore becomes direct at least to the level of dispatch:

`San Diego → Cambridge → requested onward forwarding to Hosterwitz`.

The Hosterwitz receipt is still open. Current searches have not recovered a clean letter from Leopold, Karolina or Rudolf confirming that this particular box arrived, survived and was cultivated. That final leg should remain an intended/forwarded route until a receipt source is found.

The same letter also records condition risk. Rudolf expects the material generally to arrive unharmed, except perhaps a piece of giant cactus cut from a large individual that is already beginning to rot at the cut surface. Biological deterioration is therefore underway before the parcel has even left North America.

---

## 4. The San Diego box had a deeper geography than its dispatch label

A later California letter in the same sequence (`BLA-D00840 / 017:194`) identifies part of the already-sent cactus material more precisely. Rudolf says that three cacti were collected by him in Jamaica:

- a long snake cactus;
- a small *Cereus* from Kingston;
- an *Opuntia*.

He says these plants have to be grown in the house.

Joined to the 30 April and 4 May letters, the small San Diego box now has at least three provenance strata:

1. **Jamaica** — three named cactus references carried back through Cambridge and across the United States;
2. **Arizona** — cacti collected by Rudolf and physically carried west to San Diego;
3. **San Diego** — flowering bulbs acquired through Orcutt.

Thus `sent from San Diego` is a dispatch fact, not an origin statement.

This becomes a source-critical rule for the map and later shipment analysis:

> **A packet's point of dispatch can conceal the different routes already travelled by its contents.**

The same mechanism recurs later in finished-glass shipments, where one consignment aggregates models made at different times from different reference lineages.

---

## 5. Live carriage transported a future observational event

The most interesting sentence in `BLA-D00840` explains why Rudolf wants the cactus material alive. The cacti he has sent are easier to handle than the notorious *Opuntia bigelovii* he encountered in the desert, and, because the workshop needs them, it is better to receive them **alive rather than in alcohol because they can grow the flower themselves**.

The immediate historical category is practical: live cultivation permits later flowering.

Analytically, this means the reference packet carries more than a preserved morphology. It carries a biological process whose useful state has not yet occurred. The desired observation is deferred:

`living cactus in transit → survives → grows in Hosterwitz → later flowers → workshop observes flower`.

Working term: **deferred observability** or **transporting future observation**. Neither phrase should be attributed to Rudolf. They summarize the mechanism his letter describes.

This is a particularly strong instance of the split workshop. Rudolf can leave the Arizona spring behind while moving the plant's capacity to produce a later flower into the Hosterwitz work environment.

---

## 6. A parallel lane: Ganong sends two boxes east to wait

The 7 May letter (`BLA-D00829 / 017:183`) reveals a different reference packet regime.

Rudolf says Ganong is taking care of the plants using glass containers with alcohol and water and dried specimens. He then reports that Ganong has already **sent two boxes of material to Cambridge**, where the boxes will remain until Rudolf is ready to return home.

The route looks superficially similar to the cactus box because both move east toward Cambridge while the travellers remain in California. Their logistical function is different.

For Ganong's two boxes:

`California → Cambridge → dwell / cache until Rudolf returns`.

For the live cactus-bulb packet:

`San Diego → Cambridge → requested relay to Hosterwitz`.

Cambridge therefore assigns different temporal futures to different packets.

---

## 7. Cambridge becomes a packet hub

This evidence changes the meaning of Cambridge in the 1892 route model. It is not simply the expedition's institutional starting and ending point.

In May it performs at least two functions:

- **cache:** preserved and dried materials are sent there ahead of Rudolf and held;
- **relay:** a living mixed-origin packet is sent there so Goodale can forward it to Hosterwitz.

The term `packet hub` or `switchyard` is analytical shorthand. The historical sources do not call Harvard either. But the distinction is empirically direct because the letters specify different dwell/next-hop instructions.

This also creates one of the clearest examples of asynchronous transport in the entire project. Rudolf's body is still moving west and north through California. Reference materials are already moving east. Some stop in Cambridge. Another packet is intended to continue east across the Atlantic.

The split workshop is therefore being coupled **before the travelling maker returns home**.

---

## 8. The expedition does not have one return date

A conventional travel narrative tends to give the expedition a departure, itinerary and return. The packet evidence produces several returns:

- letters and postcards return information continuously;
- seeds can be posted separately;
- dried/alcohol material can be sent ahead to Cambridge;
- living references can be routed through Cambridge toward Hosterwitz;
- Rudolf himself returns later;
- some observational value will only become available after a living plant flowers.

`Return` is therefore distributed across media and clocks.

This connects pass 22 directly to the earlier multi-clock model. The date Rudolf reaches Cambridge or Hosterwitz cannot stand in for the return of the expedition's knowledge materials.

---

## 9. Relation to the paper's argument

Pass 21 established:

`field encounter → selection → media assignment`.

Pass 22 adds:

`media assignment → packet assembly → asynchronous dispatch → cache / relay → later use`.

The result is a more concrete account of how the split workshop stayed operational. The connection between Arizona and Hosterwitz was not an abstract network. It depended on making a cactus into something that could remain alive, carrying it to another city, combining it with other botanical material, assigning a forwarding intermediary, and keeping its future flowering possible across the journey.

The field side can now be expressed as:

> **Transport did not simply carry references after fieldwork. It was part of producing the reference. What was selected, whether it was drawn, dried, preserved or kept alive, how it was bundled with other material, where it paused, and who was expected to forward it determined what the distant workshop would later be able to know and make.**

---

## 10. Map decision

Do not add a generic San Diego–Cambridge line yet.

The cactus/bulb packet is potentially strong enough to become a public object packet, but only if its internal provenance is visible. A simple origin-destination line would be misleading because the contents already incorporate Jamaica, Arizona and San Diego histories.

A useful packet card could eventually show:

`Jamaica cacti + Arizona cacti + San Diego bulbs → assembled San Diego → 4 May dispatch → Cambridge relay → Hosterwitz intended/receipt open`.

Ganong's two boxes should be shown separately as:

`western preserved/dried material → Cambridge cache → waits for Rudolf`.

This makes the map represent packet logic rather than merely adding traffic.
