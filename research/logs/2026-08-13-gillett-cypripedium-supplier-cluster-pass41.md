# Gillett Cypripedium supplier cluster — pass 41 — 13 August 2026

## Result

Pass 41 tests whether Rudolf's 2 June 1892 statement that Edward Gillett had sent the workshop `the pretty Cypripeds` can be joined to specific Harvard Glass Flower models. The current corpus gets surprisingly close but does not close item-level genealogy.

The strongest current alignment is:

- direct 1892 testimony: Gillett of Southwick had already sent catalogues and `the pretty Cypripeds` to Hosterwitz;
- the reconstructed July 1891 fifth botanical shipment contains **223. *Cypripedium pubescens*, Willd.** and **224. *Cypripedium spectabile*, Salisb.**;
- the same shipment block also contains the independently documented Gillett-supplied California *Fritillaria* reconstructed as botanical model 237.

This establishes a **supplier cluster**, not yet an object-level source chain.

> **Gillett supplied Cypripedium material to the workshop before June 1892, and two Cypripedium models occur in the same 1891 consignment that contains another independently documented Gillett-supplied plant. The surviving evidence makes 223/224 high-value candidates, but does not yet prove that either model was made from Gillett's particular plants.**

No public-map change.

## 1. Direct testimony: Gillett had supplied Cypripedium before June 1892

`BLA-D00902 / 017:257`, Rudolf from St Louis, 2 June 1892, says he intends to visit Mr. Gillett in Southwick, `who sent you the catalogues and sent us the pretty Cypripeds, etc.` He wants to discuss in person the future acquisition of seeds and plants.

This wording establishes several things directly:

- Gillett was already known to the Hosterwitz household/workshop before the 1892 trip;
- catalogues had circulated from Southwick to Hosterwitz;
- actual living botanical material, specifically Cypripedium plants, had also been sent;
- the planned 1892 visit was conceived partly as procurement-network maintenance rather than first contact;
- the same letter distinguishes commercial/deferred acquisition from Rudolf's own seed collecting, which had been limited because many plants were still in spring state and seed generally came later.

The date of the Cypripedium shipment is not stated in this letter.

## 2. Annotated Harvard shipment list: models 223 and 224

The Harvard annotated botanical shipment list preserves the following consecutive entries:

- **223. *Cypripedium pubescens*, Willd.**
- **224. *Cypripedium spectabile*, Salisb.**
- 225. *Odontoglossum rossii*, Lindl.
- 226. *Oncidium tigrinum* ...
- 227. *Lycaste skinneri* ...
- 228. *Lycaste aromatica* ...

These entries sit inside the same reconstructed shipment block **192–251**, which the 19 July 1891 workshop letter independently fixes at exactly sixty plant models. Pass 38 reconstructs *Fritillaria parviflora* Torr. as botanical model 237 in this same consignment, and the 19 July letter explicitly identifies that Fritillaria source as a California bulb sent by Gillett & Horsford the previous year.

Thus the shipment contains:

`223 Cypripedium pubescens`

`224 Cypripedium spectabile`

`...`

`237 Fritillaria parviflora [reconstructed number]`

with only the Fritillaria currently having an explicit supplier statement at item level.

## 3. Why the alignment is suggestive but insufficient

It is tempting to read Rudolf's plural `Cypripeds` backward into models 223 and 224, especially because:

1. there are exactly two consecutively numbered Cypripedium models in the July 1891 shipment;
2. Gillett is independently documented as a supplier of another plant in the same consignment;
3. Rudolf later remembers Gillett specifically through catalogues and Cypripedium supply.

But none of these points establishes identity. Several alternatives remain live:

- Gillett may have sent different Cypripedium species;
- the supplied plants may have been studied but modelled in another shipment;
- 223/224 may have come from another garden, nursery or Harvard-mediated source;
- Gillett may have sent multiple Cypripedium packets over several years;
- `pretty Cypripeds` may refer to living material retained for cultivation or future work rather than models already completed by July 1891.

Therefore this case is coded **candidate_supplier_to_model_crosswalk**, not direct provenance.

## 4. Negative search result across the current OCR corpus

Targeted searches were run for:

- `Cypripedium pubescens` + Gillett / Southwick;
- `Cypripedium spectabile` + Gillett / Southwick;
- Cypripedium + Gillett + 1890/1891;
- orchid + Gillett + Hosterwitz;
- the 1892 phrase `pretty Cypripeds`.

The only direct Gillett/Cypripedium statement recovered remains the 2 June 1892 St Louis letter. The exact species names are recovered in the annotated shipment list, but no current OCR letter joins either species to Gillett.

This negative result fixes the present evidence boundary. It is not proof that no supplier letter, invoice, catalogue entry or lost enclosure once named the species.

## 5. Supplier clusters as a useful intermediate evidential category

Passes 38–41 suggest that the project needs an evidential state between generic supplier context and item-level provenance.

**supplier cluster** — a bounded set of objects/materials for which the same supplier is independently active in the same period and source environment, but the item-to-supplier link remains unclosed.

Suggested status hierarchy:

1. `supplier_context_only`
2. `supplier_cluster_candidate`
3. `item_level_supplier_statement`
4. `transaction_document_crosswalk`
5. `closed_supplier_to_reference_to_model_chain`

For the July 1891 shipment:

- Fritillaria 237: at least `item_level_supplier_statement`, with model-number reconstruction pending image verification;
- Cypripedium 223/224: `supplier_cluster_candidate`;
- adjacent orchids such as Odontoglossum, Oncidium and Lycaste: no Gillett linkage currently established.

This prevents adjacency from turning into provenance while still retaining the pattern as a retrieval guide.

## 6. Relation to the pre-1892 supply ecology

The Cypripedium evidence makes the Gillett relationship look broader than a single California bulb. By June 1892 Rudolf remembers Gillett through at least three functions:

- catalogue circulation;
- prior living-plant supply, including Cypripedium;
- future procurement of seeds and plants to be discussed in person.

The supplier therefore acted as a continuing reference-access node rather than a one-off vendor. This supports pass 36's larger claim that the American expedition was inserted into an already functioning remote supply ecology.

The important historical unit is not `nursery → one model`, but potentially:

`catalogue information → selection/procurement → living plants → transatlantic cultivation → workshop observation → some subset enters model production → later supplier relationship renewed in person or by correspondence`.

The individual branches still require item-level closure.

## 7. Source-critical guards

1. Do not say models 223/224 were supplied by Gillett.
2. Do not infer that Rudolf's plural `Cypripeds` means exactly two plants.
3. Do not infer the shipment date of the Cypripedium material from the 1892 retrospective wording.
4. Keep the independently documented Fritillaria supplier link separate from the Cypripedium candidate link.
5. Do not extend the candidate Gillett cluster automatically to the adjacent Odontoglossum, Oncidium or Lycaste entries merely because they are orchids or in the same list neighborhood.
6. Keep Harvard botanical sequence numbers namespaced as `WARE-BOT-MODEL-0223` and `WARE-BOT-MODEL-0224`; do not confuse them with commercial zoological catalogue numbers.
7. Publication-level use should inspect the original annotated shipment-list image and the original 2 June 1892 letter/translation packet.
