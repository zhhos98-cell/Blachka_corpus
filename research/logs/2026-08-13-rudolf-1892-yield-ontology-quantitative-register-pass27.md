# Rudolf 1892 yield ontology / quantitative register pass 27 — 13 August 2026

## Purpose

Pass 26 argued that the expedition's yield was governed rather than simply harvested. This pass makes that claim operational by putting the major quantitative statements into one register and refusing to treat unlike numbers as if they measured the same thing.

The result is immediately useful. Some apparent discrepancies disappear once date and operation are restored. Others remain historically productive because different actors are valuing different outputs.

The strongest rule is:

> **A number in the 1892 archive is not yet a yield. It becomes evidence only after we identify who counted, what operation was being counted, and at what stage of the reference-to-model chain.**

No public map changes are made.

---

## 1. February already separates model-output expectations from field yield

On 15 February (`BLA-D00682 / 017:36`), Rudolf, the Wares and Goodale agree that the makers will no longer be bound to exactly **60 species**. They will make as many as can be comfortably produced, with **50** as the minimum.

These numbers belong to future model production. They are not collection quotas.

Four days later (`BLA-D00695 / 017:49`), Rudolf sends Hosterwitz **about 29 species of seeds** from the Cambridge Botanic Garden with sowing instructions. That is a reference packet count, again not a field-expedition yield.

The archive therefore contains incompatible species numbers before Jamaica has even begun because `species` is attached to different operations.

---

## 2. Jamaica 106 versus 116 remains a real unresolved ontology problem

Rudolf's 27 March Hope Garden letter is unusually clear. Before leaving Jamaica he writes that he has **finished his work with 106 species**.

The Jamaican Botanical Department's institutional report later says that Blaschka made **accurate coloured drawings of 116 species**, while Cameron made dried collections of the same and other plants.

These figures should remain side by side.

They may differ because:

- `work with species` and `coloured drawings` are not identical categories;
- the institutional report may include work completed after Rudolf's own count or compiled from a different ledger;
- one source may count multiple drawing states differently;
- transcription or reporting practice may intervene.

At present the ten-unit difference is not solved.

The important improvement is that it is no longer automatically classified as an error.

## 3. Tempe's 10 and 25 are different working sets

On 20 April (`BLA-D00779 / 017:133`), after substantial drawing Rudolf says he now has **10 interesting species**.

On 22 April (`BLA-D00785 / 017:139`), after the desert excursion and explicit filtering of tiny plants, he reports **25 species useful to them**.

Nothing requires these to be the same category or a simple cumulative sequence.

The first count belongs to a recent drawing/work set. The second belongs to a field-selection result defined by usefulness, showiness and characteristic form.

Until an intermediate source explicitly joins them, keep them separate.

---

## 4. San Diego gives a clean example of temporal progression: 25 → 36

San Diego is much easier.

In the early-May letter sequence Rudolf says that he draws during the day and has completed **25 species in San Diego so far**.

On 4 May, as he is leaving the city (`BLA-D00814 / 017:168`), he calls the stay botanically favorable and says he completed **36 species in San Diego**, aided particularly by Orcutt's procurement of plant material.

Here the difference is a closed temporal progression:

`interim San Diego count = 25`

`departure San Diego count = 36`.

This case is useful methodologically because it shows how a later number can supersede an earlier count without making the earlier one false. The earlier count records work rate and timing.

## 5. Colorado's 200, 400 and 600–800 belong to three different temporal modes

`BLA-D00879 / 017:233` contains three large numbers in one letter.

Rudolf says:

- **200** species have been drawn and studied so far;
- he thinks he may have **400** by the time he leaves America;
- to obtain **600–800** he would have to spend the whole year wandering around America.

These are respectively:

1. current cumulative throughput;
2. projected final yield;
3. counterfactual year-long yield.

They should never appear in a table as three competing estimates of `the number collected`.

The same paragraph explains the governing variables: seasonal scarcity, Cambridge duplicate rejection, bodily workload and limited travel time.

The numbers are therefore part of an argument Rudolf himself is making about feasible throughput.

## 6. June changes the unit from species to years

At the end of the journey, counting changes scale.

Rudolf's Cambridge return letter (`BLA-D00927 / 017:282` continuation) evaluates his accumulated drawings/studies as roughly **three years of material/work**. The exact wording still deserves image-level confirmation.

After Rudolf leaves, Goodale writes to the Wares (`BLA-D00091 / 015:91`) that in his own view Rudolf has material on hand for about **four years' work**.

This is not a 3-versus-4 factual dispute that needs averaging.

The two estimates belong to different actors:

- maker evaluating future production;
- commissioner evaluating contract, remuneration and Leopold's expected continued labour.

The difference is itself evidence of managerial valuation.

## 7. The current yield ontology

The register now distinguishes at least these numerical objects:

- default/negotiated model-output target;
- minimum feasible model output;
- seed-packet species;
- completed local field/study work;
- coloured-drawing species;
- recent drawing working set;
- useful field-selected set;
- local interim completed set;
- local final completed set;
- cumulative drawn/studied references;
- projected departure yield;
- counterfactual long-duration yield;
- maker-estimated years of work;
- commissioner-estimated years of work.

This is already enough to make a single `1892 species total` analytically dangerous.

---

## 8. Why mismatching numbers are useful

Three discrepancy types are now distinguishable.

### Temporal progression

San Diego 25 → 36.

No contradiction. The first is `so far`, the second near/final departure count.

### Actor-specific valuation

Three years → four years.

No need to choose one. Rudolf and Goodale are performing different forecasts.

### Ontology/reporting gap

Jamaica 106 ↔ 116.

Still unresolved because the nouns differ: finished work with species versus coloured drawings of species.

This typology should be carried into every later quantitative audit.

---

## 9. Paper-level consequence

The numerical history now supports the larger argument directly.

A potential plant is repeatedly converted:

`encountered`
→ `selected`
→ `drawn/studied/preserved`
→ `counted as local or cumulative work`
→ `filtered institutionally`
→ `stored as future material`
→ `valued as years of work`
→ `possibly reactivated as model`.

At each stage a new count can be produced.

So numerical inconsistency is not noise around the transport history. It is one way the transport/reference system becomes visible.
