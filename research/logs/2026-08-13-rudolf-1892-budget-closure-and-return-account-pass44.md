# Rudolf 1892 budget closure and return-account pass 44 — 13 August 2026

## Purpose

Pass 35 recovered the April running account but left the later accounting sequence open. A full re-read of the early-1892 Harvard/Ware correspondence in the active OCR master now supplies several later checkpoints, culminating in Goodale's 16 June letter with an enclosed summary for Rudolf's return. The expedition's finances can therefore be followed as an administrative process rather than from a single April balance.

## Result

The budget sequence now has at least four directly documented states:

1. **16 April** — Goodale sends a statement of account and a California expense estimate. From an initial $1,000 he reports $572.62 paid out and $427.38 remaining. He plans an additional transfer to Ganong, but the transfer amount remains OCR-corrupt. The estimate includes jars and a $3-per-day boarding assumption, with private-family boarding intended to reduce costs.
2. **27 April** — Goodale tells the Wares that there is more than enough money on hand for present expenses and, from Ganong's reports, that the travellers are proceeding economically. He explicitly says he has had no worry about the funds needed for the enterprise.
3. **late May** — when Rudolf's proposed/altered plans increase expenditure, Goodale again says he has ample money for their needs and does not expect an emergency telegraph for funds. The exact wording is OCR-poor, but the financial function is clear: route/planning change is being monitored against available cash.
4. **16 June** — Goodale says he has made final arrangements for Rudolf's return and has included the estimated cost in an enclosed `Summary`. He explains that expenses have been considerably greater than his original estimate because Rudolf had to be given a companion on his journeys. He nevertheless reports a remaining sum OCR-read as about **$149** for further expenditure, with possibly a little more depending on how much of a separately reserved `hundred` Rudolf uses for return-related costs. The parenthetical cost categories are OCR-corrupt but appear to concern fares/baggage/freight; retain them unresolved until image verification.

This closes a major missing mechanism from pass 35: Cambridge did not simply issue a travel fund and wait. It repeatedly re-estimated the trip as route, companionship and return arrangements changed.

## 1. April balance was a checkpoint, not a fixed budget ceiling

`BLA-D00041 / 015:41`, 16 April 1892, remains the cleanest arithmetic source:

- initial fund: $1,000;
- paid out to date: $572.62;
- remaining: $427.38.

The letter also says Goodale will hand Ganong an additional amount and later send the balance. The OCR phrase for the additional transfer resembles `the ... two hundred dollars`, but the compact master preserves only a representative OCR rendering and no image; publication use must keep the exact amount unresolved.

The important correction is conceptual. The $427.38 should not be treated as the amount available for the rest of the trip in a static sense. Goodale is actively moving cash forward to Ganong while retaining a balance in Cambridge and adjusting expected costs.

---

## 2. 27 April: expenditure is compared with field reports

`BLA-D00051–52 / 015:51–52` provides the first follow-up after the California estimate. Goodale answers a Ware inquiry by saying that they have more than enough money on hand for present expenses and that, from what Ganong writes, the travellers are getting along very economically. He adds that he has not had a moment's worry about the funds needed for the enterprise.

This establishes a feedback relation:

`field expenditure/report → Cambridge assessment of sufficiency → no new intervention needed`.

Money is therefore governed through correspondence just as collection priorities and route decisions are.

---

## 3. Route change produces an accounting problem

A late-May Goodale letter, `BLA-D00071–72 / 015:71–72`, is damaged in OCR but still preserves the operative sequence. Goodale forwards/explains a traveller letter, says he has plenty of money for their needs, and comments that Rudolf's changed views/plans increase expenses. He also telegraphs the party at Colorado Springs with a local institutional contact.

The exact changed plan should not be reconstructed from this OCR fragment alone. Its evidential value is narrower: **financial sufficiency is reassessed when the itinerary changes**.

This belongs beside the separately documented Darlingtonia/Shasta proposal, where a marginal four-day/$70 diversion could be rejected. The project now has both target-level cost judgment and trip-level budget monitoring.

---

## 4. 16 June: return itself becomes a budgeted stage

`BLA-D00079–80 / 015:79–80` is the strongest new source. Goodale says he has made `final arrangements` for Rudolf's return, that Rudolf will go to New York with Ganong, and that the estimated cost has been included in an enclosed `Summary`.

He then explicitly compares actual expenditure with the original model of the trip. Expenses have been `considerably more` than he had estimated on the basis of Rudolf travelling alone, because a companion had to be provided on his journeys.

This produces a causal cost category:

`need for companion → higher travel cost than original solo estimate`.

The companion was therefore simultaneously an epistemic/logistical actor and a budget variable. Ganong's role in field collection, introductions, navigation and communication had an accounting consequence visible to Cambridge.

Goodale nevertheless says they still have about `$149` on hand for further expenditure, plus perhaps a little more depending on use of another hundred reserved for return-related costs. The cents/terminal digits and the parenthetical wording are not secure enough for quotation from the compact OCR.

---

## 5. Arrival did not close the budget immediately

The June 16 source is important because the expedition is already effectively ending, yet money remains assigned to movement and settlement. The budget has a final stage for return travel and freight-related costs.

This parallels the finished-model shipment argument developed elsewhere in the project:

- a model can physically arrive before customs, unpacking and settlement are complete;
- a traveller can finish fieldwork before return fares, baggage/freight and final accounting are complete.

Add a **return/settlement clock** to the expedition side of the model.

---

## 6. Budget and yield can now be kept analytically separate

The archive prevents a simple efficiency calculation. By June Goodale says expenses exceeded his first estimate, but he also emphasizes the large amount of material obtained, the repaired models and Rudolf's increased understanding/interest in the work. Elsewhere he estimates years of future workshop material.

The sources therefore do not support a crude `dollars per species/model` metric. They support a historical accounting question:

> what kinds of work became chargeable when the expedition's actual operating conditions diverged from its planned form?

Directly visible categories now include:

- passage/travel;
- companion labour/travel;
- lodging/subsistence;
- jars and preservation equipment;
- optional route diversions;
- return travel;
- likely baggage/freight or related movement costs;
- institutional facilities authorized as needed.

These categories should remain attached to their documentary contexts rather than collapsed into one `trip cost` figure.

---

## 7. Revised governance sequence

The financial lane can now be represented as:

`Ware funding / authorization`
→ `Goodale initial fund and regional estimate`
→ `cash transferred in stages to Ganong`
→ `field reports on progress/economy`
→ `route changes and exceptional costs`
→ `Cambridge reassessment of sufficiency`
→ `final return estimate / enclosed summary`
→ `remaining reserve for return and settlement`.

This is a stronger first-order mechanism than `Harvard financed the expedition`. It shows **rolling financial governance under uncertain field conditions**.

## 8. Source-critical guards

1. Keep the April additional transfer amount unresolved until an image or alternate transcription is recovered; the representative OCR strongly suggests but does not securely prove `$200`.
2. The author/name attached to the California estimate in `BLA-D00041` remains unresolved.
3. The June 16 remaining amount is safely usable only as approximately `$149`; terminal digits/cents are OCR-corrupt.
4. The phrase after the separately reserved `hundred` appears to refer to return movement costs such as fares/baggage/freight but should not be normalized for quotation without image inspection.
5. Do not infer that the enclosed April account or June `Summary` itself survives merely because Goodale says it was enclosed. No separate securely identified account sheet has yet been recovered in the compact OCR master.
6. Do not calculate total expedition cost by subtracting the June reserve from the April or January fund without proving all intervening transfers, reimbursements and reserved sums.
7. `More expensive than estimated` is directly tied by Goodale to the need for a companion, but other cost changes may also have occurred.

## Working conclusion

The expedition's finances were continuously recalculated. Goodale moved money outward in stages, read field reports for signs of economy, absorbed the extra cost of companionship, and ended by budgeting the return itself. **Financial administration was another relay system: money, estimates and reports moved back and forth so that botanical work could continue under conditions the original itinerary had not fully predicted.**
