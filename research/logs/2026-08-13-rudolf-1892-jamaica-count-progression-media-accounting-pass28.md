# Rudolf 1892 Jamaica count progression / media accounting pass 28 — 13 August 2026

## Purpose

The 106/116 Jamaica discrepancy has remained open since the first route build. A targeted reread now adds an earlier quantitative statement that changes the problem substantially.

Early in the Hope Garden stay, Rudolf estimates that, judging from the plants blooming in March and his productivity in examining them, he should be able to assemble **approximately 100 species** if all goes well. On 27 March, immediately before leaving Jamaica, he says he has **finished his work with 106 species**. The Jamaican Botanical Department later reports **accurate coloured drawings of 116 species**, while Cameron made dried collections of the same and other plants.

The strongest result is therefore:

> **Jamaica did not produce one master total. It produced separate accounting surfaces for projected productivity, completed work, pictorial output and dried material.**

The 106/116 gap should remain open, but it is now much less plausible to treat 106 simply as a bad transcription of 116. The sequence `~100 forecast → 106 completed work` is internally coherent. The remaining jump to 116 occurs when actor and counted medium both change.

No public map changes are made.

---

## 1. Early March: Rudolf forecasts approximately 100 species

The Hope Garden sequence `BLA-D00705–00706 / 017:59–60` contains a forward-looking calculation.

Rudolf reasons from two variables:

- how many plants are flowering in March;
- his own productivity in examining them.

If things continue well, he thinks he can bring together a collection of **approximately 100 species**.

This number is therefore explicitly conditional and prospective. It is neither a count of all plants encountered nor a later institutional total.

The same letter moves immediately into other media. Rudolf sends example seeds, discusses species that might be grown indoors or outdoors, names plants whose seeds could be tried in Germany, and anticipates collecting many more seeds and bulbs.

So even at the moment he estimates `~100`, the Jamaica output is already branching into more than one ledger.

---

## 2. 27 March: 106 is a departure self-count

`BLA-D00725 / 017:79` is much firmer than the older secondary paraphrase suggested.

Before leaving Jamaica, Rudolf writes to his parents that he has **finished his work with 106 species**.

The count is retrospective and belongs to his own work at the end of the Jamaica phase.

Placed after the early forecast, the progression is plausible:

`~100 expected → 106 completed`.

That does not prove what exact operations are included under `work`. It does show that 106 sits naturally inside Rudolf's own productivity accounting.

This reduces the likelihood that `106` is simply a digit/OCR corruption of `116`, although publication-level certainty still requires the manuscript image.

---

## 3. The institutional report counts coloured drawings, not 'work' in general

The Jamaican Botanical Department's report uses a different formulation. It says that Blaschka made **accurate coloured drawings of 116 species**.

It then separately states that Robert Cameron made dried collections of the **same and other plants** for the herbarium.

That wording matters. The report does not give one undifferentiated expedition count.

It distinguishes at least:

- Blaschka's coloured drawings;
- Cameron's dried specimens, whose scope is explicitly broader than exact one-to-one correspondence.

The 116 therefore has a media-specific institutional form: pictorial output.

Until the original report and late-March drawing chronology are joined more tightly, `116 coloured-drawing species` should not overwrite Rudolf's `106 species of completed work`.

---

## 4. Four Jamaica ledgers are already visible

The archive now supports at least four different counting surfaces.

### Productivity forecast

`approximately 100 species`

Rudolf, early March, based on flowering availability and examining rate.

### Completed-work self-count

`106 species`

Rudolf, 27 March, before departure.

### Coloured-drawing report

`116 species`

Jamaican Botanical Department, retrospective institutional account.

### Dried collection

`same and other plants`

Cameron's herbarium material, with no single numeric total recovered in the current report summary.

Seeds and bulbs create another material lane again, but should stay outside these totals unless a source explicitly includes them.

---

## 5. The discrepancy is now analytically useful

The temptation with historical quantitative data is to decide which number is correct.

Here the better question is:

**What operation made each number countable?**

The answer differs.

The ~100 estimate turns flowering phenology and Rudolf's work rate into a forecast. The 106 turns completed study into a departure self-assessment. The 116 turns coloured drawings into institutional output. Cameron's dried collection creates a material corpus whose boundary is wider again.

The mismatch therefore exposes conversions between media and accounting practices.

This is exactly the mechanism that pass 27's yield ontology was designed to preserve.

---

## 6. A likely explanation remains possible but unproven

One obvious possibility is that additional drawings were completed after Rudolf's 27 March self-count or were counted differently in the later Jamaican report.

The present source set does not yet demonstrate that sequence directly.

Another possibility is that Rudolf's phrase `finished my work with 106 species` excludes some pictorial records that the institutional report includes. Again, plausible, not closed.

The correct status is therefore:

**media/accounting discrepancy, unresolved at the final ten-species step.**

This is more precise than `count conflict` and safer than choosing one total.

---

## 7. Paper-level consequence

Jamaica can now serve as a compact methodological demonstration.

A single field season generates several apparently incompatible numbers because the work is being converted into different media and evaluated by different actors.

That gives the paper a concrete argument against flattening archival data into a single expedition yield:

> **The numerical record changes when the object of accounting changes. The same botanical work could be projected as future species, closed as completed study, reported as coloured drawings, preserved as a broader dried collection, and circulated separately as seeds or bulbs.**

This is a stronger use of the 106/116 problem than trying to eliminate it.
