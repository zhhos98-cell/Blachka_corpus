# M. Otto W. Möller Hamburg forwarding pass — 10 August 2026

## Result

A re-read of the workshop business OCR shows that the Hamburg intermediary previously attached to the Liverpool case was used for at least two Damon-mediated British consignments in 1887.

### Marlborough College

The 11 October 1887 workshop letter to Damon says that a case marked `I.B. 266`, containing the Marlborough College models, had been sent on 8 October. The case was insured for 200 Marks and sent to `M. Otto W. Moller` in Hamburg for onward forwarding to Rev. T. A. Preston. The same passage gives an invoice of 196 Marks and carries the remaining Damon prepayment forward as credit.

This document is unusually dense because it joins case mark, destination, insurance, forwarder, consignee, invoice and dealer account in one source.

### Liverpool Museum

A 23 November letter says that the 247 Marks already paid for Liverpool were the models-only value and asks Damon to add about 50 Marks for packing. The subsequent dispatch letter records case `I.B. 268`, sent by freight to `M. O. W. Moller` for immediate onward dispatch to Higgins in Liverpool. It states a 268-Mark invoice and continues Damon's residual credit into the Galway and Cardiff orders.

The insurance figure in the Liverpool OCR is too ambiguous to normalize safely and remains unresolved. Likewise, the exact Higgins title/name should be checked against the manuscript image and Liverpool-side documentation.

## Interpretation guard

Sequential case marks do not license reconstruction of missing `I.B. 267`; no such case has been identified in the current source corpus. Möller's recurrence is sufficient to establish a repeat forwarding relationship, not to infer the total number of cases handled by the firm.

## Archive discovery

Exact-name public searches did not locate a secure M. Otto W. Möller company archive. The next step is identity resolution through Hamburg address books and commercial directories for 1887. Once legal firm name, occupation and address are established, Handelsregister, Chamber of Commerce and Staatsarchiv records can be targeted without conflating the actor with unrelated later Möller forwarding firms.

## Research significance

The British route now has the beginnings of a repeated logistics infrastructure analogous to the later Harvard/Bremen route. Damon organised the commercial account; Möller handled the Hamburg forwarding node; institutional recipients sat downstream. Insurance and case marks were attached to the movement before the museum received the models. The distribution history can therefore be written as a layered commercial chain rather than a generic shipment from Dresden to Britain.
