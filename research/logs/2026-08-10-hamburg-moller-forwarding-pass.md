# M. Otto W. Möller Hamburg forwarding pass — 10 August 2026

## Result

A broader re-read of the workshop business OCR changes the scale of this source cluster. M. Otto W. Möller was not merely the Hamburg intermediary for Marlborough and Liverpool in late 1887. The same forwarding node recurs across the subsequent Galway, Cardiff and Dublin Museum consignments, giving a British–Irish corridor that runs from at least 1887 into 1888.

## Chronology correction

One OCR date in the Galway/Cardiff block reads `28 Jan. 1886`. That reading is internally inconsistent with the transaction sequence. The December 1887 Liverpool dispatch carries Damon credit forward toward Galway and Cardiff; Liverpool is case `I.B. 268`; the next workshop record sends Galway and Cardiff as cases `269` and `270`; the Dublin Museum instalments then use `271–272` and later `275–276`. The working chronology therefore places Galway/Cardiff and the Dublin Museum instalments in 1888.

This is an explicit sequence-based reconstruction, not a silent correction of the manuscript. The year should be checked against the original images before publication.

## Late 1887: Marlborough and Liverpool

The 11 October 1887 workshop letter to Damon says that case `I.B. 266`, containing the Marlborough College models, had been sent on 8 October. The case was insured for 200 Marks and sent to `M. Otto W. Moller` in Hamburg for onward forwarding to Rev. T. A. Preston. The same passage gives an invoice of 196 Marks and carries the remaining Damon prepayment forward as credit.

The Liverpool sequence follows. A 23 November letter says that 247 Marks already paid were the models-only value and asks Damon to add about 50 Marks for packing. The subsequent dispatch records `I.B. 268`, sent by freight to `M. O. W. Moller` for Liverpool, with a 268-Mark invoice. The remaining Damon credit is explicitly carried forward toward Galway and Cardiff.

The Liverpool insurance figure is too ambiguous in OCR to normalize safely. The exact Higgins title/name also remains image-dependent.

## January 1888 working chronology: Galway and Cardiff

The next paired dispatch sends two large cases to Möller in Hamburg:

- `I.B. 269` to Prof. R. J. Anderson at the Natural History Museum / Queen's College Galway. Invoice: 288 Marks. A parenthetical 290-Mark figure is present but its documentary function is OCR-unclear and is not called insurance without image verification.
- OCR `L.B. 270` to Prof. Parker at the University Museum, Cardiff. Invoice: 197 Marks.

The two invoices total 485 Marks, exactly matching the 485 Marks prepaid, and the workshop describes the account as balanced.

This source is important because one dealer letter carries two institutions through one forwarder while maintaining case-level and invoice-level separation.

## 1888 working chronology: Dublin Museum instalments

A subsequent letter records the first Dublin Museum consignment through Möller in two cases, OCR `L.B. 271` and `272`. The models amount to 380 Marks; Damon had prepaid 264 Marks; the workshop carries 116 Marks as debit. It adds that many large objects made two cases necessary.

A later 28 July record again sends two Dublin Museum cases through the Hamburg forwarder, OCR approximately `U.B. 275` and `276`. The invoice is 900 Marks and total prepayment is also 900 Marks, so the account is described as perfectly balanced. Case-prefix OCR remains unresolved.

The order is therefore visible as an instalment structure, not merely as a museum-level acquisition.

## Dublin failure parallel

A separate mid-1887 Haddon record reads the Hamburg forwarding name as `M.Miller`; this may be Möller but is not promoted without image verification. The same source reports that a preceding shipment to W. J. Sollas at Trinity College Dublin was mistakenly landed at Bristol because of an error in the shipper's note, arrived about two weeks late, and contained broken models nos. 239 and 356. Fresh replacements were inserted into Haddon's subsequent insured case.

This gives a direct documentary chain:

`shipper's note → wrong port → delay → breakage → replacement in another institutional shipment`.

## Receiving-side archive targets

The forwarding reconstruction now creates precise receiving-side searches:

- University of Galway Archives: Bursar's Office records `UGA COL/BU`, 1870–1935, plus surviving College/Natural History Museum records, for Anderson, case 269 and the 288/290-Mark layers.
- UCD Archives: `IE UCDA RCSI`, Records of the Royal College of Science for Ireland, for the Haddon order.
- Trinity College Dublin Manuscripts & Archives: departmental/financial records for Sollas, the Bristol mislanding, damage and replacements.
- Cardiff University Special Collections and Archives: institution-level target only until an exact 1888 museum/natural-history series is isolated.

## Archive discovery for Möller

No secure M. Otto W. Möller company fonds has yet surfaced. The next step remains identity resolution through the Hamburg address books and commercial directories for 1887–1888. Once the legal firm name, occupation and address are fixed, Handelsregister, Chamber of Commerce and Staatsarchiv records can be targeted without conflating the actor with unrelated later Möller firms.

## Research significance

The source cluster now supports a stronger claim than repeat forwarding. Damon maintained rolling institutional accounts while Möller supplied a reusable Hamburg logistics node across multiple countries. Case marks, invoice values, prepayments and forwarding instructions moved together as a documentary system. The Sollas failure shows the reverse: an error in paperwork could alter the route and become material damage. The British–Irish distribution network can therefore be reconstructed as an administrative and logistical infrastructure, not a sequence of isolated museum purchases.
