# 2026-08-11 — Rudolf 1892 knowledge-production pass 05: Darlingtonia reference chain

Status: HIGH-VALUE OBJECT CHAIN. Taxon → drawing-record → Harvard model number is now closed at identifier level. Exact production/shipment date, surviving botanical specimen, and the Shasta route leg remain open. This pass follows `2026-08-11-rudolf-1892-knowledge-production-pass04.md` and keeps the public map unchanged until chronology is stronger.

## Why Darlingtonia matters

`Darlingtonia californica` is now the clearest test of the claim that Rudolf Blaschka's 1892 journey worked as a reference-production system rather than as a simple collecting itinerary. The chain contains a pre-existing commission desire, a costly route decision, remote communication with Cambridge, a cultivated institutional encounter, several forms of reference capture, a surviving preparatory-drawing record, and a named Harvard glass model. Crucially, the links do not require the plant's habitat, the place of observation, and the place of manufacture to coincide.

Operationally the chain can now be written:

`Goodale priority → costed route problem → telegram / remote decision → St Louis cultivated specimen → colour sketch + alcohol specimen → preparatory drawing record 133264 → Hosterwitz joint manufacture / finishing → Harvard model 444`

The chain is therefore closed at the level of **taxon and glass-model identifier**, but not yet at the level of **one uniquely dated source object proving that drawing 133264 directly generated model 444**. That distinction should remain explicit.

## The important epistemic result

The St Louis episode changes how the western itinerary should be interpreted. A plant whose natural habitat made it a four-day, $70 route problem could become epistemically usable somewhere else because a botanical garden supplied living material, identification, a workroom, and preservable access. The relevant geography is therefore not a single route from habitat to workshop. It is a set of substitutions between habitat, cultivated collection, correspondence, transport, preserved specimen, drawing, and glass object. This is exactly the kind of transformation that the current Operations/Flows map should eventually display.

The strongest formulation is not “Rudolf went to America to see Darlingtonia and then made it in glass.” The archive supports something more precise: Darlingtonia entered the expedition as a desired but logistically expensive target; its pursuit became a problem of time, money and telegraphic authority; a botanical garden later converted it into an observable and preservable specimen; the taxon survives in the preparatory-drawing archive; and Rudolf later identifies the Harvard glass object as Model 444. What remains to prove is the exact genealogical relation among the 1892 St Louis sketch, drawing 133264, and Model 444.

## Shasta remains a source-critical problem

The 18 May letter is explicit that the party had not yet decided whether to go to Shasta or Colorado Springs. The English translation of the 31 May letter can be read as saying Darlingtonia was the plant “because of which we travelled to the Shasta Mountains,” while the present railway reconstruction has withheld Shasta as a secure visited node. Published secondary accounts have tended to narrate a Shasta search. For this project the discrepancy is useful rather than inconvenient: the route should remain unresolved until the German originals around the 18 May and 31 May letters are compared directly. A cultivated St Louis encounter remains secure regardless of the outcome.

## A second result: fieldwork and Hosterwitz were temporally concurrent

The 7 Aug 1900 attribution letter contains a retrospective quotation from one of Leopold's letters written while Rudolf was away. OCR reads the date poorly as something like “March 30. 1892”; Rudolf translates Leopold as having written to Goodale that he was continuing work on plants “as per possibility” and that when his son returned “they can get finished” (`BLA-D01746`, `020:275`). The exact date and original letter still need recovery, so this is not yet a fully closed citation. Even in this guarded form it supplies an important research lead: the American trip and the Hosterwitz studio may have been operating simultaneously, with Leopold continuing the glass-work while Rudolf produced references abroad, and finishing explicitly deferred to Rudolf's return.

That would make the “distributed workshop” argument stronger. Distribution would describe not only Cameron, Ganong, botanical gardens, patrons and postal supply around the traveller, but also contemporaneous division of labour across the Atlantic. The field route and the workshop queue would be two synchronous parts of one production system.

## Contribution relative to the Corning narrative

The novelty threshold is now clearer. Existing scholarship already narrates the trip and connects field study to later glass flowers. The added value here is to reconstruct a source-critical, multi-medium provenance chain and to retain the frictions inside it: route choice, cost, telegraphy, cultivated substitution, preservation, archival date uncertainty, joint artisanal labour, and asynchronous production. Granularity is useful because it exposes transformations and substitutions that a narrative itinerary compresses. The paper-level object is the **reference chain**, not the travel diary by itself.
