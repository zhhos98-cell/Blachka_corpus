# Rudolf 1892 Tempe packet / selective conversion pass 19 — 13 August 2026

## Result

A targeted Harvard University Herbaria check changes the field-to-model problem in a useful way. The strongest new unit is no longer a single specimen/model pair. It is a **small dated collecting packet from Tempe in April 1892**.

The packet is presently a minimum reconstruction from independently indexed HUH records, not a claim of completeness. It already contains at least nine sheets representing eight taxa collected by **W. F. Ganong & R. Blaschka**, with day-level records running from 18 to 22 April 1892 and two additional April-only records.

The important finding is asymmetry. In the current shipment/model corpus, only one member of this packet has a secure later glass descendant: **Hoffmannseggia → model 433**. Several other retained herbarium references have no exact species-string model match yet. Conversely, *Abronia umbellata* has a direct field-observation statement and an early model but no Ganong-Blaschka herbarium sheet surfaced in the present search.

This makes the larger argument more precise:

> **The 1892 field trip did not feed the workshop through a one-specimen-to-one-model pipeline. Field encounters were converted selectively into different reference media, and only some of those references were later reactivated as glass.**

### Same genus, different model species

- field: *Lycium fremontii*; model list: model **343**, *Lycium halimifolium* / *Lycium vulgare*.
- field: *Eriogonum inflatum*; later model list: model **580**, *Eriogonum fasciculatum*, 12th shipment, January 1896.

These are **not** evidence that the field sheet somehow became those models. At present they do the opposite: they warn against genus-level matching.

### Secure metadata-level descendant

- field: **GH 01676890, *Hoffmannseggia glauca***;
- model: **433**, historical label *Hoffmanseggia densiflora (Hoffmanseggia stricta)*, updated *Hoffmannseggia glauca* [syn. *H. densiflora*];
- shipment: 9th shipment, January 1894.

So among this minimum packet, Hoffmannseggia is currently the only secure specimen/model branch.

This count is provisional because absence from the model list can be manufactured by historical synonymy, withdrawn models, unpublished studies or label changes. The right negative category is therefore **no exact model match yet**, not `no model existed`.

## 3. The packet weakens a shopping-list model of the expedition

The Tempe cluster is useful because date and locality are held relatively constant. Several botanical references were collected in the same short episode. Their later histories diverge.

One branch presently looks like:

`Tempe plant → herbarium sheet GH 01676890 → later workshop reactivation → model 433 → January 1894 shipment`.

Other branches currently stop at Harvard herbarium sheets, at least as far as the model list can show.

This makes the expedition legible as **reference accumulation** rather than simply procurement. A collected plant could remain useful as a taxonomic voucher, comparative object or institutional record without ever becoming a glass flower. Another could be reactivated months later in Hosterwitz. The historical problem shifts from `what did Rudolf bring back?` to `which references were later made operative, in what medium, and on what timetable?`

That is a much better fit with the split-workshop thesis.

## 4. Abronia now becomes a productive counter-case

Pass 14 established a different chain for **model 345, *Abronia umbellata***:

- Rudolf explicitly sees *Abronia umbellata* in the western landscape in late April 1892 (`BLA-D00797 / 017:151`);
- model 345 sits directly inside the seventh shipment, February 1893;
- the Corning finding aid lists a preparatory drawing `Abronia umbellata, 1892 or 1895`;
- the present HUH search has not surfaced a Ganong-Blaschka herbarium sheet for *Abronia umbellata*.

That negative is search-limited and should stay guarded. Analytically, however, the contrast is already useful.

Hoffmannseggia currently gives:

`field collection → surviving herbarium reference → delayed model descendant`.

Abronia currently gives:

`field encounter → possible drawing reference → rapid model descendant`, with no herbarium link yet found.

So the field-to-workshop return channel was plural even before we solve every image.

## 5. What the packet adds to the transport argument

The object of transport is becoming less singular.

A field plant could be converted into:

- a pressed specimen that travelled and then stopped moving physically in a herbarium;
- a drawing or observational record that remained portable as information;
- a later glass model that began an entirely new freight history;
- several of these at once.

The crucial historical operation is therefore **selective conversion**. Mobility depended on representational choice. The expedition did not have to return the plant itself for the workshop to continue working at a distance.

This also explains why a route-only map will flatten the evidence. The same Tempe interval generated descendants whose meaningful later movements do not share a single line.

---

## 6. Research safeguards

1. The packet is a **minimum indexed packet**, not a complete collector inventory.
2. Exact-string model negatives require historical-synonym checks before promotion.
3. Same-genus matches such as *Lycium* and *Eriogonum* should not be upgraded without species-level evidence.
4. GH 01676890/model 433 remains metadata-closed and image-open at the genealogy level.
5. The absence of an Abronia herbarium hit in the current search is a search result, not evidence of historical absence.

## 7. Next retrieval

The highest-value next step is now collector-level rather than species-level: obtain or derive the complete HUH result set for **W. F. Ganong & R. Blaschka in 1892**, then sort by date/locality and compare every taxon against Harvard model labels, Corning preparatory drawings and shipment lists.

If the Tempe packet grows substantially while model descendants remain sparse, the `selective conversion` claim gets much stronger. If many synonym-level model descendants appear, that result is equally useful because it will expose the nomenclatural and temporal work required to reconstruct the workshop's field-reference pipeline.
