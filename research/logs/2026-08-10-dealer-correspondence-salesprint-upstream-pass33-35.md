# Dealer correspondence reservoirs, sales print and upstream supply — passes 33–35

## Scope

This is an evidence-led extension after broad dealer-archive discovery was frozen. It does not reopen a general search for every Ward, Damon or Frič mention. The pass adds three source layers that the repository-level audit had left analytically separate: (1) customer-side correspondence and financial reservoirs, (2) dealer sales print, and (3) upstream collecting and preservation systems.

The governing rule remains strict: same dealer + same period does not equal a Blaschka transaction. Direct Blaschka status requires explicit model, catalogue, agency, order, payment, packing, shipment or receipt evidence.

## Pass 33 — correspondence reservoirs

The strongest new Damon lead is the Marianna Paulucci malacological correspondence in the Archivio di Stato di Firenze. Talenti, Innocenti, Lusvardi and Cianfanelli's 2024 inventory records 36 letters from Robert Damon, 1865–1873, in English, in ASFI-APXA box 51. The wider archive includes seller correspondence, invoices and dealer shell lists. Because the cluster predates the documented 1880 Blaschka agency, it is retained as ordinary Damon business evidence and a pre-agency control.

A second Damon document is unusually concrete: the Academy of Natural Sciences Conchological Section papers, Box 4 Folder 24, explicitly list a Damon invoice for British shells dated 1868. Adjacent files include cash books, ledgers, receipts and vouchers. This is potentially more useful than isolated autograph letters because it permits reconstruction of documentary form and receiving-side accounting.

Ward receives a same-period receiving-side bundle at the University of Missouri Archives, (UW:1/4/1), Box 7, FF 2G (1889). The official inventory names Ward's Natural Science Establishment correspondence concerning the elephant Emperor and other museum specimens, requests for payment and bills of lading. It is not a Blaschka file but is a close control for dealer-mediated object purchase plus freight paperwork.

The State Darwin Museum digital archive in Moscow also indexes correspondence from Robert F. Damon and from “V.Fric in Prag, Naturalien-Handlung” in the A. F. Kots papers. Public discovery metadata is too thin to impose dates or content. These remain uninspected dealer-network records and are useful because multiple natural-history suppliers coexist in one later museum-building archive.

## Pass 34 — dealer sales print

The major direct Blaschka addition is the Václav Frič catalogue cited by J. P. Short as a 43-page naturalia catalogue in the Natural History Museum London, Zoological Department, Letters January–June 1886, 29/139. Short reports that it included Blaschka holothurians, corals, medusae and sea anemones inside a much broader commodity range. The archival citation is precise but has not yet been cross-walked to a current item-level CalmView record, so the source is coded as a published archival locator pending direct inspection.

Ward's 1879 ANSP pamphlet remains the direct North-American comparator. Ward's Natural Science Bulletin (1881–1886) is retained as open dealer house print requiring page-level Blaschka verification.

For Damon, two pre-agency printed controls are now explicit: his 1857 catalogue of British and Irish shells and S. P. Woodward's 1859 `Generic catalogue of recent & fossil shells`, printed for R. Damon in Weymouth. The latter survives as a public-domain complete scan. These show printed classification and catalogue circulation as part of Damon's dealer practice before glass models entered the business.

Frič's four-page 1889 Paris exhibition guide is retained as a bibliographic sales-print witness from Grant Museum research until a standalone primary copy is isolated.

## Pass 35 — upstream supply

Ward's business is now given an explicit upstream collector layer. The Library of Congress William T. Hornaday Papers chronology records Hornaday joining Ward's Natural Science Establishment in 1873 and undertaking major zoological expeditions in 1874, 1876 and 1877–1879. A contemporary Nature review states that Ward sent him to India to obtain zoological desiderata, particularly skins and skeletons. The relevance is structural rather than Blaschka-specific: Ward actively generated material supply through field collecting and preservation.

Natural History Museum records give two compact Damon chains: Mollusca from Table Cape collected by R. M. Johnston and purchased from Damon; and American Silurian fossils sent by Paul Mohr through Damon. They show named collectors upstream of the dealer and an institutional museum downstream.

Frič offers the strongest packing comparator. Grant Museum research, citing Reiling and Spunarová, reports that Frič requested specimens from Enrique Stanko Vráz in West Africa and sent crates containing alcohol-filled glass containers for preservation and return to Prague. Surviving termite preparations retain multiple securing bands; the jars were originally protected against leakage with paraffin-wax paper and twine. This material is not connected to a Blaschka shipment. It is kept because it demonstrates a dealer already managing fragile containers, internal stabilisation, preservation media and long-distance transport in another commodity class.

## Analytical consequence

The archival unit “dealer” now separates into at least three infrastructures. Correspondence reservoirs show where the business survives when the company archive does not. Sales print shows how heterogeneous objects became named and orderable at distance. Upstream supply shows dealers constructing the stock they later distributed by commissioning collectors, routing material and supplying preservation/packing infrastructure.

This sharpens the comparison with Blaschka circulation. The research question is no longer only how Ward, Damon and Frič sold glass models. It can ask which commercial practices were inherited from ordinary natural-history trade and which had to change when a fragile manufactured representation entered the same routes.
