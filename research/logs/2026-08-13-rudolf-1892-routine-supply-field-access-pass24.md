# Rudolf 1892 routine supply / field-access gap pass 24 — 13 August 2026

## Purpose

The recent passes make 1892 look increasingly important because Rudolf selected plants in the field, assigned them to different media, assembled packets, and returned a reference stock expected to sustain years of work. That raises an obvious calibration problem. If fresh plants, seeds and roots were already moving between Harvard and Hosterwitz before 1892, then the journey cannot be described as the moment when the workshop first acquired living American reference material.

The pre-trip archive makes that correction directly.

By July 1889, provision of plants, seeds and books and the care of a Hosterwitz garden for American plants were written into the Harvard–Blaschka production arrangement. By September, Leopold and Rudolf described a functioning supply ecology based on seed cultivation, roots sent through Holland, purchased American plants, and cut flowers/leaves obtained locally or abroad. In December 1891, when they explain what the coming journey is for, they say Rudolf hopes to analyze and collect important American forms that had remained **hitherto inaccessible**.

The strongest formulation after this pass is therefore:

> **The 1892 journey did not create long-distance botanical reference supply. It targeted the access gap left by an already elaborate remote-supply system.**

The novelty lies increasingly in maker-controlled field selection and direct access to forms that seeds, roots, gardens, books and delegated fresh material had failed to make sufficiently available.

No public map changes are made.

---

## 1. Fresh-reference supply was contractual by July 1889

The July 10, 1889 Hosterwitz agreement/proposition is stronger than a retrospective statement about generosity. Alongside annual model consignments, it makes Harvard responsible for reasonable expenses connected with the **garden for American plants** during the growing season. It also provides that Harvard, through Goodale, supply **plants, seeds and books** needed for preparation of the collection.

The exact weekly garden-expense currency is OCR-corrupt and should be image-checked before quotation. The institutional obligation is clear.

This means that plant supply was part of the production infrastructure. Biological reference material sat alongside money, glassmaking labor and model consignments inside the arrangement.

The Glass Flowers project was therefore distributed from the start. Hosterwitz did not merely receive botanical information after Harvard had finished with it; Harvard was obliged to help feed the workshop with material from which models could be made.

---

## 2. September 1889: the Blaschkas describe the normal supply ecology

The 23 September 1889 letter (`BLA-D01178–01179 / 018:48–49`) is a particularly useful control because Leopold and Rudolf themselves explain how fresh material is supposed to reach their workbench.

For most plants that could grow outdoors, they planned to raise them from **seed in their own garden**. Some species were difficult to raise in Germany. Goodale had promised a large quantity of fresh seeds and, for selected difficult plants, **roots sent through Holland**.

They had also been purchasing roots and shrubs of American plants from botanical and other gardens. For hot-house plants and trees, they expected cut flowers and leaves to be obtainable locally or abroad.

This is already a heterogeneous reference system:

`seed → cultivation in Hosterwitz`

`root → transnational carriage → cultivation/study`

`purchased living plant → local/regional garden supply`

`cut flower/leaf → short-lived detached fresh reference`

`book → textual/visual reference`.

The 1892 media ecology therefore has a prehistory built directly into the project.

---

## 3. The journey's purpose is stated in December 1891 as residual inaccessibility

The key pre-trip source is `BLA-D00006 / 015:6`, 17 December 1891.

Leopold and Rudolf look forward to the younger Blaschka's coming American journey and say he hopes to analyze and collect those important forms of American plants **which had hitherto been inaccessible to them**.

That wording supplies the missing bridge between the 1889 supply ecology and the 1892 expedition.

The problem was not absence of any transatlantic reference channel. It was that the existing channels left a remainder.

Working term: **access gap** or **residual inaccessibility**.

The historical wording is `hitherto inaccessible`; the analytical terms simply name the mechanism.

---

## 4. 1892 should be read as an infrastructure intervention, not a beginning

The normal pre-1892 system could delegate much of the work of access:

- Goodale or another supplier could select seeds;
- roots could be routed through Holland;
- botanical gardens could sell living American plants;
- cut flowers and leaves could be obtained from other gardens;
- books could stabilize morphology and nomenclature at a distance.

The 1892 journey adds something different. Rudolf himself can decide, in the field, which plants are useful, which are showy or characteristic, which are too small to bother with, which are too dangerous to collect, which should be drawn immediately, and which can survive as living material.

So the sharp distinction is increasingly:

**delegated access → maker-present access**.

That does not mean every useful 1892 reference required direct observation. It means that direct travel moved part of the selection operation upstream, into Rudolf's own encounter with the plant and its environment.

---

## 5. The trip expands rather than replaces remote supply

Passes 21 and 22 showed several 1892 reference forms: drawings, freshness holders, herbarium sheets, living cacti, bulbs, preserved specimens and noncollection. The pre-trip controls now show why these should not be narrated as a wholly new system.

The field trip inserts new channels into an existing ecology:

### Routine channels already operating

- Hosterwitz garden cultivation;
- Harvard-supplied seeds;
- transnational roots;
- botanical-garden purchases;
- detached flowers and leaves;
- botanical books.

### 1892 field-added channels

- maker's direct observation;
- immediate drawing from living plants;
- collection with Ganong and local actors;
- field-provenanced herbarium specimens;
- live plants selected by Rudolf himself;
- mixed-origin packets assembled during travel;
- explicit maker judgments about representational usefulness.

The workshop after 1892 can use both systems. Travel does not supersede the garden or the postal/reference network.

---

## 6. Why this improves the article's novelty claim

A weak narrative would be:

`Blaschkas lacked American plants → Rudolf went to America → he brought living references home → better models became possible`.

The archive supports something more exact.

Before Rudolf travelled, Harvard and Hosterwitz had already constructed a distributed mechanism for overcoming distance. It included money for cultivation, seeds, roots, gardens, books and fresh detached material. Yet in 1891 the makers themselves still described some important American plant forms as inaccessible.

The journey can therefore be treated as a **diagnostic intervention into the limits of remote access**.

It asks, in practice: what remained difficult to know or reproduce when a maker depended on others to select, ship, cultivate or describe the plant?

1892 matters because the archive lets us compare the normal distributed system with an exceptional attempt to close some of its access gaps.

---

## 7. This also changes the meaning of 'fieldwork'

The strongest contrast is no longer field versus workshop.

The Hosterwitz garden was already an observational environment. A seed shipped from America could become a living plant in Saxony. A root routed through Holland could later produce fresh material. A hot-house flower obtained closer to Dresden could stand in for an American plant without crossing the Atlantic itself.

Field travel therefore becomes one modality among several for bringing relevant plant states within reach of the makers.

Its special property is **selection under maker presence**.

That is a much narrower and more defensible claim than saying authentic knowledge required being there.

---

## 8. Relation to temporal stock

Pass 23 showed Goodale converting the returned 1892 material into projected years of future work. The 1889 agreement shows that this temporal extension was already structurally anticipated by the project's cultivation system.

The garden turns transport into delayed availability:

`seed/root arrives → plant grows → useful state appears later → model work becomes possible`.

Pass 22's live cactus packet is therefore an intensified field-generated instance of an older production principle.

The historical change in 1892 is less `transporting life for the first time` than **who selected the living reference, where, and after what kind of encounter**.

---

## 9. Current strongest formulation

After this control pass, the field side of the article can be made more precise:

> **Harvard and Hosterwitz already operated a transnational reference-supply system based on seeds, roots, cultivation, garden purchases and books. Rudolf's 1892 journey addressed the residual forms that this system had left inaccessible. Its distinctive contribution was to relocate part of reference selection into the maker's own field encounter, where he could decide what to draw, preserve, transport alive or leave behind.**

This formulation protects the project from overstating fieldwork while also making the journey historically necessary.
