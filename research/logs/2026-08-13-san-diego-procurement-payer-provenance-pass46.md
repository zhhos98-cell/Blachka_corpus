# San Diego procurement, payer provenance and mixed reference packet — pass 46 — 13 August 2026

## Purpose

Pass 22 reconstructed the May 1892 live cactus/bulb packet as a mixed-origin packet moving San Diego → Cambridge → intended Hosterwitz relay. Pass 35/44 reconstructed Harvard's rolling trip budget. A close re-read of the San Diego letters adds a different financial layer: **the references inside one packet did not all enter the expedition through the same procurement or payment regime.** Field collection, companion gift/exchange, commercial purchase and institutional/patron funding coexist in the same operational sequence.

## Result

Three adjacent sources are especially strong:

- `BLA-D00802 / 017:156`: Rudolf describes Mr. Orcutt (OCR/translation first renders `Orchutt`) as a seed and plant merchant from whom they had previously requested a catalogue. Orcutt offers to sell plants, bulbs/corms and seeds and to arrange excursions to the plants' places of origin. Ganong immediately goes with him to the mesa while Rudolf stays behind to work.
- `BLA-D00803 / 017:157`: Rudolf says Ganong helps with alcohol preservation and herbarium work and **gives him the seeds he collects**. Rudolf has personally collected Arizona cacti with him in San Diego and intends to **buy** important bulbs from Orcutt so that these materials can be sent together. He then distinguishes the Wares' payment for the trip/hotels from smaller additional expenses and says it is useful that he has some spending money. One translated phrase renders the purchasing account as `our budget for the pharmaceuticals`; that category is evidently unstable and must not be normalized without the German manuscript image.
- `BLA-D00814 / 017:168`, 4 May: San Diego botanists, especially Orcutt, were generous in procuring material; Rudolf reports 36 species completed. A **small box of cacti and flowering bulbs**, with other things, is sent that day to Cambridge for intended onward forwarding by Goodale. Rudolf says he personally collected a large part of the cacti.

The packet reconstructed in pass 22 can therefore be upgraded from mixed geographical provenance to **mixed procurement and payer provenance**.

---

## 1. One reference packet contained multiple acquisition modes

The San Diego sequence distinguishes at least four routes into the working stock:

### A. Maker self-collection

Rudolf personally collected Arizona cacti and carried them to San Diego. He later says that a large part of the cacti in the dispatched box had been collected by himself.

### B. Companion contribution

Ganong collects seeds and, according to Rudolf, gives them to him freely while also performing preservation/herbarium labour. This is not simply paid assistant labour in the narrow sense. Material can enter Rudolf's reference stock through interpersonal contribution inside the expedition team.

### C. Commercial purchase

Orcutt is explicitly a seed and plant merchant and offers stock for sale. Rudolf says he wants to buy important bulbs from him. Later, while back in Cambridge, Rudolf reports receiving a large *Crinum* bulb and an earlier seed packet from Orcutt (`BLA-D00927 / 017:282`) and plans a larger future arrangement for seeds/bulbs whose flowering season the expedition could not wait for.

### D. Patron-funded travel infrastructure / small discretionary expenses

Rudolf says the Wares paid for the trip and hotel expenses but separates other small additional costs that accumulate. He explicitly values having a little spending money for purchases of the Orcutt type. The exact account/category from which these purchases were supposed to come is unresolved because the translation/OCR phrase `budget for the pharmaceuticals` is unsafe.

The resulting object is not economically homogeneous even if it travels in one box.

---

## 2. Packet aggregation erased procurement history unless reconstructed

The 4 May dispatch is materially singular: `a small box of cacti and some flowering bulbs`, plus other things. Yet its contents had different prior histories:

`self-collected field cactus`
+ `material procured through San Diego botanists`
+ `purchased bulbs`
+ potentially `companion-collected/gifted seed material`
→ `one dispatch packet`.

Pass 22 already showed that geographical origin was heterogeneous (Jamaica, Arizona, San Diego). Pass 46 adds another dimension: **acquisition mode**.

A shipment ledger that records only `one box from San Diego` would therefore compress at least three distinct kinds of ownership/labour relation into one logistical unit.

Working term: **procurement provenance** — the route by which a reference entered the travelling/workshop stock before its physical shipment.

---

## 3. Commercial purchase and field access were coupled at Orcutt's node

Orcutt's role is especially useful because it crosses the false distinction between `shop` and `field`.

He can:

- sell plants/bulbs/seeds;
- arrange excursions;
- take Ganong to source localities;
- later send seeds and a large *Crinum* bulb to Cambridge;
- potentially become a future remote supplier for plants whose flowering period Rudolf could not wait for.

The same actor therefore connects:

`commercial stock`
↔ `field locality access`
↔ `post-expedition deferred supply`.

This resembles the Southwick nursery ecology in passes 36–43 but occurs inside the journey itself. Commercial horticulture does not sit outside field science; it can redirect where field labour occurs and continue supplying references after the traveller leaves.

---

## 4. Small money mattered at the acquisition margin

Pass 35/44 documents Harvard/Ware governance at the scale of $1,000 funds, regional estimates, companion costs and return arrangements. `BLA-D00803` operates at another scale. Rudolf distinguishes major patron-paid trip/hotel costs from `little extra expenses` and links small available spending money directly to buying bulbs.

This suggests a two-scale financial architecture:

### Macro-budget

- passage/travel;
- hotels/boarding;
- companion costs;
- major route/facility decisions;
- return/settlement.

### Micro-discretion

- opportunistic plant/bulb acquisition;
- small incidental expenses encountered locally;
- purchases made without waiting for a fresh Cambridge authorization.

The sources currently do not establish a formal petty-cash account. Use `micro-discretion` analytically, not as a historical accounting label.

The important mechanism is that **a locally available reference could require immediately spendable money even inside a fully funded expedition**.

---

## 5. Payer, purchaser and beneficiary are distinct roles

The San Diego material also cautions against collapsing `funded by Ware` into `owned/bought by Ware`.

For any reference item, distinguish where possible:

- `collector` — who physically obtained it in the field;
- `procurement intermediary` — who located/supplied it;
- `vendor` — who sold it;
- `purchaser` — who made the immediate buying decision/payment;
- `funding source` — whose funds ultimately covered the cost;
- `recipient/cache` — where it was first sent;
- `intended workshop beneficiary` — Hosterwitz/Blaschkas;
- `institutional commissioner` — Harvard/Ware collection system.

The current documents securely identify some but not all of these layers for the Orcutt bulbs. The exact ultimate funding source of the small purchase is therefore intentionally left open.

---

## 6. Deferred supply turns a field contact into infrastructure

`BLA-D00927 / 017:282`, after Rudolf has returned to Cambridge, shows the San Diego relationship continuing. He receives a large *Crinum* bulb from Orcutt after an earlier seed packet and proposes arranging for many more seeds and bulbs from plants whose flowering time the travelling party could not wait for.

This upgrades Orcutt from one-time local helper to a possible **post-field supply node**:

`field encounter/contact`
→ `initial procurement/dispatch`
→ `later seed packet`
→ `large bulb received in Cambridge`
→ `planned recurring supply for missed phenologies`.

The journey thus creates or activates supplier relationships that can outlive the journey.

---

## 7. Article consequence

The reference-production system should not be drawn as if every field object were simply `collected`. At least in San Diego, `field reference` is an umbrella category covering self-collection, collaborator contribution, commercial purchase and supplier-mediated access.

A concise formulation:

> **Before references were packed together, they had already passed through different economies. A cactus dug by Rudolf, a seed given by Ganong and a bulb bought from Orcutt could become neighbours in the same box.**

A more technical version:

> **Packet aggregation converted heterogeneous procurement relations into a common transport unit. Reconstructing transport therefore requires recovering not only where contents came from, but how they entered the packet and whose resources made that entry possible.**

## 8. Source-critical guards

1. `Orchutt` in the first translated page is an OCR/translation variant; adjacent sources use `Orcutt`. Do not use the variant to create a second actor.
2. The phrase `budget for the pharmaceuticals` in `BLA-D00803` is clearly unstable translation/OCR. Do not infer a pharmacy-specific fund or quote the account name until the German image is transcribed.
3. Rudolf says the Wares paid for the whole trip and hotel costs, but this does not by itself identify the ultimate source of every small purchase.
4. `Ganong gives me the seeds he collects` proves interpersonal transfer in this letter; do not generalize all Ganong specimens/seeds as gifts.
5. `BLA-D00814` proves dispatch to Cambridge and intended onward forwarding; Hosterwitz receipt of this packet remains open unless another source closes it.
6. The *Crinum* and earlier seed packet in `BLA-D00927` are later Orcutt supplies; do not automatically equate them with the exact contents of the 4 May box.
7. Avoid assigning legal ownership to reference materials from the language of personal possession alone.

## Working conclusion

Pass 46 adds an economic provenance layer inside the packet. The travelling expedition was simultaneously a collecting party, a small purchaser, a recipient of collaborator contributions and a node in commercial botanical supply. **Transport unified materials physically only after acquisition had already differentiated them socially and financially.**
