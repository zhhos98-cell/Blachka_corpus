# 2026-08-09 — Australia deficit: University of Sydney current-presence / ownership guard

Corning's 2016 country chart gives **3 current Australian collections**. The holder table currently has two secure Australian ownership nodes: Australian Museum and Museums Victoria. University of Sydney therefore deserved a targeted check because the historical teaching-collection route was already known.

## New current/recent evidence

The University of Sydney's official July 2024 story for the Chau Chak Wing Museum exhibition **Micro:Macro: models of insight and inspiration** explicitly says that Leopold and Rudolf Blaschka's glass works were included in the exhibition. The exhibition ran from July 2024 to 29 June 2025. The University describes Micro:Macro as a major collection-based exhibition and as a survey of models used in University teaching.

This is strong evidence that genuine Blaschka objects were physically present at Chau Chak Wing Museum in 2024–25. It upgrades `CENSUS-0023` from a purely historical transfer lead to `current_presence_ownership_unresolved`.

## Why it is not yet HOLD-0067

The older provenance evidence remains a hard guard. Jan Brazier and Molly Duggins' *Visualising nature* reconstructs the Australian Museum's Frič purchase: three consignments in 1881–83 included Blaschka glass models, and in 1884 the teaching collection was **transferred on loan** to the University of Sydney after W. J. Stephens requested it for lectures. Some material remained at the Australian Museum. The source does not resolve which individual Blaschka objects moved to the University, which later returned, or whether title ever transferred.

The 2024 exhibition coverage likewise does not print a museum number, acquisition credit, lender, or collection-of-origin for the Blaschka objects. Because the project counts holder/ownership nodes and avoids turning display loans into duplicate collections, recent exhibition presence is insufficient to make University of Sydney the third independent Australian holder.

## Decisive next evidence

Any one of the following could close the node:

1. a Micro:Macro object checklist naming the Blaschka pieces and their lender/collection;
2. a Chau Chak Wing Museum collection record with `Artist/Maker = Blaschka`, museum number, and acquisition credit;
3. University teaching-collection or Macleay register entries showing title/custody of specific Blaschka models;
4. Australian Museum loan-return or transfer records showing that some 1884 Blaschka objects remained at the University or were formally transferred;
5. Jan Brazier's chapter **Trade Connections: The Acquisition of Blaschka Models of Marine Invertebrates by Museums in Australia and New Zealand**, if it itemizes the Sydney ownership outcome.

Until one of these appears, the Australian deficit remains **one unidentified 2016 current ownership node**, with University of Sydney now the strongest bounded candidate rather than a promoted holder.
