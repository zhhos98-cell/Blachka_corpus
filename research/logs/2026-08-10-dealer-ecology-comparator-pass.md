# Dealer ecology and commercial paperwork comparator pass — 10 August 2026

## Purpose

This pass deliberately moves beyond the three named Blaschka dealer routes without inventing additional Blaschka agents. Its purpose is comparative: identify archives in which Ward, Damon or Frič are preserved alongside other natural-history dealers, and identify same-period commercial records that expose ordinary dealer workflow. These controls are needed to distinguish Blaschka-specific distribution practices from generic nineteenth-century natural-history trade.

The canonical dealer archive register remains `sources/dealer-archive-register.json`. Comparative material is kept separately in `sources/dealer-comparator-register.json` so that non-Blaschka dealer evidence cannot silently inflate the Blaschka source corpus.

## Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv: controlled acquisition series

The Zoologische Staatssammlung acquisition files provide the strongest controlled comparator discovered so far. In the same archival sequence:

- ZSS 113 — V. Frič, Prague, 1864–1869 and 1883.
- ZSS 123 — Schlüter & Söhne, Halle, 1872–1873 and 1881–1885.
- ZSS 124 — Robert Damon, Weymouth, 1873.
- ZSS 127 — Ward's Museum of Mineralogy, Geology & Zoology, Rochester, 1875.
- ZSS 129 — John A. Spring, Tucson, 1876.
- ZSS 132 — Carl Baudisch, Trieste, 1877.

Only Frič, Damon and Ward are documented Blaschka dealer routes. The other files are controls. The archival advantage is that one receiving institution generated a comparable administrative record structure for competing suppliers. Inspection can therefore compare catalogues, offers, invoices, specimen lists, prices, receipts, exchanges, and other paperwork without assuming that the Blaschka transaction format was unique.

## Stadtarchiv Hildesheim: dealer ecology at one municipal zoological collection

Best. 741, `Zoologische Sammlung allgemein`, supplies a second controlled dealer ecology in the exact Blaschka marine-model period:

- Nr. 301 — V. Frič, Prague, 1880.
- Nr. 304 — H. Putze, Hamburg, zoological-anatomical teaching-aid establishment, 1880–1884.
- Nr. 306 — Hugo Schilling, Hamburg, natural-history business, 1878–1901.
- Nr. 305 — L. W. Schaufuss, Dresden, natural-history business, 1867–1882.
- Nr. 294 — Museum Godeffroy, Hamburg, 1878–1884.

The archive is especially useful because it puts a known Blaschka reseller beside businesses supplying natural specimens and anatomical teaching aids. The relevant question becomes how the museum administratively treated model suppliers relative to other natural-history vendors. No Hildesheim file is promoted to Blaschka evidence until its contents are inspected.

## Museum für Naturkunde Berlin: Frič and Umlauff in one written-record holding

The Museum für Naturkunde's official description of records holding `S` explicitly names correspondence with both `Naturalienhandel von Václav Frič (Prag)` and `Firma Umlauff`. The chronological overview places Zoologisches Museum records for 1810–1888 in `S001`. This makes S001 the first chronological target for Blaschka-era Frič correspondence, but the archive has not published an item-level Frič reference and no exact signature is imposed.

The holding therefore provides a third comparative configuration: a major museum receiving correspondence from multiple natural-history businesses. Access to the written-record collection is currently suspended during relocation, so this is retained as a future consultation target rather than a present retrieval claim.

## Damon: ordinary dealer workflow from the Ruskin letters

The Morgan Library's `MA 1406.1–18`, thirteen John Ruskin letters to Robert Damon from 1884–1885, is not Blaschka correspondence. It is nevertheless an unusually strong process control because the letters describe ordinary commercial actions in the same years as Damon's Blaschka agency.

Selected transaction sequence:

- `MA 1406.2`, 3 July 1884 — Ruskin selects minerals from Damon's shipment, returns the catalogue, and encloses a cheque for the items retained.
- `MA 1406.3`, 24 July 1884 — Ruskin asks Damon to send specimens on approval with other material.
- `MA 1406.5`, 3 August [1884–1885] — Ruskin requests Damon's catalogue and refers to agates kept, returned, and possibly reconsidered.
- `MA 1406.9`, 31 December 1885 — Ruskin refers to an enclosed list of minerals kept and offers to correct discrepancies.

This sequence gives a historically attested Damon process vocabulary: catalogue → shipment/on approval → selection → retain/return → cheque → list/reconciliation. The next step is to code Damon-mediated Blaschka transactions against these actions rather than assuming that prepayment, sample selection, returns, and account correction were peculiar to fragile glass.

## Ward: dealer-produced print infrastructure

Biodiversity Heritage Library preserves a public-domain run of `Ward's natural science bulletin`, vols. 1–3 (1881–1886), published by Ward's Natural Science Establishment and held by Smithsonian Libraries and Archives. The run is useful as dealer-produced primary print, not merely bibliography. It should be searched for the firm's commercial vocabulary, catalogue announcements, institutional customer language, product classes, freight/packing terms, and references to glass models.

No Blaschka hit is asserted from this pass because the BHL item-level OCR was not exhaustively searched here. By contrast, Academy of Natural Sciences archive item `ANSP-2012-060` (1879) is already direct Blaschka dealer evidence: the finding aid identifies the Ward pamphlet as advertising Blaschka glass models and Ward's sole American agency.

## Longer-term Ward paperwork comparators

The broader Ward business survives unusually well in receiving-side museum archives even after the Blaschka marine-model period. Smithsonian `RU000073`, Division of Mollusks Records, has a Ward's Natural Science Establishment file in Box 33 beginning in 1890. Smithsonian exposition records `RU000070`, Box 36 Folder 18, contain Ward's Natural Science Establishment material for 1892–1893. American Museum of Natural History Hovey papers preserve later Ward correspondence with estimates, account statements, invoices, postal receipts, and shipping-insurance certificates (for example Mss .H682 Box 4 Folders 206 and 204).

These later records are not evidence for nineteenth-century Blaschka orders. They are retained as longitudinal controls for the paperwork repertoire of the same commercial firm: estimate, account statement, invoice, postal receipt, and insurance certificate.

## Result

The dealer source problem now has two analytically distinct layers:

1. **Blaschka dealer archive:** direct and receiving-side evidence for Ward, Damon and Frič.
2. **Dealer ecology / process comparator:** same-series competitors and same-period business transactions used to identify what was ordinary commercial practice.

This distinction matters for the research argument. A feature should only be treated as a response to Blaschka glass fragility after comparison with ordinary dealer procedure. Catalogues, approval shipments, cheques, returns, and account reconciliation demonstrably belonged to broader natural-history trade. Packing design, breakage feedback, case construction, and model-specific risk may still prove more object-specific, but that must be demonstrated rather than assumed.
