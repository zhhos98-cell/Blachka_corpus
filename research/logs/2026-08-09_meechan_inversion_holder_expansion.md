# 2026-08-09 — Meechan inversion and holder expansion

This pass treats the mid-1990s Chris Meechan / National Museum Wales spreadsheet as a cross-institution occurrence index rather than merely a Cardiff condition file. Its 198 object rows contain a `Model Locations` field that repeatedly records comparable Blaschka holdings in other institutions. After extracting the location strings, three classes emerge: already normalized current holders; historical or merged teaching collections that require successor tracing; and previously omitted current/recent holders that can be promoted with independent recent evidence.

Three current/recent holders were added to `research/current_holders.csv`. **University of Glasgow — Hunterian Zoology Museum** is distinct from the municipal Glasgow Museums node: a recent item-level Hunterian record identifies a physical Blaschka model of *Limax albus var. flavomarginata*, inventory 2856799. **Leicester Museum & Art Gallery** has a photographed 2024 display explicitly showing a handful of Blaschka sea-anemone models; Meechan's comparative file independently places Leicester against thirteen model types. **Guernsey Museums and Galleries / Guernsey Museum at Candie** has 2022 first-hand evidence describing a collection of Blaschka sea-creature models, while the Meechan file repeatedly records Guernsey holdings around c.1885. Exact institutional totals for all three remain open.

The working file therefore now has **66 audit rows**: **64 secure or secure-recent current-holder rows**, **1 active recheck candidate** at Senckenberg Görlitz, and **1 historical exclusion** at Queen's Belfast. This remains an audit-set count, not a statement that 64 rows are independent additions to the 2017 Corning baseline.

Cardiff's count is now normalized. Julian Carter's 2025 NatSCA account states that Cardiff acquired 138 models directly in 1890 and another 62 from the Science Museum in 1927, for a historical acquisition total of 200, but only **145 survive in the collection today**. `HOLD-0021` now records 145 as the current survival count and preserves 200 only as the historical acquisition layer.

The Meechan file also generates a bounded London teaching-collection problem. **King's College London** appears against twenty-two model types, and the *Psolus phantopus* row contains the unusually explicit annotation `To Kings College. 937/056`. This makes King's the strongest current candidate for one of the still-unidentified Science Museum deaccession recipients, but it is retained in `census.csv` as a transfer candidate until the original Science Museum disposal/register entry and current KCL object survival can be closed. **Birkbeck College** appears against seven model types and enters the census as a comparative-location lead. **Bedford College** appears against five model types; Royal Holloway's current collection history states that the Bedford zoological and botanical museums were largely destroyed in the 1941 bombing, except for a handful of specimens. This creates a precise object-level survival test rather than a generic successor search.
