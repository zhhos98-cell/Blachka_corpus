## Main result

The Rakow item 87384 should no longer be treated simply as a travel landscape sketchbook. The filmed object is a portable working notebook containing at least five distinct kinds of work: landscape observation, botanical annotation, urban address/logistics notes, Atlantic distance arithmetic, and metric-English measurement conversion.

This materially strengthens the 'mobile workshop' interpretation because the same pocket object held observation and the practical calculations/addresses that allowed travel and work to proceed.

## Page 26 — Bremen urban working note

High-resolution review replaces the earlier provisional 'Zimmermann' reading.

Current diplomatic reading:

- `Chr. Baldewein & Sohn`
- `am Wall.`
- `Schüsselkorb 14.`
- `Lange,` / `Brinckmann Obere Straße`
- `Ecke Sögestraße.`
- `am Markt`

Historical controls identify Chr. Baldewein & Sohn at Am Wall 165 and Brinckmann & Lange at the Sögestraße/Obernstraße corner. The page itself appears to reverse the latter firm's normal name order. Modern address controls show that the written sequence is spatially coherent through Bremen Altstadt: Am Wall -> Schüsselkorb -> Sögestraße/Obernstraße -> Markt.

A strong but still inferential association is the documented Bremen layover on 1 February 1892. Rudolf's same-day letter says he was at the Central Hotel opposite the railway station, called on the shipping agents Ehrhorn, Emden & Mayer next door, and remained in Bremen until the next morning's special train to Bremerhaven. Page 26 may therefore record an intra-city errand/address route during this layover.

Guard: the businesses can be identified, but there is no evidence yet for what Rudolf bought, ordered, inspected, or discussed. Do not convert business type into purchase purpose.

## Page 27 — travel arithmetic

Secure text:

`Von New York bis Bremen sind 880 geographische Meilen`

The visible arithmetic is `3520 / 4 = 880`. This is more useful than forcing the result into a standardized modern geographical-mile conversion: Rudolf was explicitly transforming a source figure of 3520 by a 4:1 rule. The source unit and route convention behind 3520 remain unresolved.

## Page 28 — dimensional conversion

The heading is probably `Länge Cylinder` (medium-high confidence). The numerical lines are secure:

- `27 1/2 Centimeter`
- `11 Engl. Inches`

27.5 cm is approximately 10.83 English inches, explaining the rounded 11-inch note. The measured cylinder is unidentified. Do not associate it with a microscope, specimen jar, shipping container, model part, or other apparatus without an independent source.

## Digital-surrogate integrity

PDF pages 12 and 13 are not merely visually similar. They embed byte-identical JPEGs with the same dimensions, byte length, and SHA-256, although they are stored under separate PDF object references. Page 13 therefore contributes no independent image evidence.

The older 05B backend independently preserves the final 30 frames of an earlier 700-frame Corning/Rakow OCR batch. Its records 682 and 683 already point to the same extracted image filename and align with the present p12/p13 duplicate. The duplication therefore predates the user's current PDF download and belongs to an earlier delivery/processing chain.

This changes how the Tempe catalogue conflict should be treated. The catalogue says the beginning sketch is captioned Tempe, Arizona, 22 April 1892; the current PDF's earliest clearly legible landscape caption is Maricopa, 24 April. Since the surrogate demonstrably duplicates at least one frame, omission/reassembly of a Tempe frame remains plausible. The catalogue should not yet be treated as simply wrong.
