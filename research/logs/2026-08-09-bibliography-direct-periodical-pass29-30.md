# Bibliography expansion: direct periodical and institutional sources, passes 29–30

Date: 2026-08-09

## Scope guard

These passes continue the strict Blaschka-only bibliography. Each promoted item itself substantially concerns Leopold/Rudolf Blaschka, the Ware Collection, a named Blaschka marine-model collection, or direct exhibition/conservation history. General glass, model, museum, aquarium and natural-history literature remains excluded.

## Pass 29

Added three direct records after exact-title repository deduplication:

- Frances Richard, “Great Vitreous Tact: Thoughts (With Quotations) on The Ware Collection of Glass Flowers and Fruit, Peabody Museum, Harvard University,” *Cabinet* 6 (Spring 2002). The original Cabinet article is a substantial Blaschka/Ware Collection essay rather than a passing mention.
- “Blaschka at the Natural History Museum,” *The Glass Cone* 68 (Summer 2004), no author listed. The original Glass Society issue explicitly describes the Natural History Museum's then-170-model collection and its conservation initiative.
- “Eternal Creatures,” *Harvard Magazine*, January–February 2007; web publication 1 January 2007, no author listed. Direct report on the 29-model travelling exhibition *Glass Sea Treasures from Harvard: The Age of Darwin*.

## Pass 30

Added two more direct records:

- Meike Niepelt, “Physophora magnifica: Zwischen Kunst und Wissenschaft,” in the University of Tübingen exhibition *38 Dinge*, page state 12 June 2006. The object essay directly treats the 32-model Tübingen collection, the c.1885 Eimer purchase, Blaschka/Haeckel design relationship, scientific teaching function and later heritage status.
- Gunild Symes, ed., “Blaschka,” *X-Ray Mag* 30 (July 2009). Interview with Harvard Museum of Comparative Zoology director James Hanken on the marine-invertebrate Blaschka collection, rediscovery/conservation and exhibition. The current X-Ray Mag site identifies the article as issue 30; the magazine's historical issue index identifies no.30 as July 2009.

## Important holds

- Natural History Museum London, Katie Pavid, “Colour in the collections: Blaschka glass models”: direct and eligible in scope, but the current page does not expose a reliable publication date. Do not assign one from access dates or later citations.
- National Museums Scotland, “The glass artists who mastered museum models”: direct and eligible, but current page date remains unresolved.
- X-Ray Mag metadata is now sufficiently dated through its own historical issue index; no inferred year remains.
- *The Fragile Beauty of Harvard's Glass Flowers* (Journal of Antiques & Collectibles, 4 February 2004) is direct but explicitly reprints/excerpts Schultes and Davis; it remains lower-priority derivative reception rather than an automatic bibliography addition.

## Early chronology guard

Ludwig Reichenbach's bibliographic record securely confirms the titles “Marine-Aquarien mit Actinien oder Strahlblumenpolypen in naturgetreuen Modellen” (*Leopoldina*, 1863, 4. Heft) and “Glasmodelle lebender Schnecken” (*Allgemeine naturhistorische Zeitung*, 1864, p.231). Henri Reiling has also published a translation of a 1863 Reichenbach postscript explicitly naming Blaschka and arguing for the scientific utility of his models. However, the original 1863/1864 issue texts have still not been inspected at article level in this pass. The public bibliography therefore remains at 1870 rather than being pushed back to 1863 on indirect collation alone.
