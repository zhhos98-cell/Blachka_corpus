# Dealer archive closure audit — 10 August 2026

## Scope

This pass closes broad public-catalogue discovery for the three documented commercial dealer/distributor routes used for Blaschka marine-invertebrate models: Henry Augustus Ward / Ward's Natural Science Establishment, Robert Damon / Robert Ferris Damon, and Václav Frič / V. Frič. The working distinction is between (1) workshop-side business records, (2) dealer-side personal/corporate archives, (3) customer-side correspondence and acquisition files, and (4) museum receipt/accession records.

A museum that owns Blaschka objects is not counted as a dealer archive unless a named dealer-related correspondence, acquisition, account, catalogue, invoice, or business-record series can be identified. General correspondence with Ward, Damon, or Frič is not promoted to Blaschka evidence without item-level confirmation.

## Ward

University of Rochester remains the central dealer archive. Its collection-development statement calls Henry Augustus Ward a collection of record and lists the Henry Augustus Ward Papers, 1840–1933 (A.W23), together with the Ward's Natural Science Establishment Papers, 1876–1988 (D.231). Rochester's Ward Project further confirms that correspondence, records, catalogues, and surviving teaching specimens are preserved across the University.

Direct or near-direct Blaschka dealer witnesses already identified include the 1 June 1879 Ward pamphlet ANSP-2012-060 at the Academy of Natural Sciences, the Auckland Cheeseman–Ward correspondence, the Boston Society of Natural History second-invoice layer, and the 1893 Columbian Museum offer/contract referenced to A.W23. Receiving-side Ward archives now include Smithsonian RU000189 Box 139 Folders 1–3 (1879–1895), Smithsonian RU000208 Box 13 Folder 6 (1884 and later), the Haast/Ward correspondence at the Alexander Turnbull Library, Cornell Comstock correspondence, and BayHStA Zoologische Staatssammlung 127.

The discovery problem is therefore closed. The remaining Ward problem is item-level targeting inside A.W23/D.231: orders, invoices, customer letters, packing/shipping documents, and Blaschka-specific account rows.

## Damon

No dedicated Damon corporate fonds or order-book series surfaced in this broad public-catalogue pass. This is a negative discovery result, not proof of archival destruction.

The core Blaschka business archive remains the Rakow workshop side: business-letter drafts, Lieferungs-Buch, and Geschäfts Anmerkungen. These records already establish the British agency relationship and document institutional orders, prepayment, packing, insurance, breakage, and routing. Project provenance modules add unusually dense Damon-mediated chains at Jeypore/Jaipur and Liverpool.

The customer-side archive is now controlled more precisely. McGill's Robert Damon authority record identifies ten Damon-to-Dawson letters dated 25 October 1877; 26 April 1878; 15 January, 13 February, 3 March, and 9 April 1879; and 5 January, 22 February, 21 March, and 21 April 1881, plus one Dawson-to-Damon letter dated 7 February 1881. Verified examples include MG1022-2-1-117-0019, MG1022-2-1-135-0011, and MG1022-2-1-136-0004. A prior project misidentification is corrected here: MG1022-2-1-109-0007 (13 February 1877) is a C. A. White letter, not Damon.

Natural History Museum London records expose a wider Damon acquisition network in DF/ZOO/200, DF/PAL/100, and DF/PAL/105. BayHStA Zoologische Staatssammlung 124 is a dedicated 1873 Damon acquisition file. Morgan Library MA 1406.1–18 preserves thirteen Ruskin-to-Damon letters from 1884–1885; Te Papa MU000147/007/0017 preserves a Damon business letter dated 1 March 1889. These latter corpora provide dealer-practice context but are not Blaschka evidence by themselves.

The next Damon task is direct retrieval of the McGill letters and NHM files against the Rakow order chronology. Further broad discovery has low expected yield.

## Frič

No complete V. Frič corporate fonds surfaced. Strong dealer correspondence nevertheless survives in a distributed form.

Direct Blaschka routes already close through Rakow workshop correspondence, Australian Museum procurement files (1879–1883), the Prague four-model gift in 1884, and the Kremsmünster Frič–Eggerth provenance chain.

The major new archival lead is Museum für Naturkunde Berlin's written-records collection, holding identifier S. The museum explicitly lists correspondence with Václav Frič's natural-history trade in Prague; the collection also contains correspondence, catalogues, object lists, and administrative/collection documentation. Access is currently suspended from 1 July 2026 and is expected to resume in the fourth quarter of 2027.

Additional receiving-side dealer files include Stadtarchiv Hildesheim, StadtA HI Best. 741 Nr. 301, 'Zoologie: Erwerbungen von der Lehrmittel- und Naturalienhandlung V. Fric in Prag' (1880); BayHStA Zoologische Staatssammlung 113, 'Erwerbungen beim Naturalienhändler V. Fric (Prag)' (1864–1869, 1883); and the digitised Madrid letter ACN0364/007, Václav Frič to Ignacio Bolívar, 18 July 1885, explicitly catalogued under V. Fric. Lehrmittel und Naturalien-Handlung. The Madrid item concerns insects and remains general same-period dealer evidence.

Antonín Frič's personal fonds at the National Museum is kept strictly separate from the commercial papers of Václav Frič's firm. The later transfer of remaining company natural-history stock to the National Museum in 1959 is likewise not treated as proof that the firm's paper archive transferred with the stock.

## Cross-dealer comparator

The strongest comparative discovery of the pass is the Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv Zoologische Staatssammlung acquisition series. It contains three dealer-specific files in the same institutional record structure: 113 = V. Frič (1864–1869, 1883); 124 = Robert Damon (1873); 127 = Ward's Museum of Mineralogy, Geology & Zoology (1875). These files are not automatically Blaschka records. Their value is methodological: one receiving institution documented all three dealers, creating a controlled archive for comparing dealer/customer paperwork, acquisition language, prices, catalogues, receipts, and other administrative practices.

## Closure decision

Broad dealer-archive discovery is frozen after this pass. The canonical register is `sources/dealer-archive-register.json`, schema 2.0.0. Further additions require one of four triggers: a newly identified named archival unit or item; a digitised primary document; a published archival reference precise enough to retrieve; or an archivist/curator response.

The research phase should now move from repository discovery to document retrieval and cross-walking across workshop → dealer → customer → museum accession. Ward is a concentrated archive problem; Damon and Frič are distributed-archive reconstruction problems.
