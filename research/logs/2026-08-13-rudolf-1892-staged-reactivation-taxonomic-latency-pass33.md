# Rudolf 1892 staged reactivation + taxonomic latency pass 33 — 13 August 2026

## Result

Pass 33 changes the post-return part of the argument. The expedition did not come home and then become glass in one conversion event. Its references entered a **production stock** that was reactivated selectively across successive consignments. The surviving correspondence lets us watch that activation increase over time, while also showing that some references remained taxonomically unresolved after the field season.

The strongest formulation is:

> **The expedition returned as stock, not as a shipment plan. American references entered a workshop queue in which activation was batch-based, selective and sometimes delayed by taxonomic uncertainty.**

This adds a temporal mechanism to Gate B. Retention after Gate A did not imply immediate manufacture. A retained reference could wait, be identified later, enter a mixed consignment with unrelated work, or remain archival rather than becoming glass.

No public map change.

## 1. January 1893: only “a few” journey plants have entered glass production

Goodale's letter to Mrs and Miss Ware, 31 January 1893, is a compact but unusually important checkpoint. Forwarding a new letter from the Blaschkas, he tells them that they are to receive **“a few of the American plants collected on the journey, and a large group of Cryptogamia.”**

This belongs directly beside the annotated seventh-shipment sequence. The seventh shipment, February 1893, spans models 312–373 and includes the eleven models made by Leopold during the first half of 1892, a large cryptogamic/anatomical block, and some ordinary flowering-plant models. The Goodale note therefore makes the shipment's mixed genealogy explicit from the receiving side.

The important point is proportional and chronological, without pretending we have a denominator. Roughly seven months after Rudolf's return, the expedition appears in the next consignment only as **“a few”** American plants, while a large parallel program in Cryptogamia continues. The expedition did not displace the workshop's existing scientific agenda.

Use this as an early Gate-B checkpoint:

`1892 references returned → stored/selected/processed in Hosterwitz → only some activated into the Feb 1893 consignment → other workshop programs continue in parallel`.

Guard: “a few” is qualitative. Do not manufacture a numerical American share for shipment seven until individual model numbers are closed against field references.

## 2. July 1893: the journey becomes a quantified production block

The 18 July 1893 workshop letter gives the strongest batch-level bridge. The first portion of the current consignment contains **37 ordinary plant species**:

- 20 after Jamaica studies;
- 15 from the West;
- 2 after garden material, where the surviving OCR reads an ambiguous final character but the arithmetic `37 − 20 − 15 = 2` closes the count.

The annotated shipment list independently places 37 ordinary plant model numbers at the beginning of the eighth shipment, models 374–410, followed by a scientific/cryptogamic/anatomical block, models 411–423. Named anchors in the list align with the letter: Tribulus and Passiflora murucuja in the Jamaica portion; Rosa woodsii, Rosa gymnocarpa and Viola in the western portion.

This is a different production state from January. The expedition has moved from “a few” models within a mixed consignment to a large, explicitly quantified block. Yet the same shipment still contains a second portion devoted to anatomy, physiology and especially Cryptogams. Field-derived work and non-field scientific modelling therefore share one freight horizon without sharing one reference genealogy.

The shipment is doing aggregation work. It compresses different production histories into one transport unit.

## 3. The missing intermediate step: post-field taxonomic closure

The continuation of the 18 July letter, `BLA-D00169 / 015:170`, exposes a stage that a simple `field observation → glass` chain misses. The Blaschkas say that they have succeeded, **“though with scanty means,”** in finding the names of some plants that had remained undetermined, especially Jamaica plants. They identify the Jamaica-hills Passiflora as `murucuja` and Kingston's feared “Kill-buckra-weed” as a harmless `Tribulus`.

The western material shows an even more distributed arrangement. They say some western plants have **probably in the meantime been determined in your institution**, and then quote names they hope are correct, including a California Viola, `Rosa gymnocarpa`, and Missouri `Rosa woodsii`.

This creates an additional state between capture and manufacture:

`field encounter → drawing/specimen retained → taxon unresolved or provisionally named → identification work in Hosterwitz and/or Cambridge → model production / label stabilization`.

Call this **taxonomic latency**. The plant can already be materially useful as a drawing or specimen while its documentary identity remains unfinished. Identification itself may be distributed across the Atlantic and completed after field access has ended.

This matters because taxonomy here is not merely metadata added after the object exists. It participates in reactivation. A stored reference can wait for a sufficiently stable identity before entering a numbered model sequence or label regime.

Guard: the letter says Cambridge probably determined some western plants. It does not identify which Harvard worker performed each determination, and “probably” should remain visible.

## 4. December 1893: the expedition now dominates the next fifty-model batch

The 15 December 1893 letter gives a third checkpoint. The Blaschkas say the January consignment will be **50 species, exclusively flowers and their details**, with only a few stated exceptions made using material and studies from Jamaica and the West. The annotated list independently closes the ninth shipment as models **424–473 inclusive, exactly 50 models**; model 474 starts the August 1894 tenth shipment.

The letter names economic and western plants, including Anacardium, star-apple, Cedrela, Coca, Eucalyptus and western desert plants. The Blaschkas emphasize that some of these had never been available to them flowering in a European garden. By this stage the 1892 reference stock is no longer a marginal addition to the outgoing consignment. It supplies the principal reference base for a complete fifty-model shipment.

The sequence across 1893 is therefore historically observable:

`Jan 1893: a few American plants + large Cryptogamia`

`Jul/Aug 1893: 20 Jamaica + 15 West + 2 garden ordinary plants, alongside a separate scientific/cryptogamic block`

`Jan 1894: 50-model flower consignment overwhelmingly described as based on Jamaica/West material`

This should not be converted into a continuous conversion rate. It is a **staged-reactivation pattern** documented at three batch checkpoints.

## 5. Darlingtonia can now be closed one step further

Pass 05 established the Darlingtonia chain through model identifier 444 but left exact manufacture/shipment timing open:

`Goodale priority → Shasta proposal/cost → telegram says do not go → St Louis cultivated flowering specimen → colour sketch + alcohol specimen → preparatory drawing record → model 444`.

The 15 December 1893 workshop letter now supplies the missing contemporaneous production witness. In describing the January consignment, the Blaschkas state explicitly: **“We made also this time the curious Darlingtonia californica.”** The corrected ninth-shipment boundary is 424–473, and Rudolf's later attribution letter identifies **444. Darlingtonia**.

The event chain can therefore be upgraded to:

`pre-existing Harvard desire`
→ `18 May 1892 Shasta target and four-day/$70 access problem`
→ `Goodale telegram closes Shasta diversion`
→ `31 May St Louis garden provides plant in flower`
→ `colour sketch + alcohol specimen`
→ `reference survives/returns to Hosterwitz`
→ `15 Dec 1893 Blaschkas explicitly report Darlingtonia made for January consignment`
→ `Jan 1894 shipment 9, models 424–473`
→ `model 444 = Darlingtonia`.

This closes **production batch and shipment timing** much more securely than the 1900 retrospective identification alone. What still remains open is the one-to-one genealogy among the St Louis sketch, archival drawing identifier 133264 and the exact physical model 444.

Darlingtonia is now the strongest single example of **institutional substitution followed by delayed workshop reactivation**.

## 6. Gate B needs a queue, not a binary switch

Passes 19–32 treated Gate B as the decision by which retained field reference became glass. Pass 33 shows that Gate B is better represented as a queue with several possible states:

`retained reference`
→ `stored reference stock`
→ `taxonomic status unresolved / provisional / stabilized`
→ `production priority assigned`
→ `activated into current batch`
→ `manufactured / mounted`
→ `shipment assigned`
→ `dispatched`.

Parallel terminal/holding states remain possible:

- herbarium reference retained without identifiable glass descendant;
- drawing retained for later or uncertain use;
- living/preserved material exhausted or lost;
- reference superseded because the collection is already represented;
- reference postponed by workshop capacity, season, biological state or competing model programs.

A model's later appearance therefore cannot be read backward as evidence that its reference had priority immediately in 1892.

---

## 7. Two kinds of latency now need to be distinguished

The project already has **phenological latency**: the desired biological state may occur later, requiring seed, bulb, cultivation or future supply. Pass 33 adds **taxonomic latency**: the physical reference exists, but its identity may remain unresolved until local or distant experts determine it.

They can interact but are analytically distinct:

- `phenological latency`: the plant is not yet in the right biological state;
- `taxonomic latency`: the plant/reference is present, but its documentary identity is unfinished;
- `production latency`: the reference is usable and identified, but workshop priority/capacity delays manufacture;
- `dispatch latency`: the model is finished but awaits mounting, packing cases, insurance, certification or transport.

This yields a multi-clock system rather than one travel-to-model timeline.

## 8. Revised article consequence

The strongest article-level change is that the 1892 trip can now be followed through **three different temporalities** after return:

1. references survive as a heterogeneous stock;
2. taxonomic and selection work continues after the journey;
3. the stock is serially reactivated into mixed shipments over 1893–94.

So the article should avoid a climax in which Rudolf returns and “the models are made.” The return is instead the beginning of a long workshop afterlife. The expedition becomes legible in the outgoing shipment record gradually.

A useful sentence:

> **Fieldwork ended in 1892; the expedition remained operational in the workshop for years.**

And a more mechanistic version:

> **Return converted a moving expedition into a reference inventory whose members waited on identification, priority and workshop capacity before reappearing as glass.**

## 9. Source-critical guards

1. Jan 1893 “a few” remains qualitative; do not infer a count.
2. The July 1893 20/15/2 partition is strong at batch level; model-number subranges remain high-confidence reconstruction rather than printed regional headings.
3. `BLA-D00169` says some western plants were probably determined in Cambridge; preserve the modal wording.
4. Taxonomic latency should not be generalized to every expedition reference. It is directly documented for some Jamaica/western material.
5. Dec 1893 says the January consignment is overwhelmingly Jamaica/West-derived with a few exceptions; retain the exceptions instead of calling all fifty American.
6. Darlingtonia model 444 is now batch/shipment closed; direct genealogical identity between archival drawing 133264 and the St Louis colour sketch is still open.
7. Do not use later model success to erase references that remained as herbarium/drawing stock without secure glass descendants.
