# Watson–Morris Express counterpart transport infrastructure — pass 43 — 13 August 2026

## Result

A counterpart-archive sweep around Sereno Watson now identifies a named transatlantic express firm in repeated direct correspondence with Harvard's herbarium system during the exact 1890–91 interval in which the Blaschkas were obtaining American living material at Hosterwitz.

The firm is **Morris European and American Express Co.** Harvard/BHL metadata preserves one outgoing Watson letter to the company dated 22 September 1890 and a dense incoming sequence from the company to Watson from October 1890 through June 1891. Harvard's archival catalogue independently groups the material as a dedicated `Morris European and American Express Co., 1890–1891` folder in the Asa Gray correspondence files.

This is important source infrastructure, but the evidential boundary is strict:

> **Harvard botanists were demonstrably using a named European-American express intermediary while the pre-1892 Blaschka reference-supply ecology was active. The surviving metadata does not yet show that Morris carried a Blaschka, Gillett, Fritillaria, Cypripedium, seed or root shipment.**

Status: **counterpart_transport_infrastructure_candidate**.

## 3. Why the dates matter

The sequence overlaps tightly with the Blaschka-side supply chronology:

- September 1889: Blaschkas describe seeds, roots `via Holland`, garden purchases and cut material as separate acquisition modes;
- February 1890: workshop refers to a previously sent selection/list of American plants;
- 1890: Gillett & Horsford send a California bulb later identified in the July 1891 letter;
- October 1890–June 1891: repeated Morris Express ↔ Watson correspondence;
- July 1891: the Gillett-supplied California Fritillaria has flowered at Hosterwitz and is modelled under acknowledged taxonomic doubt;
- July–November 1891: Gillett himself writes repeatedly to Watson and sends invoices.

This chronology makes the Morris file a high-value counterpart source. It does **not** by itself join the networks.

The test is now concrete:

`Does any Morris document name Southwick, Gillett, Goodale, Dresden/Hosterwitz, Holland, Germany, plants, roots, bulbs, seeds, botanical specimens, customs, packing, charges, consignee or forwarding instructions?`

If yes, event-level crosswalk becomes possible. If no, the series remains a useful negative/control example of Harvard's contemporary international express practice.

## 4. Later institutional calibration of the company

A later early-twentieth-century description of express companies, summarizing federal transport reporting, states that **Adams Express Company handled its foreign business through an auxiliary company known as Morris European and American Express Company**, while European legs could be handed to post or railway as express freight.

This later description is useful for understanding the firm's general institutional function as an international intermediary. It should not be projected backward as proof of the exact corporate arrangement, route or carrier used in Watson's 1890–91 transactions.

Independent legal evidence confirms that Morris European & American Express Co. was acting as an importer/customs-facing express firm by the 1890s: an 1898 federal case concerns its importation of an altar from Europe into New York. That again calibrates corporate function, not the Blaschka transaction.

This is exactly the sort of counterpart evidence needed to prevent a circulation history from being reconstructed solely from sender/receiver narratives.

## 7. Source-critical guards

1. Metadata proves correspondence existence, date, sender/recipient and archival placement, not document content.
2. Do not say Morris carried the 1890 Fritillaria bulb.
3. Do not say `via Holland` in the September 1889 Blaschka letter refers to Morris Express.
4. Do not infer that all Morris↔Watson correspondence concerns shipments; it may include accounts, inquiries or unrelated business.
5. The later Adams Express relationship is contextual calibration and should not be used as direct 1890 routing evidence without contemporaneous corporate documentation.
6. Keep Morris European & American Express distinct from Ehrhorn, Emden & Mayer, Möller, Snow and later Blaschka finished-model forwarders.
7. A company-level match is insufficient: any Blaschka link must be established at transaction level through date, consignee, cargo, route, invoice, case/packet or named actor.
