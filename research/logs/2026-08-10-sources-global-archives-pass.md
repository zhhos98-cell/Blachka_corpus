# Sources page — global archival expansion

Date: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This pass expands the public `sources/` page from a short source list into an archival discovery layer for the Blaschka Object Network. The selection rule is archive-first: an institution is promoted only when a named archival collection, record group, manuscript series, accession register or stable archival locator can be identified. A museum holding Blaschka objects is not by itself enough.

## Public-page changes

- `sources-pass3.js` adds seven archive/repository nodes: Rakow Research Library / Corning, University of Rochester Ward archives, Vassar Natural History records, Tufts UA152, Auckland Cheeseman–Ward manuscripts, Australian Museum archives, and University of Dundee archives.
- `sources-pass4.js` adds eight further archival nodes: Natural History Museum London DF/ZOO/200, Academy of Natural Sciences ANSP-2012-027-Artifact, Cornell RMC Engst and Barlow Ware papers, Alexander Turnbull Library Haast and Yaldwyn papers, National Museum of Ireland Natural History archives, National Museum Prague Antonín Frič fonds, McGill Dawson-Harrington fonds, and Otago/Hocken accession-register infrastructure.
- `sources/global-archive-register.json` records sixteen archival nodes in a machine-readable register and distinguishes direct Blaschka archives, dealer/business archives, direct receiving-side archives, broader receiving-side archives and candidate archival bridges.

## Highest-value direct archival discoveries

1. Natural History Museum Archives, `DF/ZOO/200/1/169-170`: two 1875 Leopold Blaschka letters to A. C. L. G. Günther plus an enclosed catalogue of sea-animal models with prices. `DF/ZOO/200/30/38` adds a Leopold Blaschka postcard to Stuart Oliver Ridley dated 7 October 1886.
2. Academy of Natural Sciences Archives, `ANSP-2012-027-Artifact`: dedicated Blaschka object archive with 48 glass models, original back boards and labels, fragment/condition notes and item-level descriptions. Related `ANSP-2012-060` is the 1879 Ward commercial pamphlet advertising Blaschka models.
3. Alexander Turnbull Library, `MS-Papers-0037-165`: Haast family Blaschka correspondence, with surrogate `MS-Copy-Micro-0717-13`. Yaldwyn `MS-Papers-10780-26` explicitly contains 1879–1884 acquisition correspondence and 1974–1975 loan correspondence for the glass models.
4. Cornell RMC, `13-6-4088`, Box 1 Folder 66: correspondence on the permanent loan of the Blaschka models to Corning, 1963 and 1990. Barlow Ware `4-3-2587`, Box 4 Folder 18 preserves the 2006 restoration/unveiling project.
5. Auckland Museum manuscript identifiers `MUS-1996-6-2` and `MUS-1995-38` provide a receiving-side procurement sequence that can be read against Ward and Dresden records.

## Receiving-side archives with narrow remaining joins

- Vassar College Natural History records: exact 1887 chronological overlap; Box 6 Folders 4.82 and 4.83 are the priority purchase/register targets.
- National Museum of Ireland Archives: accession registers, correspondence, minutes, curator research and Natural History Division archives can bridge invoice/loan evidence to current holdings.
- Otago Museum First Register of Museum Specimens, 1868–1892: best candidate for original numbering and receipt chronology of the 57-model collection; pages still need retrieval.
- Archiv Národního muzea, Antonín Frič fonds: strong receiving-side counterpart for the 1884 Prague gift, but a Blaschka-specific item is still to be isolated.
- McGill Dawson-Harrington fonds: strong Redpath procurement context; remains a candidate until a Ward/Blaschka invoice, catalogue or order letter is identified.
- Dundee University Archives: useful institutional and D'Arcy Thompson afterlife material; the publicly identified `UR-SF 2/12/2` begins after the original acquisition and should not be used as direct purchase evidence.

## Deliberately pending

The following institutions remain outside the public archive register until a concrete record group or stable archival locator is found: Strasbourg, Rostock, Tübingen, Kremsmünster, Seitenstetten, Florence FST, Liège historical order files, Glasgow/Mason purchase files, Liverpool receiving registers, Nottingham/Wollaton accession records, Oxford 1867 accession registers, UCL/Science Museum dispersal files, Mexico City Museo Nacional/Chopo transfer archives, and Michigan receiving registers. Their object collections may be well documented; the archival trail is not yet specific enough for promotion under the current rule.

## Methodological implication

The global archive map already shows that the Blaschka source base is distributed across several documentary regimes rather than one workshop archive: maker records, dealer/business archives, museum accession registers, curator correspondence, object files, loan/custody records, conservation dossiers and later archival rediscovery. This structure is directly useful for reconstructing chains of circulation and for distinguishing a true source gap from a source that survives in a different institutional layer.
