# Bibliography expansion: Harvard periodicals and archive-list backfill, pass 31

Date: 2026-08-09

## Scope guard

The public bibliography remains strictly Blaschka-focused. Harvard University Herbaria's Series VIII publication register is used as discovery and archival-existence evidence, not as a bulk-import list. General museum reporting, travel writing, generic botany, general glass writing and incidental image use are excluded unless the item itself substantially treats Leopold or Rudolf Blaschka, the Blaschka workshop, the Glass Flowers, the marine invertebrate models, or a named Blaschka collection/exhibition/conservation history.

## Source basis

The mounted archival list `publications_referencing_blaschkas_1878-2007.pdf` preserves a long run of publications retained in the Blaschka papers. The official HOLLIS Archives collection record for the Blaschka Papers provides the collection-level authority and Series VIII context. Exact-title repository searches were run before promotion.

Pass 31 adds four direct records that were absent from the public bibliography:

- Bruno P. Kremer, “Die glasernen Wunder aus Sachsen,” *Kosmos*, March 1995.
- Henri Reiling, “Levend Glas,” *Wetenschap-Cultuur En Samenleving*, 10 October 1995.
- Susan M. Rossi-Wilcox, “Blaschka Glass Flowers,” *Glass Art*, November–December 1996. This is retained separately from Rossi-Wilcox's 1996 Glass Art Society conference paper, which is a different publication.
- Harriet Baskas, “At Corning, Art That Imitates Life — Astonishingly,” *NPR*, 25 June 2007. The live NPR-syndicated article substantially covers Corning's *Botanical Wonders* exhibition, the Harvard Glass Flowers, Blaschka drawings and tools, and the earlier marine-model work.

## Date conflict retained

The Harvard archival publication list places the Baskas/NPR item under 2 July 2007, while the surviving NPR-syndicated WLRN article is explicitly dated 25 June 2007. The public bibliography uses the surviving publication-page date, 25 June 2007. The archival-list date is preserved here as a conflicting archival filing/date layer rather than silently treated as identical.

## Metadata guards

- No page ranges are invented for the 1995 Kremer, 1995 Reiling or 1996 Rossi-Wilcox periodical items because the archival list does not supply them.
- The archive list establishes that Harvard retained the items and gives author/title/venue/date metadata; it does not by itself establish issue pagination.
- The NPR item is independently linked to a surviving full article page; the other three remain linked at collection-register level until stable issue scans or journal indexes are recovered.
