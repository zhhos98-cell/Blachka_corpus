# Morris Express procedural calibration: cases, arrival notice, invoice and valuation — pass 44 — 13 August 2026

## Result

Pass 43 identified a dense 1890–91 Harvard/Sereno Watson correspondence cluster with Morris European and American Express Co. The content of those Watson–Morris letters is still unretrieved. A separate, exactly contemporary primary source now gives a high-resolution control for what Morris was actually doing in transatlantic practice.

On 12 December 1889 Thomas Edison replied to Morris after the company had notified him that **two cases** addressed to him had arrived by the S.S. *La Normandie* from Havre. Morris had asked Edison for an **invoice specifying value**. Edison had no invoice and therefore supplied the original cablegram announcing the purchase, which stated a price of 8,500 francs.

This is unrelated cargo, a marble statue rather than botanical material. Its value is procedural:

> **By late 1889 Morris could sit between European ocean arrival and the American consignee, notify the recipient that cases had arrived, and demand documentary valuation before the shipment could be processed further.**

That makes the Watson correspondence a sharper target. We now know which documentary actions to look for without assuming that Watson's packets followed the Edison route.

## 1. Contemporary primary-source control: Edison, 12 December 1889

The Thomas A. Edison Papers Digital Edition preserves and transcribes Edison's letter to `The Morris Eur. & Am. Ex. Co., L't'd., 18 & 20 Broadway, New York City`, dated 12 December 1889 (`LB035089`).

Edison refers directly to Morris's letter of 10 December, which had:

- notified him of the **arrival of two cases** addressed to him;
- identified the contents as a marble statue and related material;
- identified the ocean carrier as S.S. *La Normandie*;
- identified the European port as Havre;
- requested an **invoice specifying value**.

Edison replied that no invoice had reached him. He therefore offered a cablegram from his Paris agent as evidence of purchase value, 8,500 francs.

Operational sequence:

`European purchase`
→ `case packing / ocean loading`
→ `Havre → New York by named steamship`
→ `Morris receives/handles arrival information`
→ `Morris notifies consignee`
→ `Morris requests invoice/value documentation`
→ `consignee supplies alternative valuation evidence`.

The source does not show every customs step, but invoice/value is plainly part of the firm's post-arrival handling.

## 2. Why this matters for the Watson file

The Watson–Morris archive now has a concrete extraction template. When the 1890–91 letters become readable, do not search only for plant names or `Blaschka`. Extract fields such as:

- number/type of packages;
- arrival notice;
- named vessel;
- European port;
- U.S. arrival point;
- sender/consignee;
- invoice status;
- declared/purchase value;
- substitute valuation evidence;
- customs or duty language;
- onward forwarding instruction;
- charges/account references;
- delay/document deficiency.

Even if no Blaschka name appears, a matching date + supplier + cargo + destination + invoice/account field could still create a transaction-level bridge.

## 3. Morris was an information intermediary as well as a carrier/forwarder

The Edison case is useful because the company is doing more than moving a case. It is converting an arrival into a documentary problem for the consignee: `what is this cargo worth, and what paper can support that value?`

This resembles several later Blaschka logistics mechanisms already established in the project:

- consular invoices and declared value;
- customs opening/inspection;
- brokers converting freight into administratively releasable cargo;
- bills that reconstruct freight/customs/inspection costs;
- paperwork constraining route choice.

Do not collapse Morris into those later actors. The point is functional comparability: **international movement repeatedly required objects to acquire documentary identities as cases, values, invoices and consignments.**

## 4. Counterflow relevance

The project has strong finished-model transport evidence moving Hosterwitz → Harvard. Passes 36–43 are reconstructing the opposite material direction: botanical references and procurement infrastructure moving toward Hosterwitz.

If Morris turns out to have participated in Harvard/Watson plant or specimen movements, this would give the pre-1892 reference side its own forwarding/documentation layer. The Edison control shows that such a layer could include invoice/valuation work even before the Blaschkas' American expedition.

This remains a hypothesis. The currently proved statements are only:

1. Morris handled the Edison two-case transatlantic arrival in 1889 and requested valuation documentation;
2. Morris and Sereno Watson corresponded repeatedly in 1890–91;
3. the Blaschka remote-supply ecology was active in the same period.

No source yet joins all three.

## 5. Evidential guards

1. The Edison cargo was an art object, not botanical material.
2. Do not infer that Morris carried Gillett & Horsford shipments, the Fritillaria bulb, Cypripedium plants, Harvard seeds, roots `via Holland`, or any Blaschka model.
3. Do not infer that Watson's correspondence concerns customs merely because Edison’s did.
4. Do not infer that *La Normandie* or Havre were part of any Blaschka/reference route.
5. Treat the Edison source as **procedural calibration**, not transaction evidence for the project chain.
6. Keep the 1889 Edison letter and the 1890–91 Watson file separate in all data tables until a document-level crosswalk exists.

## 6. New retrieval protocol for counterpart forwarder files

For each potential forwarding/express archive, code two levels separately:

### Corporate procedural evidence

What operations did the firm demonstrably perform in contemporary transactions?

Examples:

- arrival notification;
- case handling;
- invoice request;
- value verification;
- customs-facing documentation;
- onward forwarding.

### Blaschka/reference transaction evidence

Can a particular packet be matched through:

- actor;
- date;
- package/case count;
- cargo description;
- destination;
- account/invoice;
- vessel/port;
- correspondence sequence?

A company is admitted to the Blaschka chain only at the second level.
