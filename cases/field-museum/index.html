Documentary case · Chicago · 1893–present

From World's Fair display to museum collection.

Ward's exposition material, the 1893 acquisition, the 1894 reinstallation and surviving Blaschka displays form a strong collection-level chain. The exact Blaschka subset remains open.

Case 012
Field Museum
Chicago

The fair as collection source

Ward's Natural Science Establishment brought extensive natural-history material to the World's Columbian Exposition of 1893. Field Museum histories identify Ward material as part of the museum's foundation collection and explicitly include Blaschka glass models.

Purchase and reinstallation

The Ward acquisition was contracted in November 1893. Recent institutional research reconstructs the purchase at $95,000 and records transfer into the museum building, with breakdown, transport and initial reinstallation completed by January 1894.

The present endpoint

The Field Museum's current mollusk-collection page dates the Ward component to 1893, describes 2,947 series, and states that it includes some Blaschka glass models now on public exhibit. The 2,947 figure is a Ward mollusk-component count, not a Blaschka-model count.

What the chain closes

The exposition context, Ward acquisition, institutional transfer and reinstallation, and modern survival of Blaschka models can be joined at collection level.

What remains open

The exact Blaschka quantity, present catalogue numbers, object-level crosswalk from the fair to current holdings, and complete condition/location list remain open.
