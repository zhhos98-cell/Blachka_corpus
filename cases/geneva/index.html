Documentary case · Geneva · 1888–2026

From 117 objects to a vibration-controlled return.

Geneva carries the collection through an unusually long lifecycle: an 1888 acquisition, a published survival figure, restoration and dedicated display, then boxing and off-site movement prompted by construction risk.

Case 013
MHNG
Geneva

The 1888 acquisition

Local documentary reconstruction places the Geneva acquisition in 1888 under museum director Godefroy Lunel. It gives an original total of 117 objects, a 512-Mark shipment cost and a 20 February dispatch. The original order, letter and museum register still need direct recovery, so these transaction details remain tied to the published reconstruction rather than promoted into a fully imaged archival chain.

Survival and restoration

The last exact published survival figure recovered in this project is 94 objects. Geneva's 2008 annual report records restoration by Isabelle Pirotte and the opening of a dedicated Blaschka room on 12 June 2008. The city's Mirabilia platform also exposes two local inventory anchors, MHNG-INVE-79252 and MHNG-INVE-79257.

A conservation move caused by the building

The current renovation documents make the collection's material vulnerability part of its provenance. Geneva's municipal project states that the models were boxed and moved to city heritage storage because construction vibration posed a risk. Their planned return depends on further consolidation and restoration and on showcases adapted for vibration, climate and light. The museum's progressive public reopening is planned from September 2026; this case does not assume that the Blaschka collection will be visible on the first reopening date.

What the chain closes

The 1888 collection origin, a 117-object historical total, the later 94-object published survival figure, 2008 restoration and dedicated display, two public inventory identifiers, and the present preventive-conservation move are all securely separable and connectable.

What remains open

The original 1888 order and accession register, a current object-by-object export for the 94-model historical survival set, the exact 2026 survival/restoration total, and the post-renovation display count remain open.
