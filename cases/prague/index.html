Documentary case · Prague · 1884–2025

Four models, one surviving octopus.

A workshop letter draft, museum accession evidence and later conservation research make Prague an unusually tight object-level chain. The surviving object can be followed without pretending that the other three fates are equally clear.

Case 011
National Museum
Prague

The 1884 gift

On 8 November 1884 the Blaschka workshop drafted a letter to the Prague dealer and natural-history supplier Václav Frič. The gift comprised four explicitly numbered catalogue models: no. 36 Anthea cereus var. maxima, no. 224 Aurelia aurita, no. 235 Pelagia noctiluca, and no. 553 Eledone moschatus. Frič mediated their delivery to the National Museum. Henri Reiling’s reconstruction also records that the gift was entered in the museum accession books.

A split survival history

The four-object starting point does not become a four-object modern inventory. The original Eledone, now identified as Eledone moschata, survives. A recent Czech account places it in the National Museum’s permanent exhibition and reports the original Aurelia, displayed with it from 1908, as destroyed during the fighting in Prague in May 1945. The present fates of the original no. 36 and no. 235 remain unresolved. Later museum models of matching taxa therefore cannot simply be substituted for the 1884 objects.

The surviving object as material evidence

The surviving octopus is more than a provenance endpoint. Eva Rydlová and Ivana Kopecká’s conservation study describes blown-glass components around an internal wire armature, packing materials treated with sodium silicate, proteinaceous webbing, surface paint and varnish, followed by consolidation and reconstruction of damaged or missing features. The object thus preserves evidence of manufacture and later intervention in the same physical unit.

What the chain closes

The date of the gift, the intermediary, the recipient institution, all four workshop catalogue numbers, accession-book confirmation, one original survivor, one documented wartime loss, and the surviving octopus’s modern conservation history can be joined with high confidence.

What remains open

The exact National Museum accession numbers have not yet been recovered. The original no. 36 and no. 235 still lack secure fate statements. The complete 1884 commercial shipment that carried the gift with Frič’s business material also remains to be itemised. These gaps are kept separate from the closed chain.
