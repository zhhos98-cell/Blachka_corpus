# Rudolf Blaschka 1892 — shipment crosswalk run

Updated: 2026-08-13

This run joined the 1892 travel letters to the Harvard annotated shipment lists. It materially improves the object-level argument because several plants observed, collected or sent from the American field season reappear as numbered Harvard models in dated shipments within two years.

## Shipment boundaries recovered

The annotated Harvard list gives:

- `9th Shipment, January, 1894`, beginning with model 424. This block includes `452. Orthocarpus purpurascens`.
- `10th Shipment, August, 1894`, beginning with model 475. This block includes `478. Coffee arabica`, `479. Coffee liberica`, and `482. Cajanus indicus`, among other tropical/Jamaican taxa.
- The tenth-shipment scan continues through model 503 at the end of the first folder/part.
- The next scanned part begins with model 504 and continues through model 573; `12th Shipment, January, 1896` begins with model 574.

The heading for the 11th shipment is missing from the present OCR. By sequence and scan/folder structure, models 504–573 are the only possible intervening numbered block and therefore almost certainly form the eleventh shipment, but its exact heading/date should remain **inferred, not transcribed**, until the missing/unclear source image is recovered. This block includes `520 Gilia californica`, `528 Larrea mexicana`, `529 Echinocereus engelmannii`, and `531 Baeria chrysoma`.

## A. Orthocarpus: field encounter → dated model shipment → Rudolf authorship

### Field

`BLA-D00851 / 017:205`, Oakland, 15 May 1892: Rudolf describes *Orthocarpus* growing in such masses that it gave the mountains a soft pink color. The same letter records continued rapid drawing work. It proves field encounter in a drawing-intensive phase, though it does not explicitly say the *Orthocarpus* was one of the drawings made that day.

A much later Rudolf letter preserved in the archive again recalls the rose/purple California hills as covered with *Orthocarpus purpurascens*. This is retrospective memory and should be kept separate from the 1892 field letter, but it confirms the visual scene remained salient to him.

### Model/shipment

The Harvard annotated list places `452. Orthocarpus purpurascens` within the **9th Shipment, January 1894**.

### Authorship

`BLA-D01749 / 020:278` later includes `452 Orthocarpus` among the smaller models Rudolf says he constructed himself alone (with his broader caveat about analytical details and the collaborative character of the collection).

### Status

This is currently the cleanest **dated** field-to-model sequence:

`California encounter, May 1892 → model 452 → Harvard shipment, January 1894 → later Rudolf sole-construction attribution`.

Still missing: a securely identified 1892 drawing/specimen for *Orthocarpus*. Do not claim the exact visual study behind model 452 until that is found.

## B. Coffea liberica: living Jamaican plant → carried biological material → dated model shipment

### Field observation

Rossi-Wilcox's transcription of Rudolf's 6 March 1892 Hope Garden letter states that he studied both coffee varieties grown in Jamaica, *Coffea arabica* and *C. liberica*, among the useful tropical plants available there.

### Material circulation

`BLA-D00743 / 017:97`, Cambridge after Jamaica, is more specific. Rudolf says that at Castleton he personally picked berries of the best Jamaican coffee from the plant and was sending the berries home. The translation/OCR renders the taxon imperfectly (`Chofea liberia`), but the context and the earlier Hope Garden letter support *Coffea liberica*.

### Model/shipment

The Harvard **10th Shipment, August 1894** contains:

- `478. Coffee arabica`
- `479. Coffee liberica`

### Status

This creates an unusually strong biological/reference chain:

`living Coffea observed in Jamaica, March 1892 → C. liberica berries personally picked at Castleton and sent to Hosterwitz, April 1892 → Harvard model 479 Coffee liberica shipped August 1894`.

The causal step “the sent berries were directly used to make model 479” is **not yet proven**. What is proven is same-taxon field study, physical transfer of plant material to Hosterwitz, and a same-taxon model entering the Harvard shipment just over two years later. This is exactly the kind of link the article needs, provided the remaining production documentation can be found.

## C. Cajanus indicus: seed circulation → archival drawing → dated model shipment

### Biological material

`BLA-D00743 / 017:97` explicitly names *Cajanus indicus* among the Jamaican seeds/material Rudolf sorted after his return and sent home, with comments on cultivation and use.

### Drawing archive

The Corning/Rakow finding aid contains `Cajanus indicus, 1892 or 1895` in the botanical drawing series. The date remains ambiguous between Rudolf's two American trips, so this is a candidate bridge rather than a closed 1892 drawing.

### Model/shipment

The Harvard **10th Shipment, August 1894** includes `482. Cajanus indicus`.

### Status

Current chain:

`Jamaican seed/material in Rudolf's 1892 dispatch → Corning Cajanus drawing dated 1892/1895 → model 482 shipped to Harvard August 1894`.

This may become stronger than *Larrea* because it combines biological transfer, surviving drawing evidence and a dated shipment. The remaining task is to date the Corning drawing and inspect any label/model notes for reference-source information.

## D. Larrea mexicana: field encounter → drawing candidate → model → later authorship

The previous four-layer *Larrea* candidate remains strong:

- `BLA-D00779 / 017:133`: *Larrea mexicana* is explicitly part of the flowering Arizona desert Rudolf encountered while drawing intensively.
- `BLA-D00796 / 017:150`: the later Arizona/San Diego letter again identifies *Larrea* as the dominant chaparral-desert shrub and emphasizes that the party had arrived at the brief favorable flowering moment.
- Corning finding aid: `Larrea mexicana, 1892 or 1895`.
- Harvard annotated list: `528. Larrea mexicana` in the model block between the August 1894 tenth shipment and January 1896 twelfth shipment.
- `BLA-D01749 / 020:278`: Rudolf later lists `528 Larrea` among models constructed by himself alone.

The exact shipment date of 528 remains pending because the eleventh-shipment heading is absent from the current OCR. The drawing date also remains 1892/1895. For those reasons *Larrea* is richer in layers but less tightly dated than *Orthocarpus*, *Coffee liberica* or *Cajanus*.

## What this changes analytically

The article no longer needs to depend on a generalized statement that Rudolf's trip supplied “three years of material.” We now have candidate micro-histories in which the same taxon crosses distinct documentary and material states on a recoverable timeline. The best trio presently has different strengths:

1. **Orthocarpus** — strongest dated field encounter → model shipment → later authorship chain.
2. **Coffea liberica** — strongest living plant → transported biological material → dated model shipment chain.
3. **Cajanus indicus** — strongest biological material → archival drawing candidate → dated model shipment chain.

Together they support a more precise claim: the productive unit of the 1892 expedition was not simply an observation or a drawing. It was a conversion sequence in which living plants could generate sketches, preserved material, seeds/berries, later cultivation or comparison, numbered glass models and finally shipment into Harvard's display system.
