# Rudolf Blaschka 1892 — object-level crosswalk candidates

Updated: 2026-08-13

This note records the first object-level bridges found after the route/infrastructure checkpoint. The aim is to test whether individual plants can be followed across distinct evidential states: field encounter → drawing/preserved specimen/propagule → later model → workshop attribution. These are candidate closures, not yet all fully closed.

## 1. Larrea mexicana — strongest current candidate

### 1892 field layer

In the Arizona sequence, `BLA-D00779 / 017:133` places Rudolf among desert vegetation dominated by *Larrea mexicana* while he was drawing rapidly. The same letter reports a concentrated drawing session and then discusses *Larrea* as a conspicuous local plant. The present OCR does **not** explicitly state that *Larrea* itself was one of the plants drawn, so keep “observed in the same field/drawing episode” distinct from “demonstrably drawn.” `BLA-D00796 / 017:150` again identifies *Larrea mexicana* as a dominant desert plant.

### Corning drawing layer

The Rakow finding aid contains an entry for `Larrea mexicana, 1892 or 1895`. This is a direct archive-level bridge to a Blaschka botanical drawing, but the catalogue date remains ambiguous between Rudolf's two American trips. The image/record must be inspected before assigning it specifically to 1892.

### Harvard model layer

The annotated Harvard model lists contain `528. Larrea mexicana` among the later numbered botanical models.

### Rudolf authorship layer

In the later retrospective letter `BLA-D01749 / 020:278` (duplicate occurrences `039:184`, `039:509`), Rudolf includes `528 Larrea` among the smaller individual models he says were constructed by himself alone.

### Current status

**Four-layer candidate:** 1892 Arizona field observation → Corning drawing dated 1892/1895 → Harvard model no. 528 → Rudolf retrospective sole-authorship claim.

Still required for full closure:

1. resolve whether the Corning drawing is 1892 or 1895;
2. identify the exact Harvard shipment containing model 528;
3. inspect the drawing and model annotation for any field/reference note tying model 528 to the American material;
4. verify the retrospective letter against the scan before quotation.

## 2. Darlingtonia californica — strongest conversion-of-medium case

`BLA-D00896 / 017:251` is unusually explicit. At the Missouri Botanical Garden, Rudolf found *Darlingtonia* already flowering, despite having contemplated a separate Shasta Mountains excursion for it. He made a color sketch and put a good specimen into alcohol. The garden therefore substituted for distant field travel and generated two reference media from one living plant: a color study and a preserved specimen.

The Corning finding aid includes `Darlingtonia californica, 1892 or 1895`. This makes the archival drawing a plausible survivor of the St Louis episode, but its catalogue date remains ambiguous and should not yet be equated with the 1892 sketch without image-level comparison.

Next closure target: locate the Harvard model/section number and any surviving specimen or label. Even without that final model link, this is already direct primary evidence for `living collection → color sketch + alcohol specimen`.

## 3. Gilia — drawing/model/authorship candidate

The Corning botanical-drawing inventory includes *Gilia* material dated `1892 or 1895` (including *Gilia californica* in the finding-aid search layer). The Harvard annotated list includes `520. Gilia californica`. Rudolf's retrospective letter `BLA-D01749 / 020:278` identifies models `520 and 570 Gilia` among models he says he constructed himself.

Current strength: drawing → numbered model → retrospective authorship.

Current gap: the 1892 field letters have not yet yielded a secure *Gilia* encounter that can be linked to model 520. A source-017 hit for *Gilia tricolor* belongs to an earlier shipment list and must not be misread as 1892 field evidence.

## 4. Orthocarpus — field/model/authorship candidate

`BLA-D00851 / 017:205`, written from the Oakland/California phase, describes nearby mountains covered in pink *Orthocarpus* and reports rapid drawing activity in the same episode. The wording presently supports field observation plus contemporaneous drawing activity, but does not explicitly identify *Orthocarpus* as one of the drawings.

Rudolf's retrospective `BLA-D01749 / 020:278` includes `452 Orthocarpus` among models he says he made himself. The next step is to locate the exact Corning drawing record and the Harvard model-list entry for 452, then test whether species and chronology match.

## 5. Ephedra — drawing/model/authorship candidate with missing field anchor

The Corning finding aid includes `Ephedra nevadensis, 1892 or 1895`. Rudolf's retrospective letter lists `541 Ephedra` among models attributed to his own construction. Current searches of source 017 did not recover an `Ephedra` field-letter hit. This case should remain a drawing/model/authorship candidate unless a field reference emerges from image-level reading or variant nomenclature.

## 6. Gossypium barbadense — propagule/drawing/workshop candidate

`BLA-D00743 / 017:97`, after Rudolf's return from Jamaica, records the sorting and forwarding of Jamaican seeds and explicitly names *Gossypium barbadense* among the material handled. The Corning finding aid contains a *Gossypium barbadense* drawing dated `1892 or 1895`.

A later retrospective discussion of large *Gossypium* flowers describes divided labor between Leopold and Rudolf: Leopold made some larger flower components while Rudolf handled inner parts. This could become one of the best examples for connecting travel material to collaborative workshop manufacture, but the exact species/model number and chronology must be resolved before treating the later *Gossypium* passage as the same object.

## 7. Workshop bridge after the 1892 journey

`BLA-D01750 / 020:279` (duplicate occurrences `039:185`, `039:510`) is the most important conceptual bridge found in this run. In a later recollection Rudolf stresses that Leopold continued working while Rudolf was in America in 1892. He then says that when he returned with the journey plants the workload did not diminish and describes their subsequent arrangement as `quite manufacturing like`: an initial period devoted to making flowers and leaves, followed by finishing; Leopold made magnified stamens/sections, Rudolf painted, and models were finished at a regular pace.

The OCR around “journey plants” is imperfect and the scan must be checked before quotation, but the sequence is clear enough for research use: American field material entered an explicitly divided, staged workshop process. This sharply strengthens the distributed-conversion argument because it links travel reference material to the organization of production rather than leaving “fieldwork” and “making” as separate narratives.

## 8. Parallel workshop continuity during Rudolf's absence

The Harvard annotated list contains the heading `7th Shipment, Feb. 1893` and directly notes that models `#312–322` were made by Leopold Blaschka in the first half of 1892. This is independent evidence that the Hosterwitz workshop continued production while Rudolf traveled. It also cautions against attributing all models produced around the trip to Rudolf or to American reference material.

The later list containing models 504–531 includes `520 Gilia californica`, `528 Larrea mexicana`, `529 Echinocereus engelmannii`, and `531 Baeria chrysoma`, but the exact shipment heading for this numbered block has not yet been recovered. Do **not** assign 504–531 to the February 1893 seventh shipment until the intervening shipment-list pages are joined.

## 9. Immediate archival targets created by the crosswalk

The Corning/Rakow business-file finding aid lists `Invoices, 1890–1899` and label ranges that include `450–515` and `516–599`. These are unusually well aligned with the candidate model numbers 452, 520, 528, 529, 531, and 541. The Harvard finding aid places the annotated shipment lists in Series V, Carton 1, Folders 3–6. Together these sources should allow the next run to recover shipment date, model wording, corrections/withdrawals, and perhaps transaction information without widening the corpus indiscriminately.

## Current judgment

The strongest path is now narrower than “Rudolf's American trip.” A publishable mechanism is beginning to appear at the object scale: field access solved a reference problem, but the useful output of travel was a bundle of representational and biological surrogates whose later value depended on preservation, cultivation, correspondence, supplier networks, staged workshop labor, numbering, shipment, display, and repair. *Larrea* currently offers the best candidate for a full encounter-to-model chain; *Darlingtonia* offers the cleanest direct demonstration of conversion across media. The next gain will come from closing those chains, not from adding more stops to the itinerary.
