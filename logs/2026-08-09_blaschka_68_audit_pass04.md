# Blaschka surviving-collection census audit — pass 04

The 2026 current-proof census now contains **41 institution-level nodes** across the seed and four expansion files. This is still a proof-of-current-custody layer rather than a claim that 41 of the 2017 surviving 68 have been matched.

## Six additional current institutional nodes

- University of Pisa Natural History Museum: live collection page states 51 marine-invertebrate glass models; the museum's 2024 social report independently lists the Blaschka collection among current historical holdings.
- Oxford University Museum of Natural History: current object-collections page explicitly says OUMNH holds a Blaschka collection managed by zoological staff. Current total is left blank because the museum says most non-specimen objects remain uncatalogued.
- Perth Art Gallery / Culture Perth and Kinross: the 2025 GLASS exhibition explicitly included nineteenth-century Blaschka sea-creature models. This confirms current physical survival but does not prove that the historical fourteen-model corpus survives intact.
- Glasgow Museums: the live collections database currently identifies multiple Blaschka groups across brittle stars, sea cucumbers, sea-lily development, annelids and molluscs. Taxonomic collection totals mix natural specimens and models, so no Blaschka aggregate is reconstructed.
- Milwaukee Public Museum: the live Collection Highlights page retains a Blaschka Glass Works holding and the FY2023-24 annual report illustrates a Blaschka Glaucilla briareus. The stated purchase of 70 is historical and is not projected into a present survivor count; the census conservatively records at least one directly evidenced current object.
- Field Museum: the museum's current exhibition system still lists What Is an Animal? as an active gallery, while Field's exhibition history states Blaschka glass models are displayed throughout it. Current survival is plural; the exact subset is open.

## La Specola current reverification closed

A recent University of Florence / ANMS restoration report provides the current bridge missing in the previous pass. It discusses restoration associated with La Specola's reopening on 21 February 2024, lists **six glass invertebrate models** in the restoration corpus, and explicitly illustrates restoration with integration of missing parts on Blaschka glass models. This closes current physical custody for the small University of Florence / La Specola node and allows it to move from the pre-2017 reverification queue into the 2026 current census with a count of six.

This Florence university node remains institutionally separate from Fondazione Scienza e Tecnica Florence, whose nineteenth-century collection was originally 118 and had 111 survivors in the 2017 study.

## UCD remains deliberately unpromoted

University College Dublin remains a strong pre-2017 known-survivor candidate: the 2016 Irish survey located 117 models and reconstructed the 1885 A. C. Haddon acquisition. Current searches have not yet produced a sufficiently direct 2024-2026 UCD collection/CMS/display/storage statement. A present UCD staff research-interest page mentioning Blaschka work is not treated as physical-custody proof. UCD therefore remains in the reverification queue rather than being promoted simply because the historical evidence is excellent.

## Delta status

Pavia remains the strongest post-2017 delta candidate. Trento remains a baseline-source conflict after the 2022 rediscovery language was compared with the 2025 paper's citation to Ruggiero and Larson 2017. No institution has yet been assigned `baseline_2017_match=no` without direct reconstruction of the relevant 2017 map layer.

The census is now large enough that the next high-value task is less about accumulating famous museums and more about classifying the 41 current nodes into: direct pre-2017 baseline-positive, baseline conflict, unresolved map match, and strong post-2017 delta candidate.
