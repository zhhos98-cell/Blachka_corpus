Market traces

Auction appearances

A selective index of public auction appearances used as provenance evidence. It is not intended as a market-value guide.

Selection rule

Following objects after they leave stable institutional custody

This layer includes attributed Blaschka models with a public auction record when the sources checked do not identify the buyer as a museum, university or comparable institutional collection.

Where an institutional buyer is explicitly documented, the lot is excluded from this auction layer and belongs instead in the collection and provenance mapping. Earlier museum ownership does not exclude a later market appearance: a deaccessioned object can move from an institutional collection into private circulation and then reappear at auction.

Auction appearance ≠ private ownership. When the buyer is not public, the post-sale owner remains open.

Research update · August 2026. The old register numbers now expose the Science Museum dispersal at object scale. Number 1877-360 later reached Christie’s, while the immediately adjacent 1877-361 is documented in the 1927 transfer to Cardiff. The pattern reverses at 1877-380 / 1877-381: 380 survives at Cardiff, while 381 later reached Christie’s. Adjacency does not prove a shared transfer or model set; it makes these number-neighbourhoods priority search keys for the 1925–27 transfer and disposal registers.

Lot 27

Stinging polyp · Hydractinia echinata · around 1870

The German catalogue title Stachelpolyp can be resolved to Hydractinia echinata. Cornell’s Blaschka collection identifies this model design as catalogue no. 156. The number is a design/catalogue key, not a unique serial number for this physical object. Published hammer price: €30,000.

Research update · August 2026. The project’s complete transcription of the published Museum für Naturkunde OS001-02 Blaschka inventory contains 40 rows but no Hydractinia echinata, Stachelpolyp, or catalogue no. 156. This makes a direct match to a currently published MfN inventory record unsupported. Humboldt University, however, documents a 1970 reduction of its Zoologische Lehrsammlung in which material was given away, lost, or transferred to MfN. That event is now a priority provenance window, but it is not yet an object-level disposal record for lot 27. HU collection history ↗

Research use

Why keep auction records?

Auction catalogues can preserve inventory numbers, old labels, condition notes, former collection names and dimensions that are absent from current museum databases. They can also mark the point at which a model leaves an institutional chain and becomes difficult to follow.

This page therefore records provenance events first and prices second. Buyer identity is never inferred from the fact of a sale.

What is excluded? Search results can look more certain than the catalogues themselves. Wimbledon Auctions sold five lots in February 2023 headed “in the manner of Leopold and Rudolf Blaschka”, but the objects were explicitly shell-and-wax anatomical models rather than Blaschka glass. They are retained in the research backend as false-positive controls and excluded from the Blaschka auction count. Wimbledon sale ↗
