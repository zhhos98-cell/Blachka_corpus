Auctions

Auction appearances

Selected public sale records used to trace provenance, labels and former collections. Prices are recorded when they help identify the event; an auction appearance does not prove private ownership.

2019

Christie’s London · Peter Petrou: Tales of the Unexpected · 30 Jan 2019

Three Science Museum models return to the market record

All three lot pages give Science Museum acquisition in 1877 and deaccession in 1925–27. Painted inventory numbers connect the sale records back to the museum. The buyers are not identified publicly.

Lot 46

Portuguese man o’ war · Science Museum 1877-381

Physalia physalis, circa 1877. The lot record notes a later replacement to the top bulb and a revised estimate of £10,000–15,000.

Lot 47

Jellyfish · Science Museum 1877-360

Polyclonia frondosa, circa 1877. Described as reassembled. Catalogue estimate: £15,000–25,000.

Christie’s ↗

Lot 48

Jellyfish · Science Museum 1877-376

Circa 1877, mounted on an iron rod. Catalogue estimate: £15,000–25,000.

Christie’s ↗

Research update · August 2026. Number 1877-360 later reached Christie’s while adjacent 1877-361 is documented in the 1927 transfer to Cardiff. The pattern reverses at 1877-380 / 1877-381. Adjacency is a search clue, not proof of a shared transfer.

2025

Kunst & Design Auktionshaus, Krefeld · 10 May 2025

Two Berlin-labelled models open a provenance question

No institutional buyer is identified in the published results, so post-sale custody remains open. Both objects carry labels transcribed as Zoolog. Institut Universität, Berlin.

Lot 26

Sea anemone · around 1870

Handmade glass model in a display case, 10 × 13 × 13 cm. Published hammer price: €28,000. Exact taxon remains open.

Lot 27

Stinging polyp · Hydractinia echinata · around 1870

The catalogue’s Stachelpolyp can be resolved to Hydractinia echinata. Cornell identifies this model design as catalogue no. 156; that number identifies a design, not this unique physical object. Published hammer price: €30,000.

Reading the records

Provenance first, price second.

Auction records can preserve inventory numbers, labels, condition notes, dimensions and former collection names. Buyer identity is left open unless a source identifies it.

Objects catalogued only as “in the manner of” the Blaschkas are excluded from the public Blaschka count.
