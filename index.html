About the project

Following objects through the records made around them.

The project connects workshop evidence with later transactions, movement, collection records and surviving objects. Secure links, useful comparisons and unresolved gaps remain distinct.

Where the story begins

From a Bohemian glassworking family to a worldwide scientific trade

Carolina (Karolina) Riegel Blaschka

Karolina appears in the surviving archive as part of the workshop household and, more cautiously, its knowledge infrastructure. Family papers preserve her legal and property presence, while correspondence repeatedly places her inside Rudolf’s working and visitor world. Harvard/Goodale papers preserve a damaged 1897 note concerning a female geologist’s visit to Rudolf, but the OCR does not support assigning Karolina a precise action during that visit. Her role in knowledge mediation therefore remains a qualified research claim, not an attribution of model-making or scientific authorship.

Leopold Blaschka (1822–1895)

A Bohemian glassworker whose early losses preceded an Atlantic voyage in 1853. Becalmed at sea, he drew jellyfish and other marine invertebrates. By the 1860s he was making zoological glass models in Dresden and supplying an expanding scientific market.

Rudolf Blaschka (1857–1939)

Son of Leopold and Carolina. He joined the workshop in 1876. Father and son supplied museums and universities internationally; from 1890 the workshop worked exclusively on Harvard’s botanical commission while Rudolf increasingly carried the work forward.
