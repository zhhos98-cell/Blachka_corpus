About the project

A public research index for objects whose histories exceed the cabinet.

The Blaschka Object Network follows glass models as historical objects in motion. It reconstructs how they were selected, priced, ordered, packed, shipped, received, displayed, damaged, repaired, reclassified, dispersed, and rediscovered.

The project is built from archival and collection evidence. Its public pages are designed to show both the links that can be closed and the links that remain open.

surviving object→local record→transaction→workshop↔back to the object

01

What is different here?

Many catalogues begin with what survives in a collection. This project works in both directions: from a surviving model back into labels, accession registers, invoices, correspondence, and workshop papers; and from documentary traces forward toward present custody. The result is a network of object histories, not a census alone.

02

Method

Each case is assembled as an evidential chain. Workshop correspondence, dealer accounts, freight marks, museum accessions, local identifiers, conservation files, exhibition records, published images, and current collection data remain distinguishable layers. A connection is promoted only when the sources actually bridge it.

03

What it contains

Institutional cases, object-level identifiers where recoverable, commercial catalogue numbers, prices and payments, shipment routes, transfers, damage and repair, conservation, rediscovery, present display or storage, and precise archival targets for the gaps that remain.

04

Research model

This is a versioned research prototype rather than a finished catalogue. The backend can preserve more detail, OCR ambiguity, competing readings, and unresolved provenance than the public page. Cases can be revised or upgraded as new evidence closes a chain.

05

Limits

The record is uneven. OCR can be fragile; nineteenth-century ledgers compress money, quantity, and order numbers; museum names and administrative custody change; many collection databases expose only selected objects. Missing links are left open instead of being completed by analogy.

06

Contribute

Corrections and additions are welcome, especially local accession numbers, photographs of labels or marks, archival references, purchase and movement records, conservation documentation, and current display or storage information. Contributions should remain traceable to their source and will be credited.

Liverpool Museum: one order, several documentary lives

An 1887 workshop sequence follows a Liverpool order from manufacture to accounting and dispatch. More than a century later, surviving models reappear in exhibition and collection records.

11 Oct 1887

The Blaschka workshop told its British intermediary that work on “the models for Liverpool” would begin the following week.

23 Nov 1887

A workshop account separated 247 Marks for the Liverpool models from an additional packing estimate.

Dec 1887

One freight case, marked I.B. 268, was routed through M. O. W. Möller in Hamburg for onward delivery to Liverpool. The dispatch letter records an invoice total read in the project OCR as 268 Marks.

2017

A modern account described a trove of Blaschka sea anemones ↗ rediscovered in a drawer at World Museum Liverpool and shown in Manchester Museum’s Object Lessons ↗.

2019

A published collection image identified a World Museum Liverpool specimen as Blaschka catalogue no. 93 ↗.

What the chain closes

Manufacture, workshop accounting, a marked shipment, institutional destination, and modern physical survival can be connected at collection level.

What remains open

The original model count, Liverpool accession numbers, the receipt register, and the object-level bridge between case I.B. 268 and individual surviving models.
