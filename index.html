About the project

Following objects through the records made around them.

The project connects workshop evidence with later transactions, movement, collection records and surviving objects. Secure links, useful comparisons and unresolved gaps remain distinct.

Where the story begins

From a Bohemian glassworking family to a worldwide scientific trade

Leopold Blaschka (1822–1895) came from a Bohemian glassworking family. After the deaths of his first wife and son in 1850 and his father in 1852, he sailed to the United States in 1853. Becalmed for two weeks, he drew jellyfish and other marine invertebrates whose translucent forms suggested glass as a material for scientific modelling.

Carolina Riegel Blaschka married Leopold in 1854 and was Rudolf’s mother; Harvard’s Blaschka archive preserves her in family correspondence and biographical photographs. By the 1860s Leopold was producing marine models in Dresden. Rudolf Blaschka (1857–1939) joined the workshop in 1876, and together father and son supplied museums and universities internationally. From 1890 the workshop worked exclusively on Harvard’s Glass Flowers. This project follows the earlier zoological models through sale, shipment, use, damage, dispersal, rediscovery and present custody.
