About the project

A documentary research project for following Blaschka objects through workshops, transactions and collections.

The Blaschka Object Network follows glass models through the records created around them: making and ordering, prices and payments, packing and freight, institutional receipt, display, damage, repair, movement and rediscovery.

It is designed to complement collection catalogues and object-documentation projects by concentrating on the documentary chains between workshop records and surviving collections. Links remain separate from gaps, uncertainty and comparison.

01

What this project adds

Existing collections work documents, identifies and compares Blaschka models. This project concentrates on another layer: the documentary chains through which objects were made, ordered, priced, packed, shipped, received, moved, damaged and repaired. Collection records and archival records remain distinct until the evidence connects them.

02

Method

Cases are built in both directions. Surviving objects are followed backwards through labels, accession records and institutional files. Archival events are followed forwards through correspondence, accounts, dealers, freight marks and receipt records. The useful point is where those two routes meet, or fail to meet.

03

What it contains

Workshop practice and knowledge, orders, prices and payments, intermediaries, packing and shipment, institutional receipt, local identifiers, transfers, damage and repair, conservation, rediscovery, present custody, and precise archival targets for the links that remain open.

04

Research model

The research backend is deliberately richer than the public interface. It can retain OCR uncertainty, alternate readings, competing identifiers, source-level notes and unresolved provenance. Public cases expose only the connections that can be explained and sourced clearly.

05

Limits

The record is uneven, and object-level identification can remain impossible even when a collection-level chain is strong. A shared catalogue number or model type does not by itself prove that two records describe the same physical object. Open links stay open.

06

Contribute

Corrections and additions are welcome, especially accession numbers, photographs of labels or marks, archival references, purchase and movement records, conservation documentation, and current display or storage information. Contributions should remain traceable to their source and will be credited.

Liverpool Museum: one order, several documentary lives

An 1887 workshop sequence follows a Liverpool order from manufacture to accounting and dispatch. More than a century later, surviving models reappear in exhibition and collection records.

11 Oct 1887

The Blaschka workshop told its British intermediary that work on “the models for Liverpool” would begin the following week.

23 Nov 1887

A workshop account separated 247 Marks for the Liverpool models from an additional packing estimate.

Dec 1887

One freight case, marked I.B. 268, was routed through M. O. W. Möller in Hamburg for onward delivery to Liverpool. The dispatch letter records an invoice total read in the project OCR as 268 Marks.

2017

A modern account described a trove of Blaschka sea anemones ↗ rediscovered in a drawer at World Museum Liverpool and shown in Manchester Museum’s Object Lessons ↗.

2019

A published collection image identified a World Museum Liverpool specimen as Blaschka catalogue no. 93 ↗.

What the chain closes

Manufacture, workshop accounting, a marked shipment, institutional destination, and modern physical survival can be connected at collection level.

What remains open

The original model count, Liverpool accession numbers, the receipt register, and the object-level bridge between case I.B. 268 and individual surviving models.
