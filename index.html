About the project

A documentary research project for following Blaschka objects through workshops, transactions and collections.

The Blaschka Object Network follows glass models through the records created around them: making and ordering, prices and payments, packing and freight, institutional receipt, display, damage, repair, movement and rediscovery.

It complements collection catalogues by concentrating on the documentary chains between workshop records and surviving objects. Connections, comparisons and unresolved gaps remain visibly distinct.

What this adds

It follows the documentary chains linking workshop, trade, transport and collections, alongside existing object documentation.

02

Method

Cases are worked in both directions, from surviving objects backwards and archival events forwards, until the records meet or fail to meet.

03

Contents

Workshop knowledge, orders, prices, intermediaries, packing, shipment, receipt, movement, damage, repair, conservation and present custody.

04

Research model

The backend keeps OCR uncertainty, alternate readings and unresolved provenance; the public layer shows only what can be explained and sourced clearly.

05

Limits

A shared catalogue number or model type does not prove that two records describe the same physical object. Open links stay open.

06

Contribute

Corrections, accession numbers, labels, archival references, movement records and conservation evidence are welcome when they remain traceable to a source.

Liverpool Museum: one order, several documentary lives

An 1887 workshop sequence follows a Liverpool order from manufacture to accounting and dispatch. More than a century later, surviving models reappear in exhibition and collection records.

11 Oct 1887

The Blaschka workshop told its British intermediary that work on “the models for Liverpool” would begin the following week.

23 Nov 1887

A workshop account separated 247 Marks for the Liverpool models from an additional packing estimate.

Dec 1887

One freight case, marked I.B. 268, was routed through M. O. W. Möller in Hamburg for onward delivery to Liverpool. The dispatch letter records an invoice total read in the project OCR as 268 Marks.

2017

A modern account described a trove of Blaschka sea anemones ↗ rediscovered in a drawer at World Museum Liverpool and shown in Manchester Museum’s Object Lessons ↗.

2019

A published collection image identified a World Museum Liverpool specimen as Blaschka catalogue no. 93 ↗.

What the chain closes

Manufacture, workshop accounting, a marked shipment, institutional destination, and modern physical survival can be connected at collection level.

What remains open

The original model count, Liverpool accession numbers, the receipt register, and the object-level bridge between case I.B. 268 and individual surviving models.

Auckland Museum: seven samples and the order that never grew

Thomas Cheeseman approached the Blaschka models cautiously: catalogue first, exchange if possible, then samples. Seven models reached Auckland in 1885. Plans for a much larger order were repeatedly postponed.

9 Sep 1882

Cheeseman asked Ward for a priced Blaschka catalogue and said he would prefer to obtain models by exchange if possible.

28 Apr 1884

Before committing to a larger purchase, Cheeseman asked for one or two sample models so that he could inspect them first.

3 Apr 1885

Ward described an Adams-linked £20 settlement covering glass models and minerals. Payment moved through a triangular debt arrangement rather than a simple workshop invoice.

1885

Charles Adams safely delivered the Blaschka models. The surviving group reconstructed in the 2019 study contains seven models with Blaschka catalogue numbers, individual Ward prices and Auckland registration numbers.

1885–87

Cheeseman considered a much larger order, first up to £50 and later £40–£50, but repeatedly diverted funds elsewhere. By February 1887 he was reserving money for enlargement of the museum building.

2019

Gill, Grenfell and Blom published the seven-object crosswalk and reconstructed the correspondence linking Cheeseman, Ward and the 1885 supply.

What the chain closes

Catalogue enquiry, sample strategy, a seven-model 1885 supply, Ward catalogue numbers, individual prices, Auckland registration numbers, and the Adams-linked payment network.

What remains open

The exact delivery date, a dedicated seven-model invoice, whether all seven travelled together, the identity relationship between ML35749 and MA124293, and the present display or storage status.
