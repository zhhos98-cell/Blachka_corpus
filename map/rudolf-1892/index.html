Journey dossier · 1892

Rudolf Blaschka’s 1892 American Journey

← Back to collections map

This page treats the 1892 journey as a mobile extension of the Blaschka workshop. It maps documented movement, distinguishes it from the projected route circulated in newspapers, and foregrounds the multiple forms through which living plants became future glass models: drawing, microscope work, dried and alcohol-preserved specimens, seeds, bulbs, cacti, botanical-garden work rooms, and later supply by local intermediaries.

Chronology

1 February 1892 to 30 June 1892, from Bremen to Hosterwitz via Cambridge, Jamaica, the American Southwest, the Midwest, and back across the Atlantic.

Main claim

The journey redistributed workshop labour across places, people, and media. Travel did not merely precede model production. It actively reorganised it.

Editorial guard

Planned and publicly circulated routes are shown separately from documented movement. Unresolved points remain unresolved.

Method notes

  • The solid line follows documented route nodes reconstructed from correspondence and associated source layers.
  • The dashed line follows the planned or publicly circulated route that appeared in letters and newspapers.
  • The point of the page is not only to say where Rudolf went, but to show how observation, specimen handling, seed circulation, and later workshop production were linked.
  • Jamaica, San Diego, St. Louis, Cambridge, and Hosterwitz are particularly strong nodes for reconstructing knowledge production.
