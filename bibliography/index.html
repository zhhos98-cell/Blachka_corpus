Research bibliography

Secondary literature

A working bibliography for the history, design, scientific use, circulation, conservation, and afterlives of Leopold and Rudolf Blaschka’s glass models.

Selection

Scholarship behind the project

This page records secondary research used to orient the project and interpret individual collections. Workshop correspondence, historical sales catalogues, museum registers, invoices, object databases, and auction records are treated separately as primary evidence.

This is a selective working bibliography rather than a claim to completeness. It will expand as the global census and provenance modules develop.

01

Histories, design, and making

Studies of the Blaschka workshop, visual sources, glassworking practice, and the relation between scientific representation and craft.

1998

Henri Reiling, “The Blaschkas’ Glass Animal Models: Origins of Design”

Journal of Glass Studies 40: 105–126. A foundational study of the zoological models, their visual sources, preparatory drawings, catalogues, and changing design practice.

JSTOR record ↗

2002

James Peto and Angie Hudson, eds., Leopold & Rudolf Blaschka

London: Design Museum. Exhibition publication covering the makers, the zoological models, and the Glass Flowers.

Library catalogue record ↗

2007

Susan M. Rossi-Wilcox and David Whitehouse, Drawing upon Nature: Studies for the Blaschkas’ Glass Models

Corning, NY: The Corning Museum of Glass. Reproduces selections from more than 900 surviving workshop drawings and connects them to zoological and botanical model-making.

Corning Museum of Glass ↗

2008

Julia D. Sigwart, “Crystal Creatures: Context for the Dublin Blaschka Congress”

Historical Biology 20(1): 1–10. An interdisciplinary framing of the models as scientific, commercial, technical, and aesthetic objects.

DOI ↗

2008

Susan M. Rossi-Wilcox, “From Reference Specimen to Verisimilitude: The Blaschkas’ Penchant for Botanical Accuracy”

Historical Biology 20(1): 11–18. Useful for reference practice, observation, and the later botanical commission.

DOI ↗

2016

Elizabeth R. Brill and Florian Huber, Sea Creatures in Glass: The Blaschka Marine Animals at Harvard

London: Scala Arts & Heritage Publishers, in association with Harvard Museums of Science & Culture. Combines collection history, conservation, scientific interpretation, and photography of the Harvard invertebrates.

Publisher record ↗

2016

Drew Harvell, A Sea of Glass: Searching for the Blaschkas’ Fragile Legacy in an Ocean at Risk

University of California Press. A marine-biological and historical account that follows surviving glass models back toward their living counterparts.

University of California Press ↗

02

Collections, teaching, and circulation

Collection-level reconstructions are especially important for this project because they preserve acquisition routes, old inventories, institutional movement, and patterns of survival.

2008

Sabine Hackethal, “The Blaschka Models of the Humboldt University of Berlin and Their Historical Context”

Historical Biology 20(1): 19–28. Places the Berlin models within zoological teaching collections and alongside other nineteenth-century model makers.

DOI ↗

2014

Eric Callaghan, Hazel J. Doyle, and Emmanuel G. Reynaud, “The Blaschka Collection at University College Dublin: Rebuilding Its History”

Journal of the History of Collections 26(1): 63–71. Reconstructs an 1885 purchase through invoices and successive lists while distinguishing the original 139 models from the 114 then recovered.

DOI ↗

2017

Hazel Doyle, Eric Callaghan, and Emmanuel G. Reynaud, “Blaschka Invertebrate Models in Irish Institutions”

Journal of the History of Collections 29(3): 439–450. Surveys the major Irish institutional holdings and traces acquisition history across several collections.

DOI ↗

2017

Matthew D. Shaw, Joanna Z. Szczepanski, Sarah F. Murray, Simon Hodge, and Cor J. Vink, “Ideas Made Glass: Blaschka Glass Models at Canterbury Museum”

Records of the Canterbury Museum 31: 5–84. Combines acquisition history, scientific context, collection comparison, and an illustrated catalogue.

Open PDF ↗

2019

B. J. Gill, H. R. Grenfell, and W. M. Blom, “The Cheeseman–Ward Correspondence (1878–1905) and Exchanges of Natural History Specimens between Auckland Museum and H. A. Ward of Rochester, N.Y.”

Records of the Auckland Museum 54: 21–36. Reconstructs the long dealer-curator relationship that included the Auckland Blaschka purchase and wider specimen exchanges.

DOI ↗

03

Taxonomy, conservation, and material afterlives

Research that helps translate nineteenth-century catalogue names, understand damage and restoration, or interpret models as changing material objects.

2014

María Isabel Herráez Martín, “Los modelos Blaschka”

In Modelos y maquetas: la vida a escala, pp. 239–243. Madrid: Ministerio de Educación, Cultura y Deporte. A concise Spanish-language overview attentive to materials, technique, conservation, and museum use.

Spanish Ministry of Culture ↗

2015

Susan M. Rossi-Wilcox, “A Brief History of Harvard’s Glass Flowers Collection and Its Development”

Journal of Glass Studies 57: 197–211. Traces the botanical collection as a developing didactic project and examines reference sources and selection.

Corning Museum of Glass ↗

2015

N. Astrid R. van Giffen, Katherine Eremin, and Tracy Owen Drier, “Imitating Nature: The Materials and Preservation Needs of the Blaschka Glass Models”

Journal of Glass Studies 57. A technical study of the materials and preservation problems that shape the models’ present condition.

Corning Museum of Glass ↗

2020

Eric Callaghan, Bernhard Egger, Hazel Doyle, and Emmanuel G. Reynaud, “Taxonomic Revision of Leopold and Rudolf Blaschkas’ Glass Models of Invertebrates 1888 Catalogue, with Correction of Authorities”

Journal of Natural Science Collections 7. Updates the nineteenth-century catalogue taxonomy and is particularly useful when matching old model names to present collection records.

Open PDF ↗

Corpus note

From bibliography to evidence

Secondary literature is used here as a map into archival and collection evidence, not as a substitute for it. When a publication gives a model count, acquisition claim, catalogue number, or provenance chain, the project attempts to recover the underlying register, invoice, catalogue, correspondence, object record, or other first-order source before treating the claim as closed.
